## Question

From `_branch.md`: *"dive deep into this one and understand what types of seeds exist — be extremely careful and make sure the types of seeds together have coverage of the seed's definition."* "This one" is the **function-typing** family of seed-types (what a seed *does for you*), the family the prior 14-26 finding opened but left incomplete.

The goal: a **complete, covering, justified** set of seed function-types — where "covering" is the load-bearing word. The types must be exhaustive over what the seed's definition admits, and that exhaustiveness must be *shown*, not asserted.

**Background for a fresh reader.** This project runs a "harvest": it studies research papers one at a time and extracts **seeds** — small, usable, anchored germs of an idea that pay off later (a seed might be a component to build, a mechanism to try, or a confirmation that some existing design is sound). A prior inquiry defined what a seed *is* and observed that seeds come in functional *types* — but named only two and deferred the rest. This inquiry completes that enumeration.

## Finding Summary

- **The complete set of seed function-types is THREE:** **inspiration**, **affirmation**, and **provocation**. Every seed that passes the harvest's quality gate does one of exactly three things for you.

- **Inspiration** — the seed hands you something you *didn't have* (a component, a mechanism, a framing, a refinement, a connection). Its action is **ADD**: build the new thing. This is the prior finding's first type, unchanged; the five previously-catalogued "development kinds" all live here as sub-flavors.

- **Affirmation** — the seed *raises your confidence* in something you already have (a design you were unsure about turns out sound). Its action is **CONFIRM**: close the open question, stand down. This is the prior finding's second type, unchanged.

- **Provocation (the new one)** — the seed *lowers your confidence* in something you already have (a design you trusted may be flawed). Its action is **CHALLENGE**: re-open the settled question, raise something to investigate.

- **Why exactly three — and why that's covering.** The three are *derived* from two dimensions of the seed's own definition: is the thing the seed bears on **absent or present**, and if present, does your **confidence go up or down**? Absent → inspiration. Present-and-up → affirmation. Present-and-down → provocation. Because "absent/present" is a true either/or and "up/down" exhausts the present branch, the three cover the space *by construction* — not by a list someone hopes is complete.

- **Three, not two.** The prior finding's confidence door was *defined* to swing both ways ("is this design sound? does it hold up?" — a question that resolves yes *or* no) but only the "yes" swing was given a type (affirmation). The "no" swing (provocation) was latent in the definition and simply never named. Naming it completes the door.

- **Three, not four.** A deliberate hunt for a fourth type was run twice, with different candidates each time (a seed that changes your goals; a seed about a hypothetical; a seed that re-frames). Every candidate collapsed into one of the three or split into a combination of them. No fourth distinct action survived.

- **The one honest caveat:** provocation is **structurally required but un-exemplified** — the harvest has produced inspiration seeds and one affirmation seed, but zero provocation seeds so far. This is a real caveat, not a defeater: affirmation was *also* empty until one paper finally produced it. Provocation should be watched for, not assumed present.

- **Reconciliation:** **two doors, three types.** The gate still has two doors (novelty, confidence); the confidence door simply has two outcomes. This *refines* the prior finding and *strengthens* its grounding — the harness treats "detecting that something broke" as its primary quality signal, and provocation is exactly the seed-level form of that detection, which the two-type list had been missing.

## Finding

### Why we are even asking this

The harvest needs to *do something* with each seed it extracts. A seed that hands you a new component leads to different work than a seed that tells you an existing design is sound — you build in the first case, you stand down in the second. So the harvest needs to know the **functional types** of seeds: the distinct things a seed can do for you, each routing a distinct next action. The prior 14-26 finding established that seeds have such types and named two, but deferred the complete list. The user's request — "what types exist, and make sure they cover the definition" — is a request to finish that list *and prove it's finished*. The "prove it's finished" part is what makes this more than brainstorming.

### 1. What "cover the definition" actually means

A seed, by the prior definition, is always four things at once: it is *anchored* (it names what it relates to), *gated* (it cleared a quality bar), has a *deferred payoff* (its value lands later), and sits in a *lifecycle* (recorded now, used later). Every seed has all four properties regardless of its type — so those properties are the shared *genus*, not what distinguishes one function-type from another. Asking the function-types to "cover" those shared properties would be trivial; they all have them.

What the function-types must actually cover is the part of the definition that *discriminates by function*: the **gate**. The gate is what a seed must clear to count — and the prior finding established the gate as an "is this news?" test with two doors. A seed is admitted because it is *news about a load-bearing thing*. So the real coverage target is: **the ways a seed can be news about a load-bearing thing.** The function-types must jointly exhaust that space. This reframing is what makes coverage a provable property instead of a hopeful one.

### 2. The derivation — why exactly three

Take the coverage target literally. A seed is news about some load-bearing thing X. There are two independent questions you can ask about X:

- **Is X absent or present?** Either you don't yet have X, or you do. This is a genuine either/or — there is no third option.
- **If X is present, does the seed move your confidence in it up or down?** Either the seed makes X look more sound, or less sound.

Cross these and the regions fall out:

1. **X is absent** → the seed gives you X (or points at it). You *gain something you didn't have*. This is **inspiration**. Its action is **ADD** — build the new thing, adopt the mechanism, take the framing.

2. **X is present, confidence goes UP** → the seed tells you the X you already have is sound. You can *stop worrying about it*. This is **affirmation**. Its action is **CONFIRM** — close the open question, spend the confidence, stand down.

3. **X is present, confidence goes DOWN** → the seed tells you the X you already have may be flawed. You need to *look again*. This is **provocation**. Its action is **CHALLENGE** — re-open the settled question, raise an investigate-item.

This covers the space **by construction**: presence is a dichotomy (absent/present with nothing between), and once X is present, the confidence direction (up/down) exhausts what a seed can do to your belief about it. The "no news at all" case — a seed that neither adds, confirms, nor challenges — is not a fourth type; it is exactly what the gate *excludes* (decorative agreement, something you were already certain of). So the gate's own filtering is what makes the three exhaustive: everything it admits lands in one of the three, and everything that would land nowhere is refused entry.

A note on generalizing "confidence." The confidence dimension is not only about whether a design is *sound*. It ranges over any load-bearing attribute of a present thing — its soundness, its importance, its applicability. This matters because it absorbs would-be extra types: a seed that says "X matters more than you thought" is a confidence-up move on X's importance (affirmation-flavored); "X matters less" is confidence-down (provocation-flavored). Re-prioritization is not a fourth type; it is the same up/down axis applied to a different attribute.

### 3. The count is decided by *actions*, not by intuition

The reason three is the right count — not two, not four — is that **each type is individuated by the distinct action it routes.** A function-type *is*, operationally, the thing you do next because of it. Inspiration routes ADD (build). Affirmation routes CONFIRM (close). Provocation routes CHALLENGE (open). These are three genuinely different next-moves. This "a type earns its place only if it routes a distinct action" test is what guards against inventing decorative types, and it is what the count rests on.

**Three, not two (why provocation doesn't fold away).** The tempting reduction is that provocation is really just inspiration — "learning that X is flawed is just learning about an absent fix." This fails on both content and action. On content: the provocation seed's *own* payload is "X, which you have, may be wrong" — that is about the present X, not about the (absent, not-yet-known) fix. The fix, if one comes, is a *separate later* inspiration that the provocation triggers — the same way any seed can trigger later work. On action: CHALLENGE (re-open a closed question) is the *opposite* of affirmation's CONFIRM (close an open one), and neither is inspiration's ADD (build something new). Opposite actions cannot be the same type.

**Three, not four (why the hunt came back empty).** A fourth type would need a fourth distinct action. The search for one was run hard and twice. Candidates included: a seed that changes your *goals* rather than your beliefs (→ folds in: gaining a goal is ADD, raising/lowering confidence in a goal is CONFIRM/CHALLENGE — a goal is just another load-bearing thing you hold); a seed about a *future or hypothetical* thing (→ folds in: right now it either adds a possibility or moves confidence on one); a seed that *re-frames* X without adding, confirming, or challenging (→ a genuinely new frame is something you gain = ADD; a frame you already had, re-applied with no confidence change, is no news = gate-excluded). Every candidate distributed into the three or was refused by the gate. An active search that finds nothing *strengthens* the coverage claim rather than leaving it untested.

### 4. Provocation, in detail

**What it is.** A provocation seed lowers your confidence in a load-bearing thing you already have — it reveals that a present design, assumption, or decision might be flawed. In the harness's own terms, it is the seed-level form of **regression detection** (the harness's phrase for catching that "things that are broken" — format violations, removed safeguards, internal contradictions). Regression detection is the harness's *primary* quality concern, and a detector's characteristic job is to *fire* — to catch the break. Provocation is what a fired detector produces at the seed level.

**Its action, CHALLENGE, is distinct from inspiration's "refine" — but only just, and honestly.** This was the closest call in the whole inquiry, so it gets stated carefully. One might object that "investigate a flaw in X" is simply the front half of "refine X" — you always investigate before you improve. The distinction holds because CHALLENGE (open) does not yet commit to a fix: after you look, X might turn out fine (that resolves to affirmation), might need replacing wholesale (build), or might need a specific improvement (refine). An action whose outcome spans confirm/build/refine is genuinely *upstream* of refine, which already knows the improvement it's making. The harness's own architecture backs this: it treats *detecting* a break as a separate component from *repairing* it. CHALLENGE is the detect; refine is the repair.

But the honest qualification: **a provocation seed frequently matures into a downstream inspiration.** Once you investigate the doubt and find the fix, that fix is an inspiration seed. So provocation is a distinct type *at the moment of the seed* that often *triggers* a later inspiration — a lifecycle succession, not a merger. The distinctness is real; the practical overlap is also real, and pretending provocation is fully disjoint from inspiration would overstate it.

**The un-exemplified caveat — kept prominent on purpose.** No provocation seed has been harvested yet. The harvest so far is almost all inspiration, with a single affirmation seed. Provocation is included because it is *structurally forced* by the derivation, not because it has been observed. The precedent that makes this legitimate: **affirmation was also empty** until one particular paper produced the first affirmation seed — it was structurally real before it was exemplified, and then it populated. Provocation is in the same position. The correct stance is to watch for the first provocation seed, not to assume the type is already populated. If a long stretch of harvesting produces none, that emptiness itself becomes evidence worth re-examining.

**Its name is not settled.** "Provocation" is the working label. Alternatives considered: destabilization (accurate but heavy), regression-seed (maps cleanly to the harness's own vocabulary but collides with the loaded word "regression"), challenge-seed, doubt-seed. The structure is fixed regardless of the label; the naming is a separate, user-facing decision.

### 5. Reconciliation with the prior finding — two doors, three types

The prior finding built the gate as two doors: a **novelty door** (does this point at something the project doesn't have?) and a **confidence door** (does this resolve a real uncertainty about something the project does have?). It mapped inspiration to the novelty door and affirmation to the confidence door, and described the count as "two poles."

The refinement is precise and small: **the confidence door has two outcomes, and the prior finding only named one of them.** This is not a correction of an error the prior finding made elsewhere — it is completing something the prior finding's *own definition already contained*. The prior text defined the confidence door as resolving the question "is this design sound? does it hold up?" — and that question resolves either way. It then labeled the door as the home of "the affirmation seed" and equated passing it with "affirming." So the definition was bidirectional while the label was one-directional. Provocation is simply the branch the definition already admitted and the label left unnamed.

This is worth stating plainly because it changes the character of the finding: this inquiry did **not** catch the prior finding missing a whole type. It found the prior finding *90% of the way there* — its own bidirectional definition was carrying provocation latently — and gave the second branch its name. That is a restoration, not a rescue. (Notably, it is the same shape of gap the prior finding itself had diagnosed in *its* predecessor: a definition whose own text undercut one of its stated properties.)

The reconciliation therefore **strengthens** the prior finding rather than weakening it. The harness names regression detection its primary concern; the two-type list had grounded the confidence door in regression but only represented regression's *quiet* outcome (nothing broke = affirmation), leaving its *characteristic* outcome (something broke = provocation) unrepresented. Three types close that gap: both outcomes of the primary quality signal now have a seed-type.

### 6. One thing this opens but does not settle (a research frontier, not built here)

The three-type structure may propagate beyond the seed to the whole harvest scoring system. The harness has an "import test" — a check for whether a studied paper actually changes a decision — and the prior work found that test already split in two (novelty and confidence halves). If the confidence half is itself bidirectional in the same way the confidence door is, then the whole verdict-ladder might be three-fold: there could be *provocation-imports* — papers whose value is that they immediately destabilize a current design. This is a genuine extension, and it is **explicitly out of scope here**: this inquiry typed the *seed's function*, not the scoring ladder. It is flagged as a frontier to investigate later, ideally after a real provocation seed has actually appeared, so the extension is grounded rather than speculative.

## Reasoning

The inquiry's whole risk was miscounting — and because this refines *my own* prior finding, the risk ran in both directions at once: **caving** back to two (to look consistent with the prior) and **proliferating** to four (to look thorough for a user who asked "what types exist"). The verdicts below were rendered against both pressures.

- **THREE — survived (the central verdict).** Held against the fold-to-two reduction (provocation's content is about the present thing, and its action is opposite to affirmation's) and against the proliferate-to-four push (the fourth-type hunt came back empty twice). The count rests on three independent supports: the prior finding's own internal inconsistency (bidirectional definition, one-directional label), the opposite-action test (CHALLENGE vs CONFIRM), and the empty fourth-probe. It does *not* rest on the tidiness of the ADD/CONFIRM/CHALLENGE triad — the tidiness is a *consequence* of the derivation, checked to be earned rather than imposed.

- **The internal-gap framing — refined during critique (a KILL of my own overclaim).** The prosecution I first reached for — "the prior finding *missed* the fire outcome entirely" — was checked against the prior finding's actual text and found too strong. The prior finding's confidence-door *definition* already admitted provocation (it defined the door as resolving "is this design sound?", a two-way question); only the *label* was one-directional. So the honest claim is the weaker, better-grounded one: provocation was latent in the prior finding's own definition, and this inquiry names it. This correction *strengthened* the finding — a latent-in-your-own-definition type is better grounded than an externally-imposed one — and it is why the reconciliation credits the prior finding rather than correcting it.

- **The OPEN-vs-refine distinction — survived, with a caveat (the closest call).** Prosecuted hardest because if provocation's action folds into inspiration's refine, three collapses to two. It survived: CHALLENGE is upstream of refine (it doesn't yet know the fix; it resolves to confirm/build/refine), and the harness separates detecting a break from repairing it. But the caveat was kept: a provocation frequently matures into a downstream inspiration, so the two are distinct at the seed's moment while overlapping in the lifecycle. Stating only the distinctness would overclaim.

- **The un-exemplified status — survived as a caveat, not a defeater.** The "provocation is vaporware, don't mint it" objection was weighed against the affirmation precedent (affirmation was also structurally real before any paper exemplified it). The precedent holds, so structural forcing justifies inclusion — but the emptiness is flagged prominently and turned into a monitoring action rather than hidden.

- **The ladder-three-fold extension — held as a frontier, not built.** It would have been easy to over-claim that the whole scoring system is three-fold. That claim is genuinely plausible but out of this inquiry's scope (which typed the seed, not the ladder), so it is recorded as a research frontier, explicitly not built, and gated on a real provocation seed appearing first.
