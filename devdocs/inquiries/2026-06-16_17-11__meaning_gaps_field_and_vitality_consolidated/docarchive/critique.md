**Defense:** the user explicitly asked to consolidate; the single account ADDS the explicit USES join neither finding has; and the separability is *preserved* (the rubric noted independently reusable / a candidate sub-spec). At the finding level (one coherent account) the nest serves a reader who wants the whole feature; spec-level separability survives as a Next-Action.
**Collision (purpose-fitness):** "if [one-account-instead-of-two] were left in, would it still give the reader the whole feature in one place?" — yes, better than two findings. The stay-two case is real at the *spec* level, but the ask was a consolidation *finding*. **SURVIVE** — caveat: the finding must note the rubric's independent reusability (don't bury it).

### C2 — The uses-not-owns nest framing (rationalization?)
**Prosecution:** "uses-not-owns" is a verbal dodge — once the rubric is documented *inside* the field's finding, readers treat it as field-specific regardless of the label; the framing names the over-coupling away rather than preventing it.
**Defense:** the uses-edge is structurally real — 16-38's own text says vitality reuses "the project's own severity vocabulary re-pointed," i.e. the rubric is general and predates this consumer. The finding can make the generality explicit (the rubric rates ANY gap; this field is one consumer) and flag reusability (Next-Action). That is an accurate structural claim plus a concrete preservation action, not naming-away.
**Collision:** the prosecution's "readers will treat it as field-specific" is a *presentation* risk, not a structural falsity. The defense holds IF the finding actively presents the generality. **SURVIVE** — caveat (REFINE-grade): present the rubric's generality explicitly; don't let the nest imply ownership.

### C3 — The name "meaning-readiness gauge"
**Prosecution:** "gauge" implies a single measured reading; the feature produces a per-gap *profile* of tiers, not one number. Worse, "gauge/measure" could tempt reading gaps as measured *things* (identities) — colliding with the gaps-not-identities commitment.
**Defense:** "gauge" is a loose metaphor for "shows readiness," and a per-gap profile IS a readiness reading.
**Substance-axis prosecution (apply literally):** "the gauge *measures each gap's* vitality" — "measures each gap" mildly reifies the gap. "rates *how urgent each gap is to resolve*" keeps the gap descriptive.
**Collision:** the name is a genuine BOUNDARY — evocative but carrying a mild measured-identity temptation against a load-bearing commitment. **REFINE** — prefer the descriptive framing (*a soft, triage-rated Definition-of-Ready* / *a per-route readiness checklist with triage tiers*) as PRIMARY; use "gauge" only as loose flavor, and never phrase it as "measuring a gap." The name must not be the load-bearing term over gaps-not-identities.

### C4 — The dedupe (vitality stated once)

**Defense:** the decomposition splits it by an explicit interface — field layer states vitality's ROLE (per-gap attribute, distinct from Priority); rubric layer states its DECISION (how the value is computed). That is introduce-then-operationalize, a standard expository move; the seam makes the handoff explicit.
**Collision:** a real *writing* risk, but with a clean structural rule to avoid it (role vs decision). **SURVIVE** — caveat: the finding must respect the role/decision split (state vitality's role in the field section, its decision in the rubric section; do not redefine).
