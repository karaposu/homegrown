## PART 1 — FACTs surfaced from paper 7 (the territory's own claims)

- **A-1** [FACT · core] **WM capacity is severely limited** (3–4 objects), which "necessitates efficient mechanisms to select only the most relevant objects... and to restrict irrelevant items from consuming capacity" (abstract).
- **A-2** [FACT · core] **The key finding — capacity differences are partly FILTERING differences, not just storage.** "high capacity individuals are much more efficient at representing only the relevant items than are low capacity individuals, who inefficiently encode and maintain information about the irrelevant items."
- **A-3** [FACT · core] **The counterintuitive inversion — a worse system may hold MORE.** "low capacity individuals may actually store more information in memory than high capacity individuals"; "this ancillary allocation of memory capacity to irrelevant objects may be a primary source of putative differences in overall storage capacity." → irrelevant items **consume the scarce capacity**.
- **A-4** [FACT · core] **Capacity reframed — storage × filtering.** "an individual's specific memory capacity does not simply reflect 'how many' items can be stored, but also 'how efficient' the individual is at excluding irrelevant information from reaching this highly limited memory system." (L207–210, L246–249.)
- **A-5** [FACT · core] **The append-vs-exclude dissociation (experiment 3).** Both groups "can append items into working memory" — low-capacity subjects were "not impaired at appending." But on *exclude*, low-capacity subjects let irrelevant items in: "the green items had been unnecessarily appended into working memory." Conclusion: "although both low and high capacity subjects can append items into working memory, these two groups substantially diverge in their abilities to determine selectively which items will be appended into memory" (L190–193). → **loading and gatekeeping are separable control processes.**
- **A-6** [FACT · supporting] **Filtering generalizes** across colour-based AND location-based selection (experiments 1 & 2) — it is not an artifact of one hard selection attribute.
- **A-7** [FACT · core] **The framing: "control processes that regulate ACCESS to working memory."** "The control processes that regulate access to working memory are crucial for keeping irrelevant information from consuming capacity" (L194–195). Access-control = gatekeeping at the door.
- **A-8** [FACT · peripheral] **Substrate** — CDA (contralateral delay activity) ERP measure; prefrontal "bias signal"; the K=S(H−F) capacity formula; electrode montages; visual arrays of coloured rectangles/hemifields. The quarantined bulk.
