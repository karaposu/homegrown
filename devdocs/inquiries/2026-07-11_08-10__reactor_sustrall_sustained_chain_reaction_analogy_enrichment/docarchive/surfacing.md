## Region B — the reactor side at aspect-depth [knowledge-artifact case]

### B-MECHANICS
- **B1 [core] Fission.** A fissile nucleus (U-235) absorbs a neutron → splits → fragments + **~2–3 new neutrons** (avg ≈2.4 for thermal U-235) + energy (~200 MeV) . One consumed trigger → multiple candidate triggers + yield.
- **B2 [core] k, the multiplication factor.** k = neutrons in generation n+1 / generation n. **k<1 subcritical** — the population decays geometrically; with an external source it holds a steady low level (source-multiplication) and dies the moment the source is removed. **k=1 critical** — self-sustaining at steady level: *each fission causes, on average, exactly one further fission.* **k>1 supercritical** — geometric growth.
- **B3 [core] Neutron economy.** Of each fission's ~2.4 neutrons, most are LOST: leakage out of the core, parasitic capture (structures, coolant, poisons, fertile material), captures that don't fission. k is a BUDGET: production vs losses. Criticality is reached not by making more neutrons but by husbanding the ones made.
- **B4 [core] The moderator.** Fission neutrons are born FAST; fast neutrons mostly fail to trigger new fissions in low-enriched fuel. The moderator (water, graphite) SLOWS them to thermal speeds where the fission probability is hundreds of times higher. ★The raw yield is nearly useless as a trigger until PREPARED; the preparing substance is a core design component, not an accessory.
- **B5 [core] Control rods.** Strong neutron absorbers, inserted/withdrawn — the throttle. Full insertion (scram) kills the chain in seconds. Control = deliberately wasting triggers to hold k at exactly 1.
- **B6 [sub] Fuel + enrichment.** Fissile density (natural U 0.7% → power fuel ~3–5% → weapons ~90%). Higher enrichment = criticality achievable in smaller/looser assemblies.
- **B7 [sub] Reflector.** Surrounds the core; bounces leaking neutrons back — recovering triggers that would be lost to the boundary.
- **B8 [core] ★Criticality is a property of the ASSEMBLY, not the fuel.** The same uranium is critical in one geometry+moderation arrangement and inert in another. "Going critical" is an achievement of ARRANGEMENT.

### B-DYNAMICS
- **B9 [core] Startup needs an external neutron source.** A fresh core has essentially no neutron population; a startup source (Sb-Be, Cf-252) provides the initial neutrons. Even a fully critical-CAPABLE core starts from a source — criticality is about CONTINUATION, not self-starting from zero.
- **B10 [core] ★Prompt vs DELAYED neutrons — THE controllability fact.** ~99.3% of neutrons are prompt (instant); **~0.7% are delayed** — emitted seconds-to-minutes later by decaying fission products. Reactors are designed to be subcritical on prompt neutrons ALONE and critical only WITH the delayed ones — so the chain's effective time-constant is seconds-to-minutes, slow enough for humans and machinery to steer. If k exceeds 1 on prompt neutrons alone (**prompt-critical**), the period drops to microseconds — no controller can act (the excursion regime; Chernobyl's spike, SL-1). **A small DELAYED fraction of the yield is what makes the whole chain steerable.**
- **B11 [sub] Approach-to-critical practice.** Rods are withdrawn stepwise; instrument count-rates are plotted (1/M method); criticality is APPROACHED under measurement, never jumped to. The discipline is: predict, step, measure, re-predict.
- **B12 [sub] Negative feedback (inherent stability).** Well-designed cores self-stabilize: fuel heating increases non-fission capture (Doppler), pushing k down — power rises resist themselves. Chernobyl's RBMK had a POSITIVE feedback region (void coefficient) — perturbations self-amplified. ★Sitting at k=1 is safe only under negative feedback.

### B-FAILURE
- **B13 [core] ★Xenon poisoning — the reaction's own product chokes it.** Xe-135, a fission product (via iodine decay), has an enormous appetite for neutrons. It builds up from operation; under power it is partly burned off; **after shutdown or power-drop it PEAKS** (~9–11h, the "xenon pit") and can make restart impossible for a day — the recent RUN's own waste blocks the next start. (Factor at Chernobyl: operators fighting the pit pulled too many rods.) Also Sm-149: a PERMANENT poison that accumulates over core life.
- **B14 [core] Meltdown ≠ explosion.** A power reactor cannot detonate like a bomb; it fails by HEAT — loss of control or (see B16) loss of cooling melts the fuel. The catastrophic reactor failure is thermal self-damage, not a detonation.
- **B15 [core] The bomb regime.** A bomb is the SAME physics run uncontrolled: deliberately prompt-supercritical, k as high as possible, for microseconds — and it ENDS BY DESTROYING ITS OWN ASSEMBLY (the explosion disassembles the critical geometry; the chain terminates because the medium blows apart). Reactor vs bomb = control, not mechanism: the bomb maximizes growth-rate and self-terminates; the reactor holds equilibrium indefinitely.
- **B16 [core] ★Decay heat — you can stop the chain, not the afterglow.** After scram, fission stops but fission-product decay continues: ~6–7% of full power immediately, ~1% after hours, cooling needed for days-to-months (Fukushima's cores melted AFTER a successful scram — decay heat alone). A shut-down core still demands attention.
- **B17 [sub] Fuel exhaustion / burnup.** Over a cycle, fissile depletes and poisons accumulate; the core loses reactivity and must be refueled — the chain consumes its territory.

### B-ORDERING
- **B18 [sub].** The canonical sequence: source inserted → stepwise approach → **criticality** (the threshold event) → power ascension → sustained operation at k=1 → shutdown → decay-heat management (+ xenon transient governing restartability). Criticality is a THRESHOLD crossed once per startup; sustained operation is the long plateau after.

### B-SCALE
- **B19 [core] ★Chicago Pile-1 (Fermi, 2 Dec 1942).** The first human-made self-sustaining chain reaction. Graphite pile, cadmium rods, stepwise approach under counters. It ran for ~28 minutes at ~half a WATT. **The achievement was the threshold, not the power** — "self-sustaining" was proven at negligible output; power reactors came years later. The historic phrase: the pile "went critical."

### B-BOUNDARIES
- **B20 [sub] Containment.** The reaction is run inside engineered boundaries (vessel, containment building) — the assembly is bounded on purpose.
- **B21 [sub] ★Breeding.** Fertile material (U-238) captures spare neutrons → becomes fissile (Pu-239). A breeder produces MORE future fuel than it consumes — the chain manufacturing its own future fuel from otherwise-inert material.
- **B22 [side].** The standard physics phrase is itself the shared term: "**self-sustaining** nuclear chain reaction" — Fermi's milestone was named with the exact word SUSTRALL's first two letters carry.

## Region D — the shared-term material [possibility case]

- **D1 [core].** In physics, "sustained/self-sustaining" marks the k=1 THRESHOLD — **self-sufficiency, not duration**: the process's own products suffice to continue it; below that it is a reaction, not a *sustained chain* reaction. CP-1 (B19) proved "sustained" in 28 minutes at half a watt — duration and power were irrelevant; self-sufficiency was everything.
- **D2 [core].** ★But self-sufficiency alone is NOT enough for the reactor-sense: **a bomb is also self-sufficient** (briefly). The reactor-sense of "sustained" = self-sufficiency **+ persistence without self-destruction** (k held at 1, under control, indefinitely). Two components: *the yield feeds continuation* AND *the continuation does not consume the assembly*.
- **D3 [core].** SUSTRALL-side mirror: the turn→turn chain today is human-fed (sub-critical, source-driven); achieved-SUSTRALL = yield-fed continuation (component 1) — and canon's "Sustained = worth keeping in motion" quality bar + "controlled whirl" carry component 2 (the whirl must not burn its operator or its record). Candidate account: **canon's quality-bar sense and the user's chain-reaction sense are the two components of the SAME reactor-sense of "sustained"** — self-sufficiency (the aspiration) + non-self-destruction (the bar), with the bar doing double duty sub-critically (worth the human's fuel) and post-critically (worth its own continuation; the Baldwin quality condition).
- **D4 [sub].** Both engineering disciplines are fundamentally about **sitting AT the threshold under control** — not k>1 (bomb / junk-cascade), not k<1 (dies / stalls). The interesting operating point is exactly 1, held by feedback. "Controlled whirl" reads as the project's name for controlled criticality.
- **D5 [sub].** The sub-critical phase has its own named economy in both: source-multiplication (a sub-critical assembly AMPLIFIES its source — one injected neutron yields a short dying chain of fissions; useful work happens sub-critically at the cost of continuous source-feed) ↔ today's whole mode of operation: the human injects one selection, the system amplifies it into a full traverse's yield; the chain dies after each burst; the work is real, the self-sufficiency is absent. [a strong candidate for "how the logics match TODAY", not just at achievement]

## Region E — break-point candidates [possibility case — the honest list]

- **E1 [core].** **Neutrons are identical and stateless; findings are qualitatively distinct.** A numerically self-sustaining chain of junk traverses is k≥1 and worthless. Physics' k counts; SUSTRALL's continuation-condition must COUNT AND JUDGE. Poisons (C10) model waste absorbing attention, but nothing in the reactor models "the trigger fired and the fission was WORTHLESS." The quality dimension is the analogy's hardest break. [feeds the gate: any k-vocabulary import must carry a quality-rider]
- **E2 [core].** **k is an externally measurable scalar; SUSTRALL's "did the yield trigger the next turn without injection" is partly a judgment** (what counts as "triggered by the yield" vs "human re-framed"?) — the same-judge problem does not exist for a neutron detector. [caps any "measure our k" enthusiasm: a k-analog is an ESTIMATE with the usual epistemics, not a gauge reading]
- **E3 [sub].** **Product-role check (surfaced both ways, unresolved):** in a power reactor the chain is instrumental to ENERGY (the per-fission yield aggregated); in SUSTRALL the chain is instrumental to UNDERSTANDING (the per-traverse yield aggregated). Structurally parallel — possibly a MATCH, not a break (both sustain the chain FOR its per-event yield); but note CP-1/research reactors where criticality itself was the point. Leave for the pipeline.
- **E4 [core].** **No memory in physics; the chain's medium is proximity.** SUSTRALL's chain has no proximity — the medium must be BUILT (the record). A break that, read constructively, yields C18's medium-insight. [the break IS the design-yield]
- **E5 [sub].** **Conservation vs breeding-by-default:** neutron arithmetic is tightly budgeted; questions breed promiscuously (a good finding may open many frontiers — in physics that's dangerous supercriticality; in thinking it is normal and GOOD provided DISPATCH is controlled, not question-birth). The control point differs: reactors throttle the population; SUSTRALL throttles the firing (selection), never the breeding. [important asymmetry for C8's wording]
- **E6 [sub].** Time-scales and stakes differ by many orders; prompt-critical kills in microseconds, junk-cascades degrade over weeks — the analogy's urgency-rhetoric must not import nuclear stakes.
- **E7 [core].** **The theorems-never rider (A9):** no formulas, no numeric k-thresholds, no "0.7% delayed fraction" quantities transfer. Vocabulary (criticality, sub-critical, source, moderator, poison, scram) and heuristics (approach under instrumentation; negative feedback; husband the yield; prepare triggers before use) may transfer; results may not.
