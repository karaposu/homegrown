# Finding: What is Mapping — Meta-Level Definition and Paradigms

## Changes from Prior

**Prior path:** `devdocs/inquiries/2026-05-13_07-16__is_mapping_required_core_of_explore/finding.md` — the Identity Refresh v1 finding, which established that mapping is core to the `/explore` discipline (Structural Exploration) and enumerated 7 observable kinds of mapping that `/explore` produces (layout, concept, status, coverage/confidence, frontier, possibility, and a partly-excluded relational kind).

**Revision trigger:** User correction. The prior's 7-kinds typology was, in the user's words, *"good direction but not there exactly."* The prior finding operated at the concrete-kinds level (what `/explore` is observed to produce). The user wanted the meta level — what mapping IS at the foundational level, and what categorical paradigms of mapping exist independent of any specific discipline.

**What's preserved.** The prior finding's core claim — that mapping is the upstream cognitive operation core to `/explore` — is preserved without modification. The 7 kinds enumerated in the prior remain valid as `/explore`-scoped observations. The prior's contribution to `/explore`'s §1 Identity stands.

**What's added.** A meta-paradigm framework that sits one structural level above the prior's 7-kinds typology:

1. A **minimum-core definition** of mapping: a single sentence that compositionally specifies what mapping IS, applicable to every paradigm.
2. A **set of 4 primary generative axes** (with 8 secondary axes) that produce the paradigm space.
3. An **enumeration of 12 crystallized paradigms**, each grounded in established literature.
4. **Classification guidance** for placing a new mapping into one or more paradigms, with explicit compositionality.
5. A **revisability clause and stability layering** specifying which parts of the framework are stable and which are empirically refinable.

**What's repositioned.** The prior's 7 kinds are repositioned as a `/explore`-scoped projection of the meta-paradigms — they map onto 6 of the 12 meta-paradigms (see the projection table later in this finding). The prior's layer is preserved at its level; this finding adds the meta-layer above it.

**No correction.** Nothing in the prior is wrong. The relationship is REFINES (deepens, adds a structural level) rather than CORRECTS (revises a wrong claim). The user's "not there exactly" was about LEVEL of abstraction, not about CONTENT of the prior's claims.

**Meta-lesson — layer-shift semantics for REFINES vs CORRECTS vs SUPERSEDES.** When user feedback signals "good direction but not there exactly," the structural fix may be a **layer shift** (go one level deeper or broader in abstraction) rather than a lateral revision. Layer shifts preserve the prior at its level and add a new level above or below; lateral revisions correct a wrong claim at the same level. Distinguishing the two prevents two failure modes: (1) unnecessary invalidation of prior work (treating a layer-shift situation as a correction situation; the prior gets thrown out when it should be preserved); (2) staying at the wrong abstraction level (treating a correction situation as a layer-shift situation; the wrong claim survives because "the prior is preserved at its level," even though its level was the wrong level to make claims at). This layer-shift heuristic is extractable beyond this finding; future inquiries determining the REFINES / CORRECTS / SUPERSEDES choice for their own prior-relationships can reuse the diagnosis: a wrong claim at the prior's level → CORRECTS; a right claim at the wrong level → REFINES with layer shift; a fundamentally invalidated frame → SUPERSEDES.

**Migration.** None required. The prior finding continues to function at its level. This finding is consumed as a standalone meta-document; an optional secondary deliverable (a one-paragraph addition to `/explore`'s §1 Identity) makes the framework concrete for `/explore`.

## Question

What is "mapping" at a meta-theoretical level, and what categorically distinct mapping paradigms exist — independent of any specific discipline like `/explore` and independent of any specific observed kind — such that the set of paradigms is exhaustive (or close to it) and structurally grounded rather than enumerated from examples?

**Goal.** A meta-level definition of mapping plus an enumeration of mapping paradigms (the categorical types, not the concrete kinds), grounded in first principles rather than discipline-bound observation. The answer should ground `/explore`'s identity more deeply than the prior 7-kinds enumeration, classify any specific mapping act into one or more paradigms, reveal whether the prior 7-kinds typology partitions the space cleanly, and inform future discipline design.

---

## Vocabulary stack

To avoid conflating levels of abstraction, this finding uses a precise terminology hierarchy:

- **Definition** (Layer 1) — the minimum-core statement of what mapping IS, applicable to every paradigm.
- **Axis** (Layer 2) — a generative dimension along which paradigms vary. 4 primary axes and 8 secondary axes.
- **Paradigm** (Layer 3) — a categorically-distinct type of mapping, identified by which structural feature it preserves. 12 paradigms enumerated, with explicit revisability.
- **Kind** — a discipline-scoped instance of one or more paradigms; the prior identity-refresh finding's level. `/explore` produces 7 observable kinds that span 6 of the 12 paradigms.
- **Instance** — a specific observed mapping (a particular `exploration.md` file, a particular code dependency graph, a particular subway map).

"Paradigm" here means *categorically-distinct type of mapping*, not the Kuhnian sense (exemplar that organizes a field) or the ecosystem sense (technological framework).

## Finding Summary

The structural deepening from the prior finding is at **Layer 1** (the minimum-core definition + 4 generative axes); the 12 paradigms in Layer 3 are recognizable clusters in that structure, not the headline. Reading the 12-paradigm table as the answer would miss the meta-level contribution.

- **Layer 1 — Minimum-core definition.** Mapping is a purposive structure-preserving correspondence from a source to a target, specified by which structural feature of the source is preserved, what target medium encodes it, what operation produces it, and what purpose it serves. This is the meta-definition; it applies universally across paradigms and is stable across revisions.

- **Layer 2 — Four primary axes + eight secondary axes.** Any specific mapping is characterized by its values along four primary axes (what-preserved; how-encoded; what-operation; what-purpose), with eight secondary axes refining within primary-axis cells (scope, reference-frame, meta-level, origin, output-form, temporality, fidelity, viewpoint). The 4 primary axes are stable-ish; promotions or demotions between primary and secondary are possible under structural argument.

- **Layer 3 — Twelve crystallized paradigms.** Recognizable clusters in the axis space, each grounded in established literature: Cartographic, Taxonomic, Relational, Functional, Embedding, Process/Behavioral, Constraint/Boundary, Possibility/Generative, Navigational, Coverage/Field, Reflexive/Self/Meta, Analogical/Cross-domain. The enumeration is explicitly revisable; the count is empirical, not load-bearing.

- **`/explore`-specific projection.** `/explore` produces 6 of the 12 paradigms — Cartographic, Taxonomic, Relational, Coverage, Constraint, Possibility. The other 6 (Functional, Embedding, Process/Behavioral, Navigational, Reflexive, Analogical) are scoped out by `/explore`'s NOT-list (§1.3) and belong to other disciplines or specializations.

- **Classification with compositionality.** A reader places a new mapping into the framework by asking the four primary-axis questions in order. If the four axes point to the same paradigm, the mapping is a single-paradigm instance. If they diverge, the mapping is the composition of the paradigms they point to; compositionality is the rule, not the exception.

- **Relationship to prior.** REFINES via layer-shift — the prior's 7-kinds typology is preserved as a `/explore`-scoped projection of the meta-paradigms. The prior's claims are not corrected; this finding adds a structural level above them.

## Finding

### Surrounding context

Mapping is the project's most universal cognitive operation. Multiple disciplines (`/explore`, `/sense-making`, `/decompose`, `/comprehend`, `/intuit`, `/navigation`, `/innovate`) each produce some form of mapping — and yet, until this finding, the project lacked a unified meta-definition that explains what they all share. The prior identity-refresh finding (`2026-05-13_07-16`) established that mapping is core to `/explore` and enumerated 7 observable kinds. The user's correction reveals that the 7-kinds enumeration, while useful as `/explore`-specific observation, was not the meta-level answer to "what mapping IS." This finding fills the gap.

The deepening matters for three reasons. First, it grounds `/explore`'s identity in a discipline-independent foundation, making `/explore`'s NOT-list (the boundary between `/explore`'s scope and its neighboring disciplines') structurally explicit rather than enumerated case-by-case. Second, it enables future discipline-design to be paradigm-aware: a new discipline can be specified by which paradigms it produces. Third, it provides reusable vocabulary for diagnosing finding-to-finding relationships (REFINES with layer-shift semantics, distinguished from CORRECTS and SUPERSEDES).

### Layer 1 — The minimum-core definition

**Mapping is a purposive structure-preserving correspondence from a source to a target, specified by which structural feature of the source is preserved, what target medium encodes it, what operation produces it, and what purpose it serves.**

This single sentence is the meta-definition. It applies universally — to a city street map, to a function signature, to a process diagram, to a database schema, to the inventory section of an `/explore` output. The sentence compositionally specifies four primary axes; reading the sentence already introduces them.

**Stability.** This layer is stable across paradigm revisions. The definition specifies what mapping IS; the primary axes specify the dimensions any specific mapping varies along. Both hold across additions to or restructurings of Layer 3's paradigm enumeration.

### Layer 2 — The 4 primary axes (and 8 secondary)

Any specific mapping is characterized by its values along the four primary axes:

**1. What structural feature of the source is preserved.** The mapping's core commitment: spatial relations, type membership, connection structure, transformation rules, similarity relationships, causal trajectories, boundary structure, generative rules, route structure, continuous values over a domain, the mapping operation itself, or cross-domain structural correspondence. This axis generates the paradigm taxonomy in Layer 3.

**2. What target medium encodes the source.** The representational form the map uses: iconic resemblance (visual/spatial), symbolic labels (discrete tags), indexical references (pointers/links), continuous embeddings (geometric space), discrete combinatorial structures (graphs, trees, lattices), or sequential ordering.

**3. What operation produces the map.** The act: recording (observing/transcribing), constructive inference (building from indirect evidence), compression (lossy summarization), projection (high-dimensional → low-dimensional with chosen perspective), generation (a generator function whose outputs are the map), or prediction (anticipating future or inferred states).

**4. What purpose the map serves.** The use: navigation, communication, prediction, decision-support, reasoning-substrate (the map IS the thinking medium), memory/archival, or coordination across actors.

Eight secondary axes refine within primary-axis cells. They differentiate near-neighbor mappings that share primary axis values but differ at a finer grain:

- **Scope / grain** — what the map's unit is: element / structure / behavior / constraint / possibility / aggregate.
- **Reference frame** — what the map is anchored to: absolute coordinates, relative to a chosen object, functional (relative to purpose), or egocentric (relative to the mapper).
- **Meta-level** — whether the map operates at the object level (mapping a territory) or at the meta level (mapping the act of mapping, the mapper's trajectory, or the map's own coverage edges).
- **Origin** — whether the map is derived a priori (from logical or structural principles), a posteriori (from observation), or via generative-rule application.
- **Output form** — the map's shape as an artifact: catalog/list, graph/network, field/continuous surface, hierarchy/tree, manifold/topological space, trajectory/sequence, or matrix/table.
- **Temporality** — static snapshot, dynamic capture of change, counterfactual (what could be), historical (what was), or predictive (what will be).
- **Fidelity** — lossless (bijective, reversible), lossy (selected preservation), lossy-with-error-bound (quantified loss), or pathological (deliberate distortion for emphasis, like a subway map).
- **Viewpoint** — allocentric (god's-eye), egocentric (first-person), mechanistic (causal), categorical (classification), or analogical (cross-domain).

**Relationship between primary and secondary axes.** Secondary axes refine within the cells of the primary axes — they do not generate the paradigm space, but they distinguish near-neighbor paradigms that share primary values. For example, Cartographic and Navigational paradigms both preserve spatial structure (primary axis 1), but differ on viewpoint (Cartographic is allocentric; Navigational is egocentric). The boundary between primary and secondary is empirical; an axis could be promoted or demoted under finer structural analysis. **This layer is stable-ish.**

### Layer 3 — The 12 crystallized paradigms

Each paradigm is a coherent cluster of axis values that historically appears together as a recognizable way of mapping, with established literature in some domain:

| # | Paradigm | What it preserves | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cartographic / Spatial** | Spatial relations and topology of physical or abstract space | Cartography; geographic information systems |
| 2 | **Taxonomic / Categorical** | Type membership and containment relations between categories | Biological taxonomy; ontology engineering; library classification |
| 3 | **Relational / Graph** | Connection structure between items; "is-related-to" links | Graph theory; relational databases; knowledge graphs |
| 4 | **Functional / Algorithmic** | The transformation rule — what input becomes what output | Function theory; type theory; algorithm specification |
| 5 | **Embedding / Continuous** | Similarity relationships as geometric proximity in a continuous space | Manifold learning; vector embeddings; latent-space methods |
| 6 | **Process / Behavioral** | Causation and trajectory over time; how things evolve | System dynamics; process modeling; state-machine specification |
| 7 | **Constraint / Boundary** | What cannot happen; the edges of the feasible region | Constraint satisfaction; feasibility analysis; type-bounded spaces |
| 8 | **Possibility / Generative** | The generative rule for what could exist; the design space | Possibility theory; generative grammars; design-space enumeration |
| 9 | **Navigational / Routing** | Route structure from a viewpoint; how to get from here to there | Navigation systems; route planning; planning trees |
| 10 | **Coverage / Field** | Continuous values defined over a domain; values-at-locations | Field theory; coverage analysis; heatmaps |
| 11 | **Reflexive / Self / Meta** | The mapping operation itself; the map describes or refers to itself | Reflexivity studies; self-describing systems; provenance graphs |
| 12 | **Analogical / Cross-domain** | Structural correspondence across domains; same shape, different content | Analogical reasoning; conceptual blending; cross-disciplinary transfer |

### Classification — how to place a specific mapping into the framework

To classify a mapping into one or more paradigms, ask the four primary-axis questions in order:

1. **What does this mapping preserve?** The answer locates one or more candidate paradigms in the 12-paradigm enumeration (each paradigm is named by what it preserves).
2. **What target medium encodes it?** The answer narrows the candidates by representational form — for example, a graph encoding distinguishes Relational from Taxonomic (which can be tree-encoded but also list-encoded).
3. **What operation produced it?** The answer narrows further by act — for example, recording versus constructive inference versus generation distinguishes paradigms that share preserved-structure.
4. **What is the map's purpose?** The answer narrows by use — for example, navigation-purpose distinguishes Navigational from Cartographic when both preserve spatial structure.

**Disagreement-handling.** If the four axes point to the same paradigm, the mapping is a single-paradigm instance. If two or more axes point to different paradigms, this signals **compositionality, not confusion**: the mapping is the composition of the paradigms identified by the divergent axes. Record all paradigms identified. **Single-paradigm membership applies only when all four axes converge on the same paradigm.**

**Compositionality is the rule, not the exception.** Most real-world mappings compose multiple paradigms. A process diagram is Process + Relational. A state-space heatmap is Coverage + Possibility. A semantic embedding visualization is Embedding + Cartographic. The framework permits — and expects — multi-paradigm classification; the composition itself often carries the structural information that distinguishes one mapping from another.

### Revisability and stability layering

The 12-paradigm enumeration is empirical, not exhaustive. New paradigms may surface from inquiries that map territories the current 12 do not cover. Existing paradigms may merge under finer structural analysis — for example, Navigational could be reframed as Cartographic + egocentric viewpoint + route-purpose; whether this reduction is structurally clarifying or content-losing is itself a research question.

**Revisions are triggered when any of the following observable conditions hold:**

- **(a)** Three or more inquiries surface mappings that don't fit any of the 12 paradigms cleanly; OR
- **(b)** Two or more pairs of paradigms repeatedly merge in observation (the structural distinction between them stops being load-bearing across multiple cases); OR
- **(c)** A paradigm's literature-grounding is contested by a structural argument (the named domain is shown to encompass multiple distinct mapping operations rather than a single paradigm).

Until one of these triggers fires, the 12-paradigm enumeration is the working set; revisions should be triggered by structural arguments grounded in observed mapping practice, not by terminological preference.

**Stability layering.** The minimum-core definition (Layer 1) is stable across revisions. The 4 primary axes (Layer 2-primary) are stable-ish; axis promotions or demotions are possible under structural argument. The 12 paradigms (Layer 3) are explicitly revisable. The 8 secondary axes (Layer 2-secondary) are intermediate.

### Projection of the prior 7 kinds onto the 12 meta-paradigms

The prior identity-refresh finding's 7 kinds of mapping that `/explore` is observed to produce project onto the meta-paradigm space as follows:

| Prior `/explore` kind | Meta-paradigm(s) it projects onto | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **layout** | Cartographic (primary); Relational (when only topology is recorded) | Layout preserves spatial relations between items in a territory; when only neighbor-of links are recorded without metric distance, it becomes Relational. |
| **concept** | Taxonomic (when class-membership); Relational (when concept-relations) | Concept mapping spans both: classifying items into concepts is Taxonomic; mapping how concepts relate is Relational. |
| **status** | Coverage/Field (continuous); Taxonomic (categorical state) | Status varies: a confidence gradient is Coverage; a discrete state like "scanned/unscanned" is Taxonomic. |
| **coverage / confidence** | Coverage/Field (primary) | Confidence is a continuous value over the territory; canonical Coverage paradigm. |
| **frontier** | Constraint/Boundary (primary) | The frontier IS the boundary between mapped and unmapped territory; canonical Constraint paradigm. |
| **possibility** | Possibility/Generative (primary) | Possibility-mode `/explore` enumerates what could exist; canonical Possibility paradigm. |
| **partly-excluded relational** | Relational (partly-excluded by `/explore`'s NOT-list) | Co-location adjacency is preserved; full relational meaning is excluded and belongs to `/sense-making`. |

**Coverage check.** The 7 prior kinds project onto 6 of the 12 meta-paradigms (Cartographic, Taxonomic, Relational, Coverage, Constraint, Possibility). The other 6 paradigms — Functional, Embedding, Process/Behavioral, Navigational, Reflexive, Analogical — are NOT produced by `/explore`. They are scoped out by `/explore`'s NOT-list (§1.3) and belong to other project disciplines: Functional and Process/Behavioral to `/comprehend`; Embedding to `/intuit`; Navigational to the `/navigation` specialization; Reflexive to self-describing systems and meta-inquiries; Analogical to `/innovate` (cross-domain transfer mechanism) and `/sense-making` (analogical perspective).

### Why the meta-paradigm framing matters

The framework's value lies in three places. First, it provides a discipline-independent foundation for the project's most universal cognitive operation. Multiple disciplines produce mappings; the meta-paradigm framework explains what they all share (the minimum-core definition) and how they differ (which paradigms each produces). Second, it enables future discipline-design to be paradigm-aware — a new discipline can be specified by its paradigm-coverage subset, and unoccupied paradigm slots become visible. Third, it adds reusable vocabulary for diagnosing finding-to-finding relationships (REFINES with layer-shift, distinguished from CORRECTS and SUPERSEDES) — vocabulary that is extractable beyond this finding's immediate scope.

## Reasoning

### Why a 3-layer answer (definition + axes + paradigms) over alternatives

The exploration enumerated 10 candidate forms for the answer (D1 through D10), ranging from "single sentence definition" to "irreducibly multi-paradigmatic enumeration." Sensemaking adjudicated: the answer is structurally multi-layered because the question is compound ("what IS mapping" + "what paradigms exist") and these questions operate at different levels. A single-sentence definition under-specifies; a paradigm enumeration without a definition leaves the enumeration ungrounded; both together produce the structural answer. The 4 primary axes layer was added as the generative mechanism that explains why paradigms cluster the way they do.

### Why these 12 paradigms (not 10, not 15)

Each of the 12 paradigms corresponds to a recognized literature with operationally distinct mapping practices (cartography, biological taxonomy, graph theory, function theory, manifold learning, system dynamics, constraint satisfaction, possibility theory, navigation systems, field theory, reflexivity studies, analogical reasoning). The 12-paradigm count is the working set, not load-bearing — the revisability clause names three concrete triggers under which the enumeration shifts. Critique surfaced the count-arbitrariness concern; the response is the gate specification, which converts revisability from a vague clause into an operational rule.

### What was considered and rejected

- **Single-sentence answer.** KILLED in sensemaking: the user's compound question demands a multi-layered answer; a single sentence loses the paradigm enumeration.
- **Paradigm-enumeration without meta-definition.** KILLED: leaves paradigms ungrounded; reads as a longer 7-kinds list rather than a structural deepening.
- **Treating the prior 7-kinds as the meta-answer.** KILLED: the user's explicit correction said this was "good direction but not there exactly." The prior was at the concrete-kinds level; the meta level was missing.
- **Kuhnian or ecosystem senses of "paradigm".** KILLED in sensemaking via the load-bearing concept test: the user's grammar parallel with "different kinds" signals the categorical-type sense; Kuhnian is sociological and doesn't fit the operational use.
- **All 12 axes as equally orthogonal.** KILLED: some axes are correlated (what-preserved correlates with output-form; operational-act correlates with purpose). The 4-primary + 8-secondary tiering is structurally cleaner.
- **Continuous (0-1) scoring or fully binary classification.** Not in scope for this finding (it would be relevant for an applied operational variant of the framework); deferred.
- **Treating count "12" as load-bearing.** KILLED via the revisability clause: the count is the working empirical set, not a structural commitment.
- **REFINES as "the prior was almost-wrong".** KILLED via layer-shift semantics: the prior's claims are preserved at their level; this finding adds a level above. REFINES means deepens, not revises.
- **`/explore` spec change as REQUIRED.** KILLED via primary/secondary split: the meta-doc is primary; the `/explore` §1.7 addition is optional secondary.
- **Discipline-paradigm mapping for ALL disciplines as part of this finding.** KILLED via scope-control: deferred with a revival trigger (when each discipline's spec is being updated, add the paradigm-coverage there). Premature here.

### Research Frontiers

- **Composition algebra for paradigms.** When two paradigms compose (e.g., Process + Relational), what are the rules for the composite's properties? Is composition commutative? Are there primitive compositions from which all others derive? This is a real research question beyond per-inquiry scope.

- **The reductionist question.** Can the 12 paradigms be reduced to a smaller set (e.g., 4-5) by treating some as compositions of others? For example, Navigational = Cartographic + egocentric viewpoint + route-purpose. Whether this reduction is structurally clarifying or content-losing is itself a research question; the reduction would shift the framework's "what is preserved" axis to a more fundamental level.

- **The Reflexive paradigm's status.** P-REFL preserves the mapping operation itself. Is this a paradigm at the same level as the others, or is it a meta-modifier applicable to any paradigm (e.g., "a Cartographic-Reflexive map" would be a map that maps cartography itself)? The framework currently treats it as a paradigm in the 12-paradigm list; the alternative treatment as a meta-modifier is structurally defensible and not yet adjudicated.
