## Phase 2 — Adversarial Evaluation

### Candidate 1 — The Assembly "Meta-Paradigm Framework for Mapping v1"

#### Prosecution

**Dimension-level objections:**

- **D5 Robustness — PARTIAL FAIL.** Three calibration concerns surface: (a) Layer 1's prominence relative to the 12-paradigm enumeration; (b) revisability gate specification; (c) classification procedure's handling of axis-disagreement. All three are REFINE-able.

- **D10 Revisability-mechanism — WEAK PASS.** P3.3's revisability clause says "revisions should be triggered by structural arguments" but doesn't specify the gate format. The DEFERRED items list does specify gates ("≥3 inquiries surface unfitting mappings; ≥2 paradigm-pairs repeatedly merge"), but a reader of P3.3 alone wouldn't see this. Specification gap.

- **D13 Operation-parsimony — STRONG PASS.** Doc-only deliverable; one optional one-paragraph addition. No new disciplines, runners, or files (beyond the finding).

**Multi-axis prosecution depth check:**

**(a) User-perspective objection** — is this genuinely the META level the user wanted?

Strongest prosecution: *"The 12 paradigms include 6 that map directly onto the prior 7 kinds (CART = layout, TAX = concept, REL = partly-excluded relational, COV = coverage, CONST = frontier, POSS = possibility). The other 6 (FUNC, EMB, PROC, NAV, REFL, ANALOG) are just 'paradigms /explore doesn't produce.' The user could read this and say: 'You added 6 paradigms /explore doesn't produce and called it meta-level. The actual structure of mapping wasn't deepened — you enumerated more types.' The 12-paradigm table is the most visually prominent element of the finding; if the user reads that as the headline, they could reasonably conclude this is re-organization, not deepening."*

Defense: *"The structural deepening is at Layer 1, not Layer 3. The prior finding did NOT have a meta-definition; this finding does ('purposive structure-preserving correspondence from source to target, specified by 4 axes'). The prior did NOT have generative axes; this finding does. The 4 primary axes are not a list — they are a generative mechanism that produces the paradigm space. The 12 paradigms are clusters in that mechanism, not arbitrary additions. This IS meta-level deepening."*

Verdict: The defense wins structurally — Layer 1's meta-definition and Layer 2's axes are genuinely new and constitute the structural deepening. BUT the prosecution surfaces a real PRESENTATION concern: if the 12-paradigm table is the most prominent element, readers might miss the Layer 1 contribution and read this as re-organization. This is a REFINE concern, not a KILL concern.

**REFINE-1:** In the finding's `Finding Summary` and the body's opening, lead with Layer 1 (the minimum-core definition + 4 primary axes) BEFORE the 12-paradigm enumeration. Make explicit: "The structural deepening from the prior finding is at Layer 1 (the meta-definition + 4 generative axes); the 12 paradigms are recognizable clusters in that structure." This frames the 12-paradigm table as a consequence, not the headline.

**(b) Specification-gap probe** — does the revisability clause handle "what if paradigm 13 surfaces"?

Strongest prosecution: *"P3.3's revisability clause says 'revisions should be triggered by structural arguments grounded in observed mapping practice, not by terminological preference.' This is general guidance, not a specified gate. Who decides? When? What counts as a structural argument? The DEFERRED items section has concrete gates, but P3.3 itself doesn't. A reader of P3.3 might assume 'revisability' means 'anyone can revise anytime,' which would destabilize the framework."*

Defense: *"The DEFERRED items section's empirical refinement entry IS the gate specification ('≥3 inquiries surface mappings not fitting the 12 paradigms cleanly OR ≥2 paradigm-pairs repeatedly merge'). The clause and the gate are in different sections, but both are in the finding."*

Verdict: The prosecution wins this round. The gate specification is correct but split across two sections. P3.3 should be self-contained — readers should see the gate in the same place as the revisability clause.

**REFINE-2:** Strengthen P3.3 with explicit gate wording. Add: *"Revisions are triggered when any of the following observable conditions hold: (a) three or more inquiries surface mappings that don't fit any of the 12 paradigms cleanly; OR (b) two or more pairs of paradigms repeatedly merge in observation; OR (c) a paradigm's literature-grounding is contested by a structural argument (e.g., the named domain is shown to encompass multiple distinct mapping operations rather than a single paradigm). Until one of these triggers fires, the 12-paradigm enumeration is the working set."*

**(c) Failure-case scenario** — inconsistent classification:

Strongest prosecution: *"A reader applies the 4-axis classification to a turn-by-turn driving direction. Axis 1 (what preserved) says: 'this preserves spatial structure → Cartographic.' Axis 4 (purpose) says: 'this is for navigation → Navigational.' The axes point to DIFFERENT paradigms. The classification procedure says 'ask the axes in order' but doesn't say what to do when answers disagree. The reader is stuck — pick one wrong; or list both and the framework feels indecisive."*

Defense: *"The compositionality note in P3.2 says: 'Most real-world mappings compose multiple paradigms. ... When in doubt about which single paradigm applies, list the multiple paradigms the mapping composes; the framework permits this.' So the answer IS 'list both Cartographic and Navigational.' This case isn't actually a failure — it's the expected behavior."*

Verdict: The compositionality note ADDRESSES the case, but the procedure doesn't EXPLICITLY tell the reader "when axes disagree, this signals compositionality." A reader following the 4-axis procedure step-by-step might not connect the disagreement to the compositionality note (which appears as a separate paragraph). The procedure should make the disagreement-handling step explicit.

**REFINE-3:** Add an explicit disagreement-handling step to P3.2. After the 4 axis questions, add: *"If the four axes point to the same paradigm, the mapping is a single-paradigm instance. If two or more axes point to different paradigms, this is a signal of compositionality, not of confusion: the mapping is the composition of those paradigms. Record all paradigms identified by the axes. Single-paradigm membership applies only when the 4 axes converge."*

**(d) Layer-shift meta-lesson scrutiny** — is the meta-lesson within scope?

Strongest prosecution: *"The inquiry's question is about MAPPING, not about project vocabulary or relationship-label semantics. The meta-lesson on REFINES vs CORRECTS vs SUPERSEDES extends project vocabulary. By including it, the finding does two things at once: (1) answers the mapping question, and (2) extends project vocabulary. A reader expecting the first might find the second a digression. Worse: extending project vocabulary in a finding about mapping is the kind of move that distributes vocabulary changes across findings, making the vocabulary itself hard to track."*

Defense: *"The meta-lesson is internally required to justify the REFINES relationship to the prior finding. Without explaining WHY this is REFINES and not CORRECTS, the reader can't understand the inquiry's positioning. The meta-lesson is load-bearing for this finding's relationship semantics; the fact that it's extractable beyond this finding is emergent value, not over-reach. The meta-lesson's first half (the LOCAL claim: this finding is REFINES because it preserves prior + adds a layer) is in scope; the second half (the EXTRACTABLE general semantics for any future inquiry's REFINES/CORRECTS/SUPERSEDES choice) is the emergent contribution."*

Verdict: The defense wins on the LOCAL claim (the meta-lesson is required for THIS finding's REFINES to make sense). The prosecution surfaces a presentation concern about the EXTRACTABLE claim — readers might miss that the second half is meant to be reusable beyond this finding. This is a minor REFINE concern.

**REFINE-4:** In P4.2's meta-lesson paragraph, add a closing sentence that tags the extractable portion: *"This layer-shift heuristic is extractable beyond the immediate finding-to-prior relationship in this inquiry; future inquiries determining the REFINES / CORRECTS / SUPERSEDES choice for their own prior relationships can reuse the diagnosis (wrong claim at prior's level → CORRECTS; right claim at wrong level → REFINES with layer shift; fundamentally invalidated frame → SUPERSEDES)."*
