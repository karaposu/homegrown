### P1.1 — Minimum-core definition

Mechanism: **Constraint Manipulation** (single sentence; compositionally specifies the 4 primary axes).

**Generic candidate:**
> "Mapping is a structure-preserving correspondence from a source to a target."

**Focused candidate (committed):**
> **"Mapping is a purposive structure-preserving correspondence from a source to a target, specified by which structural feature of the source is preserved, what target medium encodes it, what operation produces it, and what purpose it serves."**

**Contrarian candidate:**
> "Mapping is the act of producing a navigable surrogate by selectively preserving structure."

**Choice:** Focused. It compositionally specifies the 4 primary axes inside the definition itself; reading the sentence already introduces the 4 axes that the next sub-piece names. The generic version under-specifies; the contrarian version over-narrows by anchoring on the surrogate/navigation framing (one purpose among many).

### P1.2 — Four primary axes

Mechanism: **Combination** (combines axis A "what-preserved" + axis B "encoding-type" + axis D "operational-act" + axis J "purpose" from exploration).

**Committed text:**

> **Primary axes of mapping (four).** Any specific mapping can be characterized by its values along four primary axes:
>
> 1. **What structural feature is preserved.** The mapping's core commitment: spatial relations, type membership, connection structure, transformation rules, similarity relationships, causal trajectories, boundary structure, generative rules, route structure, continuous values over a domain, the mapping operation itself, or cross-domain structural correspondence. (This axis generates the paradigm taxonomy in Layer 3.)
>
> 2. **What target medium encodes the source.** The representational form the map uses: iconic resemblance (visual/spatial), symbolic labels (discrete tags), indexical references (pointers/links), continuous embeddings (geometric space), discrete combinatorial structures (graphs, trees, lattices), or sequential ordering.
>
> 3. **What operation produces the map.** The act: recording (observing/transcribing), constructive inference (building from indirect evidence), compression (lossy summarization), projection (high-dim → low-dim with chosen perspective), generation (a generator function whose outputs are the map), or prediction (anticipating future/inferred states).
>
> 4. **What purpose the map serves.** The use: navigation, communication, prediction, decision-support, reasoning-substrate (the map IS the thinking medium), memory/archival, or coordination across actors.

---

### P1.3 — Stability note for Layer 1

> **Stability.** This layer (the minimum-core definition + the 4 primary axes) is stable across paradigm revisions. The definition specifies what mapping IS; the primary axes specify the dimensions any specific mapping varies along. Both hold across additions to or restructurings of Layer 3's paradigm enumeration.

### P2.1 — Eight secondary axes

Mechanism: Combination (refining within primary axes).

**Committed text:**

> **Secondary axes (eight; refine within primary axis cells).** Secondary axes further differentiate mappings that share primary axis values:
>
> - **Scope / grain.** What the map's unit is: element / structure / behavior / constraint / possibility / aggregate.
> - **Reference frame.** What the map is anchored to: absolute coordinates, relative to a chosen object, functional (relative to purpose), or egocentric (relative to the mapper).
> - **Meta-level.** Whether the map operates at the object level (mapping a territory) or at the meta level (mapping the act of mapping, the mapper's trajectory, or the map's own coverage edges).
> - **Origin.** Whether the map is derived a priori (from logical/structural principles), a posteriori (from observation), or via generative-rule application.
> - **Output form.** The map's shape as an artifact: catalog/list, graph/network, field/continuous surface, hierarchy/tree, manifold/topological space, trajectory/sequence, or matrix/table.
> - **Temporality.** Static snapshot, dynamic capture of change, counterfactual (what could be), historical (what was), or predictive (what will be).
> - **Fidelity.** Lossless (bijective; reversible), lossy (selected preservation), lossy-with-error-bound (quantified loss), or pathological (deliberate distortion for emphasis, like a subway map).
> - **Viewpoint.** Allocentric (god's-eye), egocentric (first-person), mechanistic (causal), categorical (classification), or analogical (cross-domain).

---

### P2.2 — Relationship to primary axes

> Secondary axes refine within the cells of the primary axes — they do not generate the paradigm space, but they distinguish near-neighbor paradigms that share primary values. For example, Cartographic and Navigational paradigms both preserve spatial structure (primary axis 1), but differ on viewpoint (Cartographic is allocentric; Navigational is egocentric). The boundary between primary and secondary is empirical; an axis could be promoted or demoted under finer structural analysis. **This layer is stable-ish.**

### P3.2 — Classification guidance

Mechanism: **Inversion** (what does a confused reader produce without this guidance? — they apply paradigm labels arbitrarily and assume single-paradigm membership when most real mappings are composite).

**Committed text:**

> **How to place a specific mapping into the framework.** To classify a mapping into one or more paradigms, ask the four primary axis questions in order:
>
> 1. **What does this mapping preserve?** The answer locates one or more candidate paradigms in the 12-paradigm enumeration (each paradigm is named by what it preserves).
> 2. **What target medium encodes it?** The answer narrows the candidates by representational form — e.g., a graph encoding distinguishes Relational from Taxonomic (which can be tree-encoded but also list-encoded).
> 3. **What operation produced it?** The answer narrows further by act — e.g., recording vs constructive inference vs generation distinguishes paradigms that share preserved-structure.
> 4. **What is the map's purpose?** The answer narrows by use — e.g., navigation-purpose distinguishes Navigational from Cartographic when both preserve spatial structure.
>
> **Compositionality is the rule, not the exception.** Most real-world mappings compose multiple paradigms. A process diagram is Process + Relational. A state-space heatmap is Coverage + Possibility. A semantic embedding visualization is Embedding + Cartographic. When in doubt about which single paradigm applies, list the multiple paradigms the mapping composes; the framework permits this and the composition itself often carries the structural information.

The 4 primary axes (Layer 2-primary) are stable-ish; axis promotions or demotions are possible under structural argument. The 12 paradigms (Layer 3) are explicitly revisable. The 8 secondary axes (Layer 2-secondary) are intermediate.

**Committed text:**

> **Prior path:** `devdocs/inquiries/2026-05-13_07-16__is_mapping_required_core_of_explore/finding.md` — the Identity Refresh v1 finding which established that mapping is core to `/explore` (the Structural Exploration discipline) and enumerated 7 observable kinds of mapping (layout, concept, status, coverage/confidence, frontier, possibility, and a partly-excluded relational type).
>
> **Revision trigger:** User correction. The prior finding's 7-kinds typology was, in the user's words, "good direction but not there exactly." The prior operated at the concrete-kinds level (what `/explore` is observed to produce). The user wanted the meta level — what mapping IS at the foundational level, and what categorical paradigms exist independent of any specific discipline.
>
> **What's preserved.** The prior finding's core claim — that mapping is the upstream cognitive operation core to `/explore` — is preserved without modification. The 7 kinds enumerated in the prior remain valid as `/explore`-scoped observations of what gets produced. The prior finding's relationship to `/explore`'s §1 Identity is preserved.
>
> **What's added.** A meta-paradigm framework that sits one structural level above the prior's 7-kinds typology. Specifically: (1) a minimum-core definition of mapping as a purposive structure-preserving correspondence from a source to a target, specified by 4 primary axes; (2) an enumeration of 12 crystallized paradigms, each grounded in established literature; (3) classification guidance for placing a new mapping into one or more paradigms; (4) a revisability clause and stability layering.
>
> **What's repositioned.** The prior's 7 kinds are repositioned as a `/explore`-scoped projection of the meta-paradigms. Each prior kind maps onto one or more of the 12 meta-paradigms (see the mapping table in the next sub-section). The prior's layer is preserved at its level; this finding adds the meta-layer above it.
>
> **No correction.** Nothing in the prior is wrong. The relationship is REFINES (deepens, adds a structural level) rather than CORRECTS (revises a wrong claim). The user's "not there exactly" was about LEVEL of abstraction, not about CONTENT of the prior's claims.
>
> **Meta-lesson — layer-shift semantics for REFINES vs CORRECTS vs SUPERSEDES.** When user feedback signals "good direction but not there exactly," the structural fix may be a layer shift (go one level deeper or broader in abstraction) rather than a lateral revision. Layer shifts preserve the prior at its level and add a new level above or below; lateral revisions correct a wrong claim at the same level. Distinguishing the two prevents two failure modes:
>
> 1. Unnecessary invalidation of prior work (treating a layer-shift situation as a correction situation; the prior gets thrown out when it should be preserved).
> 2. Staying at the wrong abstraction level (treating a correction situation as a layer-shift situation; the wrong claim survives because "the prior is preserved at its level," even though its level was the wrong level to make claims at).
>
> Future inquiries should diagnose the type of correction needed BEFORE choosing the relationship label: a wrong claim at the prior's level → CORRECTS; a right claim at the wrong level → REFINES with layer shift; a fundamentally invalidated frame → SUPERSEDES.
