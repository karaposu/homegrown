# Branch: What is Mapping — Meta-Level Definition and Paradigms

## Question

What is "mapping" at a meta-theoretical level, and what categorically distinct mapping paradigms exist — independent of any specific discipline like `/explore` and independent of any specific observed kind (layout, concept, status, etc.) — such that the set of paradigms is exhaustive (or close to it) and structurally grounded rather than enumerated from examples?

## Goal

A meta-level definition of mapping plus an enumeration of mapping paradigms (the categorical types, not the concrete kinds), grounded in first principles rather than discipline-bound observation. The answer should be usable to:
(a) ground `/explore`'s identity (mapping IS the core, but mapping is — what, exactly?) more deeply than the prior identity-refresh finding's 7-kinds enumeration;
(b) classify any specific mapping act (e.g., a `/explore` run, a `/sense-making` run, a human reading a codebase) into one or more paradigms;
(c) reveal whether the prior 7-kinds typology partitions the space cleanly, partially, or only by example;
(d) inform future discipline design (do we need disciplines per-paradigm? do paradigms compose?).

## Scope Check

Question covers goal. The question asks for (a) a meta-definition of mapping and (b) the categorical paradigms. The goal asks for the same plus its practical applicability. The question's "meta-theoretical level" framing matches the goal's "categorical types, not concrete kinds."

Specific-vs-pattern check: The user explicitly stated the prior finding's 7-kinds list (layout, concept, status, coverage/confidence, frontier, possibility, partly-excluded relational) was "good direction but not there exactly." This inquiry addresses the BROADER PATTERN — the meta-paradigms underneath those observed kinds — not the specific 7 examples. The 7 kinds are data; the paradigms are the structural categories the data instances belong to.

This inquiry is meta-theoretical first, applied second. The applied use (grounding `/explore`'s identity, classifying any mapping act) follows from the meta-definition; the meta-definition does not depend on `/explore`.
