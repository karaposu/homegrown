## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity A1: Form-(ii) trigger labeling-compatibility (FQ1; LBT1)

**Ambiguity:** the trigger phrase "produced when the inquiry's deliverable requires per-candidate assessment on shared criteria" is ambiguous between:
- (A) **Descriptive (labeling-compatible):** "form-(ii) outputs share the property of being produced for deliverables that require per-candidate assessment." Shape-characterization.
- (B) **Prescriptive (anchor-extraction territory):** "produce form-(ii) WHEN you judge the deliverable to require per-candidate assessment." Runner-judgment-required.

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** (B) is the natural reading. The phrase says "produced when" — that reads as a prescription for when to produce.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the phrase appears as a CLAUSE describing form-(ii). In context, it's characterizing the form, not prescribing runner behavior. A scanner looking at an output that IS form-(ii) (a pros/cons table) can describe: "this was produced because the inquiry's deliverable required per-candidate assessment." That's retrospective characterization. The §4.4 heuristic asks "would naive scanners produce the same description of THIS item?" — looking at a form-(ii) output, multiple scanners would describe its purpose-of-production similarly.

The forward-looking interpretation (when to produce) would require the runner to judge deliverable requirements — but the spec text doesn't ask the runner to judge; it describes the form. Runners observe what their inquiry's output IS, not prescriptively decide what it SHOULD be.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH (interpretation depends on reading; the descriptive reading is the structurally cleaner one per §4.4).

**Resolution:** KEEP the current trigger wording with the explicit interpretation that it is DESCRIPTIVE (characterizing form-(ii) outputs), NOT PRESCRIPTIVE (telling runners when to produce form-(ii)). This interpretation is consistent with /explore's purposive open-mode surfacing identity (§1.1) — purpose biases what the inquiry produces, and the form characterizes purpose-driven outputs.

**What is fixed:** the trigger phrase stays as-is with descriptive interpretation.
**What is no longer allowed:** prescriptive readings that require runner-judgment of deliverable.
**What depends:** Critique adversarial prosecution of the labeling-compatibility claim.

### Ambiguity A2: §4.1 #6 sharpening (FQ2)

**Ambiguity:** keep "(see §4.1)" general reference or sharpen to "(see §4.1 #6)"?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** sharpen — more specific cross-references are operationally better; the 21-05-00 redesign explicitly used "§4.1 #6" verbatim.

**Why the counter is partially valid:** specificity helps future readers.

**Why the counter fails on scope grounds:** this inquiry's scope is OPERATIONALIZING 21-07-00's partial-warrant verdict. §4.1 #6 sharpening is a separate cosmetic improvement; it's NOT REQUIRED by 21-07-00. Adding it would extend scope beyond identity-restoration.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH (scope-discipline argument).

**Resolution:** KEEP current "(see §4.1)" reference. Cosmetic sharpening is a separate future edit.

### Ambiguity A3: "The output takes one of two forms:" preamble (FQ3)

**Strongest counter:** omit — the 2 sub-bullets could appear directly after the Possibility mode bullet's existing content without preamble.

**Why the counter fails:** without preamble, the sub-bullets feel orphaned. The preamble provides cognitive anchor for readers ("here are the two forms"). It's one short clause; no operational cost.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Resolution:** KEEP the preamble "The output takes one of two forms:" before the 2 sub-bullets.
