The finding serves three reader audiences: (1) **future spec-maintainer** navigating innovate's preserved-frontier landscape; (2) **future inquiry-author** adjudicating a new preserved-frontier candidate; (3) **cross-discipline applier** assessing whether the framework applies to a discipline beyond innovate.

### 1. The typed framework's 2-level structure

The framework codifies preserved-frontier resolution at 2 LEVELS:

- **Level 1 — Candidate-level templates.** Classifies preserved-frontier candidates by structural identity. A candidate enters the framework with a question: "what kind of preserved frontier is this — what promotion path applies?" Level 1's templates answer this question. Currently 3 named templates (T-A capability-gap; T-B structural-consolidation; T-C distributed-fix META-observation); enumeration is OPEN — future T-E+ templates may emerge as new candidate-types surface.

- **Level 2 — Commit-form calibration mechanisms.** Operates at per-sub-piece scope WITHIN a candidate's commit decision. When a template's promotion path leads to a commit (or to a commit candidate adjudication), Level 2 mechanisms calibrate the form of the commit text per sub-piece. Currently 1 named mechanism (4-alternative-analysis: Alt D full / Alt A mid / Alt B see-also / Alt C status quo, established at the 05-00 Q1 deep dive + applied asymmetrically at the 06-00 Q4 deep dive); enumeration OPEN — future mechanisms may emerge.

**Scope-level distinction.** Level 1 operates at per-candidate scope (classify the candidate). Level 2 operates at per-sub-piece scope WITHIN a candidate's commit decision (calibrate the form). These are DIFFERENT cognitive operations at different scope-levels — conflating them under a flat 4-template structure would obscure the distinction operationally.

Both levels carry OPEN enumeration commitments. The framework is intentionally non-closed; future candidates + mechanisms invited.

### 2. Decision tree — classifying a candidate into a template

Level 1 classification operates via 3 sequential tests with gated outcomes:

1. **Test 1 — Scope-coverage check.** Does any existing rule in the discipline operate at the candidate's scope?
   - Result: NONE / PARTIAL / FULL.

2. **Test 2 — Role check.** (Gated by Test 1 = PARTIAL.) Would the candidate UNIFY existing rules under a single meta-rule, or ADD a new defense-in-depth vantage?
   - Result: UNIFY / ADD.

3. **Test 3 — Fix-wise unity check.** (Gated by Test 2 = UNIFY.) Does a single rule fix the candidate's pattern, or are fixes distributed across multiple primary mechanisms?
   - Result: YES (single rule) / NO (distributed).

```
Test 1 (scope-coverage)
├── NONE → T-A capability-gap
├── PARTIAL → Test 2 (role)
│   ├── ADD → T-A (capability-gap; with partial-coverage acknowledgment)
│   └── UNIFY → Test 3 (fix-wise unity)
│       ├── YES → T-B structural-consolidation
│       └── NO → T-C distributed-fix META-observation
└── FULL → edge case (research-frontier; NOT named template at current calibration)
```

**4 reachable named leaves + 1 edge case.**

**Why sequential, not multi-dimensional?** Test 2 is undefined for Test 1 = NONE (no coverage to assess role over) or = FULL (no role-question; candidate is redundant). Test 3 is undefined for Test 2 = ADD (no fix-wise-unity question; candidate adds new vantage rather than unifying existing rules). Tests are GATED by prior outcomes; a 12-cell multi-dimensional lookup would have UNDEFINED cells. Sequential decision tree is structurally correct.

**FULL-coverage edge case.** A candidate whose scope is FULLY covered by existing rules classifies to NONE OF THE NAMED TEMPLATES. The current framework treats this as research-frontier territory; no worked example exists; a 5th classification (T-Z "redundant") is NOT introduced because the cases observed don't warrant it. Future inquiry adjudicates if/when a FULL-coverage candidate emerges.

#### 3.4 Cross-template observation

The 3 templates share a 5-element articulation structure (name; structural identity; worked example; promotion path; trigger style) — the parallelism makes the framework's regularity visible. The 6th implicit element (trigger-outcome) is articulated cross-template at the next section.

### 4. Trigger-outcome semantics — 3 structurally distinct categories

When a template's trigger fires, what HAPPENS varies in structure across templates — not just in description. The 3 categories:

- **T-A trigger fires → COMMIT.** The trigger advancement PRODUCES the spec edit (via branch-experiment → spec-edit inquiry path). The trigger is necessary AND sufficient (with the design + edit inquiry steps) for the candidate to become a live rule in the spec. The path from trigger firing to commit is direct.

- **T-B trigger fires → PROMOTION-INQUIRY ADJUDICATION.** The trigger advancement OPENS a new inquiry; the inquiry then adjudicates whether to commit OR further preserve. The trigger is necessary but NOT sufficient for commit. The adjudication may use Level 2 mechanisms (e.g., 4-alternative-analysis per-sub-piece) to decide the form of commitment, OR may decide to defer commitment again under refined trigger conditions.

- **T-C trigger fires → META-RULE RE-EXAMINATION.** The trigger advancement OPENS a re-examination of whether a META-RULE is warranted. The outcome may be (a) NO META-RULE committed (preserve the constellation's redundancy + extend the META-observation's documentation); (b) META-RULE committed (extends the framework with a new rule + possibly a new template at Level 1); (c) framework extension without a META-RULE (e.g., update the META-observation's sub-pattern enumeration). The trigger is NOT a promotion mechanism in the T-A or T-B sense.

**Why distinguishing matters.** Conflating these three would be the structural error the codification's anti-silent-widening discipline prevents. The 22-00 synthesis's silent-widening incident (subsuming Pair 12 under ADD-MULTI-AXIS's strict trigger) was structurally a conflation between T-B's trigger-outcome (promotion-inquiry adjudication) and T-C's trigger-outcome (META-RULE re-examination, which is NOT promotion). Explicit category articulation prevents future incidents.

**Cross-template common structure.** All 3 triggers are:
- **Observation-driven** (track empirical evidence; no design-event advances the count).
- **Strict-reading honoring** (original specifications preserved; no silent widening).
- **Per-template granularity** (each template's trigger style + outcome are template-specific; not interchangeable).

Common structure across the templates' triggers; distinct outcomes per template.

### 5. Transferability beyond the originating discipline

The framework's structural identity is discipline-agnostic at the meaning layer. The 3 named templates (T-A / T-B / T-C) + 3-test decision tree + per-template promotion paths apply to ANY cognitive discipline that satisfies 4 necessary conditions:

1. **Preserved-frontier candidates exist.** The concept of "candidate rule emerged from cases but not immediately committed" must be operational in the discipline.

2. **Evidence-accumulation mechanism.** Diagnostic cases must accumulate across multiple inquiries (LOOP_DIAGNOSE-like cross-case observation).

3. **Commit mechanism.** Spec edits or analogous structural commitments must be possible.

4. **Named structural elements.** The discipline must have named elements (rules; steps; mechanisms; anchors; or analogous) that can be classified + discussed structurally.

Conditions list OPEN — future per-discipline applications may surface conditions not yet identified.

**Per-discipline applicability sketches:**

- **intuit** (project's intuition discipline): if preserved-frontier candidates exist + intuit has named mechanisms, framework applies. Adaptation: intuit's "fix" vocabulary differs from innovate's mechanism + axis vocabulary; templates' per-mechanism content adapts per-discipline.
- **comprehend** (modeling discipline): likely applies; anchor + model vocabulary substitutes for innovate's mechanism vocabulary.
- **sense-making refinement**: the discipline has had refinements (some preserved; some promoted); framework's 3 templates may apply with adapted worked examples.
- **protocols** (CONCLUDE; LOOP_DIAGNOSE): condition 4 (named structural elements) is satisfied operationally (protocols have named steps); but protocols typically don't have "rule preservation" in the innovate sense — framework's direct application may need protocol-specific adaptation.

**Cross-frontier coupling with Layer-3 §9 trigger redesign.**

The framework's T-A template uses a cumulative-N count trigger. Layer-3 §9 (in innovate's spec at Phase 1 Seed) uses a count-based override trigger. Both are count-based. The "discipline-prevents-Layer-3-advancement" emergent pattern (now spanning 9 cumulative inquiries with disciplined no-override) suggests count-based triggers may not advance reliably under disciplined operation.

**META-level coupling, SUBSTANCE-distinct.** T-A counts EVIDENCE-ACCUMULATION (advances with new diagnostic cases; not suppressed by disciplined operation — new cases happen organically). Layer-3 §9 counts INCIDENT-RECORDING (advances with override events; suppressed by disciplined operation). The substance differs:

- T-A's count: external event-rate (cases accumulating).
- Layer-3 §9's count: internal-discipline event-rate (overrides being recorded).

T-A is less vulnerable to the discipline-prevents-advancement pattern than Layer-3 §9. The cross-frontier coupling exists at the META level (both count-based; trigger-design questions apply to both) but is substance-distinct (different what's-being-counted).

Future trigger-design meta-inquiry (Layer-3 §9 redesign frontier per `docs/unanswered_frontiers.md`) would apply to both at the META level; the substance-distinct analysis would inform the redesign.
