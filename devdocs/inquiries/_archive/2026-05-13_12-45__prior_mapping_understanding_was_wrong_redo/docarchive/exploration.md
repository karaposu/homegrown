### Region J — The "lesson-introduces-its-own-trap" pattern (D3 probe; jump-scan addition)

The just-completed finding demonstrated a pattern worth naming explicitly: a new vocabulary introduced in a lesson can become a VECTOR for the failure mode the lesson names.

**Mechanism:**

1. A discipline runs and surfaces a failure mode (e.g., "wrong claims survive because the prior is preserved at its level").
2. The discipline names the failure mode and introduces vocabulary to discuss it (e.g., "layer-shift semantics").
3. The vocabulary is APPEALING because it neatly resolves the tension (preservation of prior + addition of new = layer shift).
4. The lesson includes a WARNING (don't use this when the prior's claim is actually wrong).
5. The warning requires a DIAGNOSTIC CHECK (is the prior's claim actually right at its level?).
6. The diagnostic check is HARDER than just applying the new vocabulary.
7. Path of least resistance: apply the vocabulary without the check.
8. Result: the failure mode is COMMITTED using the vocabulary that was supposed to prevent it.

**Why this is recognizable as a pattern:**

- The new vocabulary's appeal makes the warning easy to skip
- The check requires self-suspicion that the vocabulary's own framing discourages
- The pattern occurs WITHIN A SINGLE FINDING (introducing the lesson and then committing the failure), making it especially insidious

**Prevention:**

- Make the diagnostic OBLIGATORY before the first application of the new vocabulary
- Make the failure case CONCRETE in the lesson (a worked example)
- Pair the lesson with a counter-question that requires explicit answer ("have I tested whether this case actually fits, or am I applying the vocabulary because it's available?")
- Surface the pattern itself as a meta-failure-mode (a lesson can become a trap for the discipline that introduces it)
