## Phase 2 — Adversarial Evaluation

### Candidate 1 — The Assembly "Meta-Paradigm Framework for Mapping v2 — CORRECTS edition"

#### Prosecution

**Dimension-level objections:**

- **D5 Robustness — PARTIAL FAIL.** Four operational-clarity concerns surface: (a) the diagnostic's enforcement is rhetorical, not mechanical; (b) the 3 questions admit interpretive flexibility; (c) UNCLEAR diagnostic results are not explicitly mapped to CORRECTS in the decision rule wording; (d) the strengthened vocabulary could be invoked as a trump card. All four are REFINE-able.

- **D8 Diagnostic-operational-clarity — WEAK PASS.** The questions ("true at claimed level?", "coherent and useful level?", "different context?") are interpretive. Structural framing channels interpretation but two reasoners could still disagree on edge cases. Illustrative examples per question would anchor consistency.

- **D9 Default-handling — WEAK PASS.** "Default to CORRECTS under uncertainty" handles UNCLEAR results in principle, but the decision rule wording ("any NO → CORRECTS") doesn't explicitly say "UNCLEAR → CORRECTS." A reasoner might interpret UNCLEAR as not-NO and proceed to REFINES.

**Multi-axis prosecution depth check:**

**(a) User-perspective objection** — does the strengthened diagnostic ACTUALLY prevent the failure?

Strongest prosecution: *"The strengthened diagnostic is text in a finding. It doesn't 'prevent' anything; it just describes what should happen. A future inquiry could read the lesson, nod, and then commit the same failure because the path of least resistance still wins. The diagnostic's force depends on it being APPLIED — but nothing in the system mechanically ENFORCES application. The 'obligatory' MUST is rhetorical, not mechanical. The just-completed paradigm finding also had warnings in its meta-lesson; the warnings did not prevent the failure. Why would this stronger warning succeed where the previous warning failed?"*

Defense: *"The diagnostic's force is rhetorical, true. But rhetoric matters in stateless LLM-driven disciplines. Three reinforcements operate: (i) the worked example shows the failure concretely — a reader sees what going wrong looks like; (ii) the self-reference acknowledgment shows the diagnostic applied successfully on the case that motivated it; (iii) the MUST keyword aligns with project conventions that runners respect. The combination raises the threshold significantly even if it doesn't guarantee prevention. The just-completed finding's warning was abstract description; this lesson is description + demonstration + self-application + named failure modes. The probability of catching the failure is higher."*

Verdict: defense wins narrowly. The prosecution surfaces a real concern (rhetorical enforcement); the response (worked example + self-reference + named failure modes) is adequate but improvable. REFINE-worthy.

**REFINE-1:** Add a behavioral pointer to the strengthened lesson — explicit action when about to declare REFINES based on level-distinction alone. E.g., "If you find yourself about to declare REFINES because the prior is at a different level, STOP and run the 3-question check. The check takes 1-2 minutes; the failure takes much longer to undo."

**(b) Specification-gap probe** — is the diagnostic operationally clear?

Strongest prosecution: *"Each question has interpretive flexibility. 'True at claimed level' — true by what standard (logical consistency, empirical evidence, user adjudication)? 'Coherent and useful level' — coherent to whom, useful for what? 'Different context' — what context counts? Two reasoners applying the diagnostic to the same prior might arrive at different verdicts because the questions admit interpretation. The diagnostic is structurally framed but operationally underspecified."*

Defense: *"The questions ARE interpretive — that's the point. They force STRUCTURAL reasoning rather than precedent-citation. 'True at its claimed level' is more rigorous than 'plausible'; 'coherent and useful level' is more rigorous than 'reasonable.' The structural framing channels interpretation toward consistency. Plus, the worked example provides a reference point: future reasoners can compare their case to the just-completed finding's case and see how the answers look there."*

Verdict: defense holds, but the prosecution wins on the wording-clarity sub-issue. Illustrative examples per question would anchor interpretation more strongly.

**REFINE-2:** Add brief illustrative examples to each diagnostic question. E.g., for Claim-truth test: "YES example: a prior claiming 'this method runs in O(n)' that empirically does. NO example: the prior 7-kinds claiming to be a typology when no axes generate it." Each question gets a YES + a NO example.

**(c) Failure-case scenario** — ambiguous diagnostic results?

Strongest prosecution: *"The decision rule says 'any NO → CORRECTS.' But what if a reasoner can't cleanly answer NO; they can only answer 'UNCLEAR'? The decision rule treats UNCLEAR as not-NO, which would tip toward REFINES. The default-to-CORRECTS rule says 'default to CORRECTS under uncertainty,' but the decision-rule sentence doesn't explicitly map UNCLEAR → CORRECTS. A reasoner reading the rule might interpret UNCLEAR as 'no positive NO, so REFINES.' This is exactly the path of least resistance the lesson is correcting."*

Defense: *"The default-to-CORRECTS rule explicitly says 'under uncertainty.' UNCLEAR is uncertainty; default applies. The text is internally consistent; the prosecution's reading is a misreading."*

Verdict: defense holds on internal consistency, but the prosecution wins on wording-precision. The decision rule and the default rule are stated separately; a single-pass reader might apply only the decision rule.

**REFINE-3:** Strengthen the decision rule wording. E.g., "Decision rule: any explicit NO → CORRECTS. Any UNCLEAR answer also → CORRECTS (the default-to-CORRECTS rule applies; UNCLEAR is uncertainty, and uncertainty defaults to CORRECTS to keep the burden of proof on REFINES). Three explicit YES answers → REFINES is structurally appropriate."

**(d) Self-reference scrutiny** — genuine grounding or self-congratulation?

Strongest prosecution: *"P3.8 says 'the strengthened diagnostic, applied to this finding's own choice, passes.' This is the finding endorsing itself. The acknowledgment looks rigorous but it's circular — the diagnostic was designed with this case in mind; of course it passes. A genuine grounding would be independent: a critic, a different domain, empirical observation. Self-application of the diagnostic is informative but not independent."*

Defense: *"P3.8 grounds the test in EXTERNAL signals — the user's correction and the diagnostic applied to the just-completed finding (yielding three NOs). The self-application is the confirmation, not the primary grounding. The test could have failed: if applying the diagnostic to THIS finding's CORRECTS yielded a NO, the finding would have to revisit its own choice. The acknowledgment also explicitly notes the regress concern and bounds it."*

Verdict: defense wins on the structural argument but the prosecution surfaces a worth-noting concern: the diagnostic was developed in light of this case, so the test's passing is partly tautological. The acknowledgment could be more honest about this.

**REFINE-4:** Add a sentence to P3.8 acknowledging the partial tautology. E.g., "The diagnostic was developed in light of this case, so its passing here is informative but not fully independent grounding. Stronger grounding comes from the user's external correction and from future cases where the diagnostic is applied to inquiries not yet seen; if the diagnostic produces robust verdicts on those, it earns confidence beyond this self-application."

**(e) Lesson-introduces-its-own-trap recursion** — can THIS lesson become a vector for ITS OWN trap?

Strongest prosecution: *"The strengthened lesson introduces new vocabulary: 'obligatory diagnostic,' 'preservation-for-preservation's-sake,' 'lesson-introduces-its-own-trap,' 'default-to-CORRECTS.' By the lesson's own meta-pattern, this new vocabulary can itself become a vector for the failure mode it names. A future inquiry could invoke 'lesson-introduces-its-own-trap' as a trump card to win arguments without applying the diagnostic. Or worse: a future inquiry could invoke 'default-to-CORRECTS' to invalidate prior work without doing the structural check. The vocabulary's appeal makes its warning easy to skip — exactly the pattern the lesson names. The lesson is susceptible to its own trap."*

Defense: *"The lesson acknowledges this in P3.5's 'Acknowledged scope' subsection: 'The strengthened meta-lesson here is itself an instance of new vocabulary... this finding pairs the vocabulary with its concrete worked example and applies it to its own choice of CORRECTS in the self-reference acknowledgment.' The pairing (vocabulary + worked example + self-application) is the prevention mechanism the lesson itself prescribes. The lesson eats its own dog food."*

Verdict: defense wins but the prosecution surfaces a worth-mentioning concern. The lesson's acknowledgment could be more explicit about "vocabulary is not a trump card; invoking it requires running the diagnostic."

**REFINE-5:** Add an explicit warning to P3.5 (or a new subsection) — the strengthened vocabulary (named failure modes; obligatory diagnostic; default-to-CORRECTS) is not a trump card. Invoking any of them in a future inquiry requires running the obligatory diagnostic on the specific case. Otherwise the vocabulary becomes itself a vector for the failure mode it names.
