## Phase 2 — Perspective Checking

### Technical / Logical perspective

The count-based trigger has well-defined operational semantics: increment on each `Methodology-mode-alternative-marked-inapplicable` recording; threshold at N=5. The mechanism is structurally simple but has an implicit assumption: "the rate of override recordings approximates the rate of override-quality-decay." Under disciplined operation, this assumption is FALSE because the relationship between count and formulaicness is broken (count doesn't advance even as formulaicness signal — "shared structural pattern" — was already visible at N=4 per 22-00).

**New anchor:** SP11 — the count's implicit assumption (count ≈ formulaicness) is structurally invalid; the count tracks a PROXY, not the target signal.

### Human / User perspective

The user identified Frontier #7 as BIG benefit and explicitly framed the trigger as "broken under disciplined operation." The user's framing suggests they want redesign, not removal. But the user's "dive deep" language doesn't preclude the inquiry from concluding "the rule should be redesigned at a deeper level than just the trigger" or even "the rule's purpose is the wrong question." Trust the user's framing to set scope (trigger-focused) but allow the inquiry to surface alternative framings if structurally warranted.

**New anchor:** KI9 — the user's framing presupposes the trigger needs redesign; the inquiry should test this presupposition (per _branch.md item (g) frame-level alternative) and produce findings even if they conflict with the presupposition.

### Strategic / Long-term perspective

Layer-3 §9's protective purpose serves project-level discipline. The trigger is intended to fire after enough accumulated evidence to investigate whether the override mechanism has become formulaic. If the trigger doesn't fire because discipline prevents the formulaicness, this is operationally good (discipline working) but mechanistically problematic (trigger silent). Long-term: discipline can erode; future inquiries (with different runners, different sensemaking depth) might not produce the same disciplined-no-override pattern. The trigger needs to be ready to fire when discipline weakens.

Strategically, the trigger should fire on EARLY signals of discipline-weakening, not on accumulated formulaicness only. This argues for a structural-ambiguity-detection-based or conjunctive-signal-based form (which can fire on early pattern signals) rather than pure count-based.

**New anchor:** SP12 — strategic design intent = early-detection of discipline-weakening, not late-detection of accumulated formulaicness. The current trigger is late-detection; redesign should consider early-detection.

### Risk / Failure perspective

Failure modes of the current count-based trigger:
- **F1.** Count never advances under discipline → trigger silent → unable to detect formulaicness that emerges in non-override paths (e.g., consideration-step rejections becoming templated).
- **F2.** Count advances but pattern-recurrence not measured → trigger fires on event-rate alone, missing or false-positive depending on whether events are diverse.
- **F3.** Trigger fires correctly → investigation → but the investigation's output is "compliance-criterion strengthening" which assumes the criterion exists; if the criterion is the wrong concept (e.g., if compliance is satisfied vacuously), strengthening the criterion doesn't help.

Failure modes of redesigned alternatives:
- Structural-ambiguity-detection-based: who detects the pattern? Risk: pattern-detection requires reading multiple inquiries' overrides; if done by individual inquiry runners, perspective is too narrow.
- Conjunctive-signal-based: more signals = harder to fire = trigger never fires from the other direction.
- Self-referential / observation-based: relies on critique-level observation; if critique is disciplined, may also miss.
- Mechanism-redundancy-based: requires monitoring upstream sensemaking patterns; cross-discipline visibility needed.

**New anchor:** KI10 — every trigger form has its own failure modes. The redesign isn't about picking "the right" form; it's about picking a form whose failure modes are STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT from the current count-based form's failure modes, ideally complementary in a multi-signal design.

### Resource / Feasibility perspective

The 8 alternative trigger forms have different cost profiles:
- Count-based: low cost (just count events).
- Period-based: low cost (just count inquiries).
- Calibration-state-based: medium cost (track state shifts; define what "calibration state shift" means operationally).
- Structural-ambiguity-detection-based: medium-high cost (define + detect patterns in override reasons).
- Conjunctive-signal-based: medium-high cost (multiple signals must be tracked).
- Self-referential / observation-based: low cost (offloads to critique discipline; relies on existing observation channel).
- Negative-evidence-based: medium cost (track inquiries where override was available but not taken).
- Mechanism-redundancy-based: high cost (cross-discipline monitoring; what counts as "upstream adjudicating"?).

For a MEANING-layer artifact, cost doesn't gate the choice; the structural-rightness criterion gates. But the future STRUCTURAL inquiry will need to weigh feasibility. Sensemaking should flag the cost-spread for downstream.

**New anchor:** SP13 — cost-spread across 8 trigger forms is wide (low to high). Cost matters for the future STRUCTURAL commit but not for the MEANING adjudication. Flag for downstream.

### Definitional / Internal Consistency perspective

Check: does the §9 rule contradict itself? The rule says (i) identify inherited mode; (ii) generate alternative; (iii) state what follows; (iv) decide (default / mode-switch / override). The compliance criterion at the artifact says the seed/preamble section must contain (a) inherited mode named; (b) ≥1 alternative named; (c) "what follows" description; (d) decision. 

Internal tension: step (iv) names 3 outcomes; the compliance criterion requires recording the decision but doesn't distinguish "default because alternative was inapplicable" (which is operationally what ACTIVE-NO-OVERRIDE cases do) from "default because inherited mode is right" (which is the unconsidered baseline). The rule's structure permits documenting the reasoning for choosing default in narrative form WITHOUT entering the override path. This is what 5/5 ACTIVE-NO-OVERRIDE production cases did.

The rule's outcome enumeration (3 outcomes) is INCONSISTENT with the compliance criterion (which accepts narrative reasoning at step (iv) without distinguishing 4 implicit outcomes). The internal gap is real.

**New anchor:** KI11 — the §9 rule has an internal definitional gap: outcome enumeration (3 outcomes) vs compliance criterion (permits 4 operational outcomes implicitly). The gap explains the 5/5 ACTIVE-NO-OVERRIDE pattern.
