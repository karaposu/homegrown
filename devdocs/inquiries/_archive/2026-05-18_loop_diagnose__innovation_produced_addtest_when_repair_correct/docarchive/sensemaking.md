### Ambiguity 1: Is Q3 (from the prior diagnostic) genuinely necessary-but-not-sufficient, or does the failure live elsewhere?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Q3 IS sufficient if applied with proper judgment by the runner; the failure in `14-00` is a one-off discipline-application miss (the runner didn't apply judgment about which axis to invert on), not a spec-level gap.

**Why the counter-interpretation fails (structural grounds):** Q3's compliance criterion is artifact-observable. The criterion checks for: (a) principal candidate present; (b) Inversion-candidate paragraph naming assumption and reversal; (c) 5-test cycle on both. `14-00` satisfies all three sub-criteria — the artifact contains an Inversion-candidate paragraph ("turn check inward") with a named assumption ("the check operates on external content") and a stated reversal ("the check operates on internal content"), and the 5-test cycle is present. Adding "judgment" to the criterion as a fix moves the criterion from artifact-observable to runner-interpretable; this weakens the rule's enforceability. The structural problem is that the criterion's bar is set at piece-level, not axis-level. Setting the bar at the right level (axis-level for pieces with intervention-shape commitments) is a spec-text fix, not a judgment-quality fix.

**Confidence:** HIGH. The counter requires Q3 to be effectively unenforceable (relying on runner judgment that isn't artifact-checkable); the structural fix preserves Q3's enforceability while extending its coverage.

**Resolution:** Q3 is genuinely necessary-but-not-sufficient. The failure lives at the axis-of-inversion level, which Q3's compliance criterion doesn't reach. The fix is either an extension to Q3 (specify axis) or a separate parallel rule (axis-of-inversion at meta-decision pieces with intervention-shape commitments).

**What is now fixed?** The relationship between this diagnostic and the prior 2026-05-18 diagnostic: composition, not replacement. Q3 stands for what it catches; the new structure addresses what Q3 doesn't reach.

**What is no longer allowed?** Treating Q3 as fully sufficient for catching piece-level Inversion failures across all axes.

**What now depends on this choice?** The downstream maintenance candidates' framing: they extend or compose with the prior refinement-set, not replace it.

**What changed in the conceptual model?** SV2's two-anchor model (vocabulary absence + axis-specification absence) gains a third anchor at the prior-rule-level: Q3's compliance criterion stops at piece-level and doesn't reach axis-level. The three anchors are layered: spec vocabulary (root) → prior-rule prescription (mid) → discipline application (operational).

### Ambiguity 2: Is "intervention shape" a real structural concept or a proxy?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** "Intervention shape" is just a verbal label for different content-with-different-verbs (ADD vs REPAIR vs REMOVE are different content with different verbs). There's no deeper structural distinction.

**Why the counter-interpretation fails (structural grounds):** ADD, REPAIR, REMOVE, REVERT, REFRAME-AS-BUG are structurally distinct operations on the spec:

- ADD operates on the spec by appending: no existing text changes; new text is added.
- REPAIR operates on existing text: existing text is modified in place; size may grow or shrink; new content fills the modification.
- REMOVE operates on existing text: existing text is deleted; size shrinks; no replacement.
- REVERT operates on the spec's history: current text is replaced with a prior version's text; size may change in either direction; no NEW content.
- REFRAME-AS-BUG operates at the failure-mode-categorization level: instead of treating the failure as a pattern requiring a new defense, it is treated as a localized bug requiring fix-and-move-on.

Each shape has a distinct cost profile (REPAIR has higher risk of breaking adjacent functionality than ADD); a distinct verification path (REVERT can be verified by diff-against-prior; REPAIR needs structural verification); a distinct downstream-effect profile (ADD doesn't change existing behavior; REPAIR/REMOVE/REVERT do). The user's correction in this case is explicitly at this level: "remove it" (REMOVE-shape) "not just add more tests" (not-ADD-shape). The counter fails: the structural distinction is operationally generative.

**Confidence:** HIGH. The structural distinction is grounded in the spec-text-operations + the cost/verification/downstream profiles + the user's explicit language.

**Resolution:** Intervention shape is a real structural concept. The case-evidence + the project's correction-chain history at Pair #7 + the substantive content of the nine enumerated shapes converge.

**What is now fixed?** Intervention shape is a structural anchor of the conceptual model.

**What is no longer allowed?** Treating intervention-shape as a verbal label without operational consequences. Future maintenance candidates that propose interventions must commit to a shape and that commitment is artifact-observable.

**What now depends on this choice?** The maintenance-candidate territory includes a vocabulary enumeration of intervention shapes (P-Maintenance-A); downstream candidates can reference the vocabulary.

**What changed in the conceptual model?** MN2 (intervention shape) is committed as a structural concept with operational consequences.

### Ambiguity 3: Is the case Gap-2 (vocabulary absence) or axis-application gap (existing mechanisms applied to the wrong axis), or both?
