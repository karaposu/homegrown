## Finding

### Context — why this correction was needed

The user's correction (*"meta operations sounds like sth belong to td-critique"*) targeted the prior finding's most load-bearing architectural commitment: that `/innovate` should expand from a 2-operation discipline (Generation + Framing) to a 3-operation discipline (adding a new "Meta-Operations" top-level category). The new category was supposed to house mechanisms whose output targets the loop's procedure / history / output-confidence rather than conceptual content.

On adversarial test, that expansion fails. The proposed Meta-Operations don't add new cognitive operations to the harness — they re-implement operations that `/td-critique`, `/sense-making`, and `/reflect` already perform under their existing specs. The prior finding had committed a category error: treating every "move the human contributed that the loop missed" as identical to "improvement /innovate needs," without checking whether the move was genuinely `/innovate`-shaped (creating novel content) or whether it was actually one of the other disciplines' operations the loop should have produced but didn't.

Naming this correctly matters because the user's downstream redesign work hinges on it. If /innovate's spec is expanded to include territory already covered by /td-critique, the harness gets two disciplines doing the same job — overlapping, inconsistent, and harder to call confidently in future runs. The boundaries between disciplines aren't arbitrary; they're the harness's structural commitments. When boundaries leak, the discipline that absorbs the leak becomes a do-anything mechanism, and the discipline that loses its territory becomes redundant.

This inquiry corrects the prior by (a) committing a discipline-boundary framework with operational tests, (b) re-classifying every one of the 19 pair-rows from the 2026-05-17 dataset against the boundaries, (c) producing a much smaller but honest `/innovate`-specific improvement set, and (d) handing off the remaining gaps to inquiries on the OTHER disciplines that genuinely need them.

### Part 1 — The Discipline-Boundary Framework (T1-T5)

Five operational tests, derived from direct spec quotes, let a future agent adjudicate "which discipline does this move belong to?" without re-litigating per case. T1 and T5 are primary (they do most of the routing work); T2-T4 are corroborating (used when T1+T5 give ambiguous results on borderline moves).

**T1 — Output-Shape Test (PRIMARY).** What kind of artifact does the move produce? *Novel content (a new idea, concept-name, edge-case, novel combination, novel condition) that didn't exist before* → `/innovate`. *Evaluative verdict on a candidate (SURVIVE / REFINE / KILL) with optional constructive direction* → `/td-critique`. *Process-observation about how S/I/C performed; failure-mode labeling of prior outputs* → `/reflect`. *Cognitive anchor / stabilized meaning / ambiguity-collapse / re-anchoring at foundational layer* → `/sense-making`. *Predictive internal model of an opaque artifact* → `/comprehend`. Anchored in /innovate's spec definition (*"creating something that doesn't exist"*), /td-critique's (*"determining which survive, which need refinement, and which die"*), /reflect's (*"second-order observation of a first-order cognitive process"*), and /sense-making's (*"constructing stable meaning by organizing cognitive anchors"*).

**T5 — Novel-vs-Existing Test (PRIMARY).** Is the move creating something new, or operating on something already produced? Creating new → `/innovate`. Operating on existing candidates → `/td-critique`. Operating on existing process traces → `/reflect`. Operating on existing claim/ambiguity to stabilize meaning → `/sense-making`. Operating on existing opaque artifact to build a predictive model → `/comprehend`. Anchored in /innovate's explicit exclusion of optimization (*"optimization improves within a known space; innovation changes the space"*) — /innovate doesn't operate on existing; it creates new.

**T2 — Input-Type Test (CORROBORATING).** What does the move consume? Seed → /innovate. Candidates → /td-critique. Completed run's S/I/C outputs + human interventions → /reflect. Vague/ambiguous input → /sense-making. Opaque artifact → /comprehend.

**T3 — Order-in-Loop Test (CORROBORATING).** When does the move fire? Creation phase → /innovate. Evaluation phase → /td-critique. Second-order post-everything → /reflect. Pre-candidate problem-understanding → /sense-making.

**T4 — Content vs Process Test (CORROBORATING; hard boundary for /reflect).** What does the move target? The content under inquiry → any of /innovate, /td-critique, /sense-making, /comprehend (different content aspects). The PROCESS that produced the content → /reflect exclusively. Anchored in /reflect's explicit spec text: *"Critique evaluates CANDIDATES — ideas, plans. Reflection evaluates the PROCESS — how S, I, C each performed."*

The framework applies forward to any cognitive move the project encounters. When the user introduces a move and the loop didn't natively produce it, T1+T5 say which discipline should have. If the answer is "/innovate" and `/innovate`'s current spec can't produce it, that's a `/innovate` gap. If the answer is another discipline, that's a different discipline's gap — and the right response is to refine that discipline, not to expand /innovate.

### Part 2 — The Two Confirmed /innovate-Specific Improvements

**Improvement #1: Combination's input-source list extended to include cross-output observations.** /innovate's Combination mechanism currently names four input sources for finding "second concepts to connect with": *"what's already nearby, what other mechanisms produced, what shares the same structure, what the user/audience is already thinking about."* Add a fifth source: *"what recurring patterns appear across multiple prior loop outputs"* — drawing input from the loop's accumulated history of discipline outputs from past inquiries. The transform stays unchanged (connect previously unrelated concepts to produce something neither contained alone); the output stays unchanged (novel concept-name or novel connection). The change is single-bullet — no new mechanism, no new operation, no category change.

This catches Pair 7 (the Phantom Canon move). The user looked at multiple discipline outputs across past inquiries, recognized a recurring pattern of "project-specific over-specification at certain stages," and coined "Phantom Canon" as the named concept that captured the pattern. The naming is genuine novel-content creation (T1: novel concept-name; T5: creating new). The cross-output observations are INPUTS to that creation, not the thing being labeled.

**Improvement #2: Absence Recognition's question set extended with an edge-case sub-mode.** /innovate's Absence Recognition currently asks structural-level absence questions: *"What's missing? What should be here but isn't? Who needs something that doesn't exist yet? What would the complete picture look like? What would exist if this were designed from scratch today?"* Add a sub-mode that asks case-level absence questions: *"What specific cases, edge-cases, or particular instances are missing from the current frame? What input shapes, value variants, or boundary conditions are not currently considered?"* The output is named edge-cases not present in the current frame.

This catches Pair 12 (the type-key multi-value edge case). The user observed that the prior's `type:` key was designed for single-value but didn't address the multi-value case; the user surfaced this specific edge-case absence. Edge-case-probing is genuine novel-content creation (T1: novel case that didn't exist in the frame; T5: generating new, not extracting an existing anchor).

Both improvements extend existing /innovate mechanisms rather than adding new top-level mechanisms or new operations. The 2-operation structure (Generation + Framing) is preserved exactly. The 7-mechanism vocabulary is preserved exactly. Only two sub-section refinements within Combination and Absence Recognition are needed in the spec.

### Part 3 — The Cross-Discipline Improvement Portfolio

Four future-inquiry suggestions cover the 11 mis-attributed pair-rows. Each handoff names a specific destination + suggested inquiry title + pair-row mapping + framing.

**Handoff #1 — /td-critique improvements.** Title for downstream: *"Top-N /td-critique improvements: extending REFINE constructive output, KILL seed extraction, and Scrutiny Survival post-ACTIONABLE timing — derived from the 19-pair dataset's /td-critique-shaped moves."* Pair-row mapping: Pairs 2, 5, 10, 15, 17, 19, 20 plus partial 8 and partial 14. Framing: the 19-pair dataset shows the human frequently provides REFINE-with-explicit-direction and KILL-with-new-seed contributions that the loop misses natively. /td-critique already has REFINE and KILL as spec'd operations — but the dataset reveals two spec gaps that the inquiry must address: (a) REFINE is framed as feedback-to-INNOVATION, not feedback-to-loop-runner; the inquiry should extend REFINE to produce procedure-directives (action-shape changes like REPAIR-not-ADD-TEST, mode-shifts like contrarian rethink); (b) Scrutiny Survival fires at generation-time only; the inquiry should extend it to fire post-ACTIONABLE on outputs downstream work will consume.

**Handoff #2 — /sense-making improvements.** Title for downstream: *"Top-N /sense-making improvements: Definitional/Internal-Consistency perspective extension, Foundational-Principles altitude re-anchoring, missing-dimension sub-mode — derived from the 19-pair dataset's /sense-making-shaped moves."* Pair-row mapping: Pairs 1, 3, 4, 8, 9, 11 plus partial 14. Framing: the dataset shows the human frequently re-anchors at deeper altitudes and surfaces missing constraint-anchors that /sense-making's existing perspectives don't catch natively. The inquiry must address two spec gaps: (a) Definitional/Internal-Consistency perspective currently fires during initial sensemaking; it should extend to fire on downstream-discipline outputs (a sensemaking-after-S phase); (b) Foundational-Principles is an anchor type but not an explicit "altitude-pull-back operation" — the inquiry should add an operation that, when current anchors are too operational, re-extracts at Foundational Principles depth. **Handoff #3 — /reflect improvements.** Title for downstream: *"Top-N /reflect improvements: cross-run process observation, pattern-extension across instances, failure-mode-labeling refinements — derived from the 19-pair dataset's /reflect-shaped moves."* Pair-row mapping: Pairs 6, 18, 21. Three of the four handoffs require the destination discipline to ADD new operations to its existing spec, not just refine. /td-critique needs REFINE-for-procedure-directives and Scrutiny-Survival-post-ACTIONABLE. /sense-making needs downstream-output-scope and explicit altitude-pull-back. /reflect needs cross-run scope. Only the runner-handoff is purely additive (new feature, no existing operation to extend). This finding does not produce those operations — it identifies that they're needed.

### Why this answer over the alternatives

Three alternative architectures were considered and rejected.

**Alternative A — preserve the prior finding's Meta-Operations category by stretching /innovate's spec.** Rejected. /innovate's spec explicitly excludes optimization ("optimization improves within a known space; innovation changes the space"). The proposed Meta-Operations all operate on existing outputs (REFINE/KILL on prior candidates; failure-mode labeling on prior runs; confidence-audit on prior confident claims). Operating-on-existing is optimization-shaped, not innovation-shaped. Stretching /innovate to admit them would violate the spec's own NOT-list — a worse failure than the prior's mis-attribution.

**Alternative B — create a new procedural-meta discipline alongside /innovate.** Considered. If procedural-meta cognitive operations are genuinely a unified territory, they might warrant their own discipline. But Exploration's mapping showed the territory distributes naturally across existing disciplines: REFINE-with-direction is /td-critique; cross-run process observation is /reflect; altitude pull-back is /sense-making. No single procedural-meta operation cluster emerges that would justify a new discipline; the operations belong to their respective content-targeting disciplines. Creating a new procedural-meta discipline would overlap with multiple existing disciplines, which is structurally worse than the distribution. The user can still fork a separate inquiry on creating a new discipline if a unifying procedural-meta thread becomes visible later, but the current evidence doesn't support it.

**Alternative C — keep the prior's top-5 but rename "Meta-Operations" so it stops sounding like /td-critique.** Rejected. The user's correction was structural, not cosmetic. The proposed operations don't just sound like /td-critique; they operationally collapse into /td-critique's spec'd outputs. Renaming wouldn't change the structural overlap. The honest answer is to acknowledge the structural overlap and route the operations to the disciplines whose specs actually cover them.

## Reasoning

### Significant rejections

**The prior finding's 3-operation expansion was killed because category-overlap is worse than category-absence.** The expansion would have created a Meta-Operations category in /innovate that duplicates /td-critique's verdicts-with-direction, /reflect's per-step observations, and /sense-making's anchor stabilization. **The prior finding's 5 Meta-Operation mechanism proposals were killed (with one partial-survive) because each one operationally collapses into an existing discipline's spec.** Procedural-Directive Generation IS /td-critique's REFINE-with-direction + KILL-with-seed-extraction operationally; calling it something else doesn't change what it does. **The 11/13 pair-coverage claim was corrected because the prior counted gap-attributions, not gap-discipline-mappings.** 13 pair-rows have gaps; only 2 of those gaps are genuinely /innovate-shaped. The other 11 are gaps in /td-critique (7-9), /sense-making (5-7), /reflect (3), and the runner (1). The prior's 11/13 was technically correct (11 pair-rows were "addressed" by Meta-Operations) but structurally misleading (the addressing routed them to the wrong discipline).

**The 19-pair dataset survives entirely as evidence.** Every pair is still valid; only the discipline-attribution changed. The dataset's value actually INCREASED under the corrected reading: it now reveals harness-wide gaps rather than /innovate-specific gaps.

**The boundary framework (T1-T5) survives adversarial test.** T1 (Output-Shape) and T5 (Novel-vs-Existing) are operationally sharp enough that two readers applying them independently would route a borderline move to the same discipline.
