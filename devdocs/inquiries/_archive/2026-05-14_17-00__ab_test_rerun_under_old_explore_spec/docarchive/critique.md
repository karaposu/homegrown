## Phase 2 — Adversarial Evaluation (Multi-axis Prosecution)

The candidate set is structurally one assembled artifact (the 5-piece Confirmation Report). Probes (a)–(i) are conducted on the artifact in pieces, then assembled.

### Probe (a) — Empirical-claim-calibration genuineness (D1)

**Prosecution-too-conservative angle:** "P2 says substance is inferred-not-empirical, but the OLD-spec run's lighter form is exactly the substance — less protocol bookkeeping IS the substance change. Calibrating that as 'inferred' under-claims."

**Defense:** "Substance in this context means *quality of cognitive operation*, not visible form. Less bookkeeping is a precondition for the cognitive operation receiving more attention, not proof that it does. A reader-study or controlled multi-task evaluation would establish substance; one A/B run on the same investigation doesn't disentangle 'less bookkeeping' from 'better thinking'."

**Prosecution-too-generous angle:** "Even form-confirmed overreaches given residual-context confound. The LLM had iteration #8's diff catalogue, hypotheses, and conclusions in working memory. Form-difference might be entirely an artifact of 'I already solved this in iteration #8 so I'll be brief.' The right calibration would be form-suggested, not form-confirmed."

**Defense:** "The form differences are not summary-style brevity — they are specific structural absences (no Step 0 declarations table; no D0-D4; no 5-annotation-layer table; no 11-failure-mode enumeration; 4-vs-5 NOT-list). These structural absences are observable in the artifact regardless of LLM mental state. A short summary of iteration #8 would have produced summary-form prose, not OLD-spec-structured exploration output."

**Collision:** Defense wins on both sides. The three-level calibration is placed correctly. The structural specificity of the form differences (P2's table) rebuts the over-claim charge; the bookkeeping-vs-thinking distinction rebuts the under-claim charge.

**Verdict on D1: PASS.** Calibration is genuine.

### Probe (b) — Residual-context confound treatment (D4)

**Prosecution:** "P2 acknowledges the confound but then immediately dismisses it: 'the form difference observed is large enough that residual-context effects alone are unlikely to fully account for it.' This is a hand-wave. There is no empirical bound on what residual-context effects can account for. The acknowledgment is a fig leaf, not honest engagement."

**Defense:** "The acknowledgment goes beyond mere mention: it explicitly names the confound, identifies what a cleaner test would require (fresh LLM context), declares that test out of scope, and qualifies the form-confirmed claim accordingly. The 'unlikely to fully account' clause is a judgment, not a measurement, and is correctly framed as a judgment — there's no false measurement claim."

**Collision:** Prosecution lands a real critique (the "unlikely to fully account" sentence is the weakest line in P2 — it's a defensible judgment but it's not load-bearing-strong). Defense holds because the structural acknowledgment + out-of-scope flag is intact regardless of how strongly that one sentence is read.

**Verdict on D4: PASS-with-caveat.** Caveat: the "unlikely to fully account" sentence could be softened to "may not fully account for the observed magnitude" without losing meaning. Not material enough to require REFINE — but flagged for the finding's final wording.

### Probe (c) — CONFIRMS verdict shape accuracy (D2)

**Prosecution (toward ADDITIONAL-EVIDENCE framing):** "CONFIRMS suggests this iteration *adds something* to iteration #8's epistemic state. But iteration #8 was already calibrated as 'contributing factor (with caveats).' This iteration removes some of the caveats — that's REFINE-with-empirical-evidence, not CONFIRMS."

**Defense:** "Iteration #8's claim was: 'contributing factor at form level, observable in static diff.' This iteration's claim is: 'contributing factor at form level, observable in static diff AND in live execution.' That's CONFIRM, not REFINE — iteration #8's claim is unchanged; the evidence supporting it is now broader (static + dynamic instead of just static)."

**Prosecution (toward CORROBORATES):** "CONFIRMS is too strong. The right word is CORROBORATES — supplies independent corroborating evidence without changing the verdict-state."

**Defense:** "Within the project's chain vocabulary (CORRECTS / REFINES / SUPERSEDES / CONFIRMS / SUPPLEMENTARY), CONFIRMS is the correct verdict for 'iteration #8's claim survives empirical test on its own terms.' CORROBORATES is not part of the established vocabulary; introducing it now would create vocabulary drift across the chain. CONFIRMS-at-form-level is precise: the verdict states what is confirmed (form-level claim) and what is not (substance-superiority and mistake-prevention)."

**Collision:** Defense holds. CONFIRMS is structurally correct given the chain vocabulary. The "at form level" qualifier is the critical part — it prevents CONFIRMS from reading as "the whole iteration #8 claim is now empirically proven."

**Verdict on D2: PASS.**

### Probe (f) — Iteration #10+ threshold rigor (D7)

**Prosecution:** "'Structurally NEW questions' is a phrase, not a gate. What qualifies? Any new question dressed up as structurally new can pass the bar. The threshold is rhetorical, not operational."

**Defense:** "P4 provides three concrete examples of what would qualify (failure mode in a new discipline outside the /innovate—/explore axis; longitudinal mistake-rate measurement; new project decision dependent on unresolved ambiguity) AND three concrete examples of what would not (refinements, residual calibrations, follow-up A/B runs on the same chain topic). **Prosecution-resurges:** "The examples are illustrative, not exhaustive. A future iteration could construct a 'this is a new structural question' framing that fits neither bucket and exploit the ambiguity."

**Collision:** Defense holds, marginally. The threshold is operationally meaningful enough to constrain casual iteration but not a hard gate. Given that thresholds in this domain are necessarily judgment-based, this is sufficient.

**Verdict on D7: PASS-with-caveat.** Caveat: a user-facing reminder note ("the iteration #10+ gate is a Schelling point, not a hard gate; the named exclusions are the operational content") would strengthen the threshold's force. Not material enough to REFINE.
