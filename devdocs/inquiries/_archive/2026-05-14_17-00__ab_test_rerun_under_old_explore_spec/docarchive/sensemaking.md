### Ambiguity 2 (Adjudication 2): What A/B test establishes vs doesn't

**Counter-interpretation.** "The form difference proves the substance difference and the mistake-prevention claim too."

**Why counter fails.** Form ≠ substance ≠ mistake-prevention. The A/B test shows form difference. Substance (does the OLD-spec output reason better?) requires reading both exploration.md outputs for substantive quality, which is harder to assess from one A/B run. Mistake-prevention requires multiple inquiries under each spec — out of scope.

**Resolution.** **Established: form-level contributing factor demonstrated. Not established: substance-level superiority OR mistake-prevention effect.** Honor what's established; flag what isn't.

### Ambiguity 3 (Adjudication 3): REPAIR decision implications

**Counter-interpretation.** "The form difference is now visible; user should do full revert E1."

**Why counter partially holds + partially fails.** Full revert E1 IS more tempting now (form difference is visible). E2 selective revert achieves the form-level improvements (removing project-coupling A1+A2+A3) without losing protocol-overhead that may be genuinely useful.

**Resolution.** **A/B test supports iteration #8's E2 selective revert. Does NOT compel E1 full revert. Does NOT justify no-change.** This inquiry's REPAIR recommendation INHERITS iteration #8's E2 (don't duplicate; reference). If user wants to escalate to E1 based on form-difference visibility, that's a separate decision; this finding doesn't recommend escalation.

### Ambiguity 4 (Adjudication 4): 9-MVL+ cost at iteration #9

**Counter-interpretation.** "iteration #9 was unjustified; static spec comparison alone would have established the form difference."
