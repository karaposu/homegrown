## SV1 — Baseline Understanding

From iter-2 exploration: "to explore" is the cognitive operation of purposive open-mode surfacing — a cognizer enters territory whose contents are not pre-known, attends to what stands out as relevant to the purpose, and accumulates a confidence-tagged map of what was encountered. ## Phase 1 — Cognitive Anchor Extraction

### Constraints

- **C1.** Must define a verb-named cognitive operation (consistent with the project's discipline pattern, per `thinking_disciplines/anatomy_of_disciplines.md`).
- **C2.** Must be distinguishable from adjacent verbs: research, browse, survey, scout, sample.
- **C3.** Must be domain-agnostic (spatial, technical, scientific, conceptual, personal exploring).
- **C4.** Must not collapse into neighbor discipline territories (sense-making, comprehend, decompose, innovate, navigation).
- **C5.** Must preserve what iter-1 got right: the NOT-list against 5 neighbors, the upstream-precondition relationship, the 5-section skeleton structure (Identity / Components / Process / Quality / Output).
- **C6.** Must reconcile with the user's "mapping relevant content together with relevance understanding" framing.

### Key Insights

- **K1.** Open-mode vs closed-mode is the load-bearing cognitive distinction. Open-mode = the cognizer accepts they don't know what they'll find. Closed-mode = the cognizer has a defined question.
- **K2.** Relevance is a *surfacing criterion* (biasing attention during scan), not an *annotation* (tag after scan). This dissolves iter-1's F-weak/F-strong dispute.
- **K3.** The unit's user-facing name is "surfaced item." "Existence claim" was iter-1's typed-record framing; same referent, different level.
- **K4.** Mode confusion (drifting from open-mode into closed-mode interrogation) is a load-bearing failure mode iter-1 missed.
- **K5.** In the project's pipeline, `/comprehend` already does closed-mode targeted modeling. That makes `/comprehend` the closest *discipline-level* analogue to research; `/explore` is uniquely the open-mode surfacing discipline.
- **K6.** Purposiveness distinguishes exploring from browsing — exploring has a *why* even when the destination is open.
- **K7.** Map-coverage commitment distinguishes exploring from browsing — browsing has no coverage commitment.
- **K8.** Signal-driven attention distinguishes exploring from surveying — surveys use closed instruments to bound what gets recorded.

### Structural Points

- **SP1.** Two cognitive commitments: open-mode (explore) and closed-mode (research). A discipline formalizes one or the other.
- **SP2.** Exploring has three sub-operations: entering territory (going out), encountering what's there (registering items), and accumulating (building a confidence-tagged map).
- **SP3.** Attention is purpose-biased: relevance is what makes something stand out as worth surfacing.
- **SP4.** Output is a confidence-tagged map. User-facing unit: surfaced item. Data-shape (when schema activated): existence claim record.
- **SP5.** Frontier-state is the discipline's progress indicator.
- **SP6.** Two axes are *orthogonal*: cognitive-commitment mode (open/closed) and territory-type mode (artifact/possibility). The discipline commits to open-mode in BOTH artifact and possibility settings.

### Foundational Principles

- **P1.** A discipline formalizes ONE cognitive operation (one commitment).
- **P2.** The verb the discipline names IS the operation. "To explore" is the verb; the discipline is the formalization of that verb.
- **P3.** Comparison with adjacent verbs sharpens the definition.
- **P4.** Surprise is acceptable in exploring — the operation accepts not knowing what will be found.
- **P5.** Purposiveness is what distinguishes exploring from aimless wandering.

### Meaning-Nodes

- **MN1.** **Surfacing** — the core act. Bringing into view what wasn't yet in view.
- **MN2.** **Open-mode commitment** — accepting that what's found may not be what was sought; success = map changed.
- **MN3.** **Closed-mode commitment** — having a defined question; success = question answered. (Comparator, not what /explore formalizes.)
- **MN4.** **Purpose-aware attention** — the explorer's purpose biases what stands out as relevant during surfacing.
- **MN5.** **Map** — the format surfaced items accumulate in.
- **MN6.** **Frontier** — the boundary between known and unknown within the territory.
- **MN7.** **Confidence** — epistemic state per region of the map (5 levels: confirmed / scanned / inferred / unknown / confirmed-absent).
- **MN8.** **Mode confusion** — drifting from open-mode into closed-mode mid-invocation; named here as a failure mode.

With anchors extracted, "to explore" resolves to: *the cognitive operation of purposive open-mode surfacing — entering territory whose contents are not pre-known, attending to what stands out as relevant to the purpose, and accumulating a confidence-tagged map of what was encountered.* The load-bearing distinction is the cognitive commitment (open vs closed). The unit is the surfaced item. Relevance biases attention during surfacing. The commitment is held throughout an invocation. Open-mode and territory-type are orthogonal axes — open-mode commitment applies to both artifact and possibility territories.

## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity 1: Unit name — "surfaced item" or "existence claim"?

Both refer to the same thing (an item the discipline has brought into the inquiry's view).

**Strongest counter-interpretation (against using "surfaced item"):** "Existence claim" is typed and machine-checkable; "surfaced item" is naturalistic but loses precision.

**Why the counter partially holds:** at the data-shape level, "existence claim" is more rigorous.

**Why the counter doesn't displace "surfaced item":** the verb-named cognitive operation is "to explore" = to surface. The unit's user-facing name should match the verb's action, not jump to data shape. The project's other disciplines follow this pattern: innovation produces "novel ideas" (action: innovate); critique produces "verdicts" (action: judge); sense-making produces "stable meaning" (action: stabilize). The unit name names the action's output. "Existence claim" is iter-1's data-shape framing; useful for the typed-schema deferred addition but not the load-bearing user-facing unit.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Resolution:** Use **"surfaced item"** as the load-bearing user-facing unit. Keep **"existence claim"** as the typed-record representation at the schema level when the deferred SK-STD+ schema addition is activated. Both terms remain in the spec at different levels.

- **Now fixed:** user-facing unit = surfaced item; data-shape representation = existence claim record.
- **No longer allowed:** collapsing the two levels.

### Ambiguity 2: Relevance — surfacing criterion or post-scan annotation?

Iter-1 resolved as F-weak annotation. Iter-2 surfaced that relevance actually biases attention during scan.

**Strongest counter-interpretation (against the iter-2 reframe):** iter-1's decoupling (surface everything broadly, then tag by relevance) is *conservative* — it preserves the option to revisit items if purpose shifts.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** in practice, no one explores by surfacing every item in the universe and then tagging. The cognizer's attention IS biased by purpose — that's what "purposive" means in the meaning-definition. Iter-1's framing was descriptively wrong. Relevance is woven into attention, not bolted on as a tag.

Items can still be surfaced even at low relevance (confirmed-absent regions are low-relevance findings but high-information). The point is that relevance shapes WHAT GETS ATTENDED TO, not what gets tagged after.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Resolution:** Relevance is a **surfacing criterion** — purpose-aware attention bias that shapes what gets surfaced and at what depth. The iter-1 "F-weak anti-drift" rule restructures: drift isn't about annotations leaking into meaning; drift is about MODE — drifting from open-mode surfacing into closed-mode interrogation. This is a different failure altogether.

- **Now fixed:** relevance = attention bias during scan.
- **No longer allowed:** framing relevance as a post-scan tag.
- **Changed:** iter-1's F-strong drift failure mode reframes as a sub-case of mode confusion (Ambiguity 4 below).

### Ambiguity 3: Mode shift within an invocation — allowed or not?

Real cognition shifts modes within a session. Should /explore allow this?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** allow mode shifts. Real explorers shift to research-mode when a question emerges, then back to explore-mode.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** if /explore allows mode shifts, it formalizes TWO cognitive operations (open-mode surfacing AND closed-mode interrogation). The project's discipline pattern is one operation per discipline. Mode shifts would mean /explore is formalizing two disciplines worth of work.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Resolution:** /explore commits to open-mode throughout an invocation. Mid-invocation questions worth pursuing become **frontier-output handoffs** to closed-mode disciplines (in MVL+, that's the downstream pipeline; in /comprehend's case, that's a separate invocation). The cognizer blends modes by running multiple disciplines in sequence; the single discipline holds one mode.

- **Now fixed:** per-invocation open-mode hold.
- **No longer allowed:** mode shifts within /explore invocation.
