**Each formalizes a different cognitive operation that produces a different Transform.** The question for explore is: what Transform does it produce that none of these produces?

Quick contrast claim (testable, not yet proven):

- Sense-making operates on already-surfaced material. It does not surface material; it stabilizes the meaning of material already in front of it.
- Comprehend operates on a named, identified artifact. It does not find the artifact; it models the artifact.
- Decompose operates on a complex whole already named. It does not find the whole; it cuts the whole.
- Innovate operates from a seed. It does not find the seed; it generates novelty from it.
- Navigation operates on a completed state. It does not find new territory; it enumerates moves from a known state.

**Inference:** All five neighbors presuppose that *something has been surfaced into the explorer's view*. The operation that surfaces it is what `/explore` must own. This is the "upstream" position.

### Cycle 2 — Possibility scan (candidate meta-definitions of `/explore`)

Per *Completeness before novelty* rule: standard/obvious candidates listed first, then user's hypothesis, then adjacent-domain transfers, then novel framings. Each candidate is a 1–2 sentence description, not yet a developed definition.

**Standard / obvious:**

- **A. Territory Mapping** — `/explore` maps unknown territory at surface resolution to produce a map of what exists in it, with confidence levels.
- **B. Surface Inventory** — `/explore` enumerates items at the surface of a stated territory; no relations, no depth.
- **C. Discovery / Surfacing the Unknown** — `/explore` brings into view what was not known to be there. Its success is *what gets added to the map that wasn't on it before*.

**Standard but less common:**

- **D. Frontier-Pushing** — `/explore` extends the boundary between known and unknown. Its product is *a new frontier position*, not a map.
- **E. Negative-Space / Confirmed-Absent Mapping** — `/explore` also produces *knowledge of absence* (this region was checked and contains nothing), not only knowledge of presence.

**User's hypothesis:**

- **F. Relational Mapping with Relevance Understanding** — `/explore` produces a map where items are placed *together* (with their relations to each other) and where each item is tagged with relevance to the inquiry's purpose.

**Adjacent-domain transfers:**

- **G. Reconnaissance / Scouting** — military framing: cheap forward observation to inform later commitment. Maps approximate, not full.
- **H. Indexing** — library framing: catalog every item by addressable identity so others can retrieve them. No interpretation, no relations.
- **I. Possibility Enumeration** — generative framing: when no artifacts exist, generate candidates for "what could be here."

**Novel / contrarian:**

- **J. Boundary Discovery** — `/explore` does not always start with a known territory boundary. Sometimes the territory's edges are themselves unknown, and the first job is to find them.
- **K. Question-Generation Mapping** — `/explore` produces *the questions the territory raises*, not the answers. Output is structured ignorance, not structured knowledge.

### Cycle 4 — Probes on detected signals

**Probe on S1 (boundary tension):** *Does Candidate F leak into sense-making or comprehend?*

The risk is real but resolvable. "Mapping together with relevance understanding" can mean two different things:

- **F-strong** — explore extracts relational meaning between items ("X causes Y," "A is a kind of B"). This IS sense-making (or part of comprehend). It crosses the boundary.
- **F-weak** — explore tags items with *low-commitment relational annotations*: items appear near each other in the territory, items address the same sub-region, items emerged from the same scan. No meaning is claimed; only co-location and relevance-to-inquiry are recorded.

**Resolution:** the user's "together" can be honored by F-weak without crossing into neighbor territory. F-strong is rejected. The exploration map can carry adjacency and relevance tags without modeling relations.

**Probe on S2 (the upstream-surfacing operation):** *What exactly does `/explore` own that no neighbor does?*

The operation no neighbor performs is **bringing into the workspace's view the existence of items that were not previously claimed to exist.** Sense-making refines what's already in view. Comprehend models a named artifact. Decompose cuts a known whole. Innovate generates from a seed. Navigation reads a completed state. None of them *introduce new existence claims into the workspace*.

So `/explore`'s irreducible operation is: **producing existence claims about items in a territory, with confidence about each claim, before any other discipline depends on those items being there.**

Mapping-with-relevance (F-weak) is a *form* of this operation, not a different operation. Relevance and adjacency are annotations on existence claims.

**Probe on S3 (how does explore know what NOT to map?):** *Decompose the "not to map" question.*

The question has three distinct sub-questions:

1. **Outer-boundary "not":** *Where does the territory end?*
   - Answered by: the inquiry's stated scope (input).
   - Explore does NOT decide its own outer boundary; the inquiry hands it the territory. If the boundary is unclear, explore raises a flag (Candidate J's boundary-discovery sub-mode).

2. **Inner-depth "not":** *How deep into each region?*
   - Answered by: signal-driven probing + diminishing returns. Explore stays at *existence-claim depth*: items are recorded as present, with some adjacency / relevance annotation; but mechanism, meaning, and internal model are deferred to comprehend/sense-making.
   - The depth boundary is the cross-discipline boundary: explore knows it's NOT supposed to model how an item works internally.

3. **Cross-discipline "not":** *What aspects of items should not be extracted?*
   - **Not** the meaning of items as concepts (that's sense-making's anchor extraction).
   - **Not** the mechanism of how items work (that's comprehend's CV2–CV3).
   - **Not** the partition of items into independent pieces (that's decompose).
   - **Not** the novelty assessment of items (that's innovate's tests).
   - **Not** the choice of which item to act on next (that's navigation / wayfinding).
   - **YES** the existence of items, their location, their adjacency, and their relevance to the inquiry.

**Probe on S4 (the "together" hint):** *Should explore produce a graph or a list?*

The output is neither a flat list nor a meaning-rich graph. It is a **map with structure-by-co-location**: items are organized by which region of the territory they were found in. Items in the same region carry implicit adjacency (they emerged together). Items can be cross-referenced when scans surface them in adjacent regions. No claim about WHY they relate; only that they were found near each other in the territory.

This honors the user's "together" without crossing into sense-making's anchor-relation work.

### Cycle 5 — Jump scan (before convergence)

Per the *Jump Scan refinement* (False Confidence prevention), one deliberate scan in a previously unscanned direction:

**Direction not yet considered:** the temporal dimension. Does `/explore` re-explore the same territory at different times if the territory changes? Or is each invocation a one-shot mapping?

**Finding:** This question reveals that the existing definition implicitly treats territory as static (frontier-stability is a convergence criterion). But real inquiry territory can change between invocations — new artifacts get added, the inquiry's purpose shifts, neighbor disciplines produce new items that join the territory. A skeleton for `/explore` should explicitly state whether re-exploration is idempotent or whether explore can update an existing map. **This is a frontier question for sensemaking to stabilize.**

**Second direction not considered:** the meta-recursion. Can `/explore` be invoked on the *output of explore* itself? E.g., explore the map produced by an earlier explore run. Yes — at higher resolution within a chosen region. This is what "zoom in" does within a single run, but cross-invocation it would be a re-explore-with-finer-resolution. **Adjacent to frontier question 1.**

No other major surprises. Jump scan confirms convergence; the frontier did not lurch in a new direction on this jump.
