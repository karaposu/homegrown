## Iteration 2 — refined focus (added 2026-05-12 after user correction)

The iteration-1 finding answered "what should `/explore` LOOK LIKE as a discipline skeleton" but skipped the prior question: **what does "to explore" MEAN as a cognitive operation**, independent of any skeleton choice. The user rejected the F-weak / F-strong framing of "relevance understanding" as a load-bearing user-confirmation question — that was a downstream symptom, not the load-bearing question itself.

### Refined question

*What does "to explore" mean as a cognitive operation? In particular, how is exploring different from researching, and what concrete example scenarios reveal the distinction such that one is the right cognitive move and the other is not?*

### Why a comparator (research) is load-bearing

Defining a cognitive operation by what it is alone is hard — the operation can fit too many adjacent verbs. Defining it by contrast against a near-neighbor (research) sharpens the definition: where exploring and researching diverge, the divergence reveals the load-bearing core of "explore."

Other relevant adjacency-distinctions the loop may use as additional contrasts: explore vs browsing, vs scouting / reconnaissance, vs surveying, vs sampling, vs sensemaking, vs comprehend. Research is the primary comparator the user named; others are available as confirmation that the definition holds across multiple boundary surfaces.

### Iteration 2 goal

A meaning-definition of "explore" as a cognitive operation, structured the same way innovation has been defined (as a verb-named cognitive operation with its own irreducible mechanism), with explicit scenario examples where exploring is the right move and researching is the wrong move (or vice versa). This definition feeds back into the iteration-1 skeleton — confirming or revising the standard skeleton from this finding's deeper layer.
