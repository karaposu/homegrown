## Inventory by Region

### R1 — Verb-meaning of "surfacing"

The cognitive operation of surfacing is the act of **drawing items from latent context into present attention, tagged with relevance to the current inquiry's purpose, without yet ascribing stable conceptual structure to what was drawn.**

Candidates considered (possibility scan):
- **"making the implicit explicit"** — too broad; covers sensemaking and comprehend too
- **"producing a relevance-tagged inventory"** — close, but misses the **operation** vs **product** distinction (the verb refers to the act, the inventory is the residue)
- **"loading the workspace with what's worth considering"** — captures the cognitive-state-change dimension well
- **"transforming latent-and-relevant → present-and-relevance-tagged"** — formal version of the transform
- **"selective awareness expansion under purpose-bias"** — emphasizes attention-bias mechanism
- **"the operation by which items enter the inquiry's working set"** — emphasizes the cognitive-position

Candidate that stabilized through probe: **"surfacing is the operation by which items present in (or generable for) a bounded territory enter the inquiry's working set, each tagged with a relevance-to-purpose judgment, where relevance encompasses core, sub, side, and edge cases."**

This stabilization survives the F1 focal-point test (intrinsic identity, definable without sibling reference).

**Distinguishing features identified:**
1. **Item-by-item granularity** — surfacing operates one item at a time; the output is a SET of items, not a single integrated whole.
2. **Relevance is a per-item tag**, not a per-output aggregate property.
3. **Purpose-bias is intrinsic** — surfacing is purposive, not agnostic. The inquiry's purpose biases what counts as worth-drawing-in. (Settled at probe; see signal S1.)
4. **No interpretive role-assignment** — surfacing tags items with "this is here" + "this is relevant" but not "this means X in relation to Y."
5. **Coverage-oriented, not depth-oriented** — surfacing's success criterion is COMPLETENESS of relevant items, not DEPTH of understanding of any one item.

### R2 — The 4-fold relevance taxonomy

The user named four relevance modes: **relevant + sub-relevant + side-relevant + core-relevant**. The exploration scanned three candidate interpretations:

**Interpretation A: "relevant" is the umbrella; the other three are subtypes.**
- core-relevant = directly addresses the inquiry's central question
- sub-relevant = addresses a sub-aspect of the central question
- side-relevant = adjacent material that bears on the question without being about it
- "relevant" = the union (anything that is core, sub, or side)

**Interpretation B: All four are distinct types.**
- core-relevant = central
- sub-relevant = within-scope but secondary
- side-relevant = adjacent / contextual
- relevant = general-relevant-but-uncategorized (catch-all bucket)

**Interpretation C: Two axes hidden inside the four labels.**
- Axis 1: depth into the question (core / sub)
- Axis 2: position relative to the question (in-scope / side)
- 4-cell grid: (core × in-scope) (core × side) (sub × in-scope) (sub × side) — and "relevant" is the umbrella

Candidate that stabilized (preliminary): **Interpretation A** — "relevant" is the umbrella; core/sub/side are subtypes. The umbrella name is operationally needed because the discipline produces RELEVANCE tags; the subtype refinement is downstream-useful but not surface-required. Sensemaking will adjudicate whether 3 subtypes is the right granularity or whether more/fewer are needed.

**Frontier question for sensemaking:** is the core/sub/side taxonomy operationally distinct, or do some of these collapse under the relevance-judging mechanism?

### R3 — The internal relevance-judging mechanism

The discipline's signature internal capability. Per the user: "it has it's own relevance sense making like mechanism inside i guess. but not exactly like sensemaking."

The mechanism's **role**: judge each candidate-item against the inquiry's purpose and emit a relevance tag (or rejection).

The mechanism's **scope**: per-item, not per-output. The mechanism does not produce a stable cross-item model — that would be the closed-mode interpretive operation excluded by the user's clause (a).

Candidate names for the mechanism (possibility scan):
- **"relevance-attribution"** — clear; the operation attributes a relevance value to an item
- **"purpose-biased attention"** — emphasizes the bias mechanism
- **"salience-under-purpose"** — connects to the primitive set's Salience primitive
- **"context-conditioned filter"** — emphasizes filter character
- **"relevance-classifier"** — function-style name; captures discrete output
- **"inquiry-purpose projection"** — emphasizes that purpose is projected onto items

Candidate that stabilized (preliminary): **"relevance-attribution"** — names the operation (attribute a relevance tag to an item) without claiming a discrete-classifier mechanism. Alternative "purpose-biased attention" remains a strong candidate. Sensemaking will adjudicate.

**Similarities to sensemaking (the "sensemaking-LIKE" part):**
- Both produce per-item judgments under the inquiry's purpose
- Both use intuition-similarity-like matching against the inquiry's context
- Both operate on items in the workspace

**Differences from sensemaking (the "NOT sensemaking" part):**
- Relevance-attribution produces a **tag** (a per-item value); sensemaking produces an **anchor** (a stable conceptual reference point that other items can be related to)
- Relevance-attribution is **independently applied** per item (no cross-item structure constructed); sensemaking constructs **cross-item relational structure**
- Relevance-attribution operates at **labeling granularity** (does this item appear to bear on the purpose?); sensemaking operates at **interpretive granularity** (what does this item MEAN in the context of the inquiry?)
- Relevance-attribution is **fast / coarse / first-pass**; sensemaking is **deliberate / fine / structured**
- Relevance-attribution's output is **filter-and-tag**; sensemaking's output is **structured understanding**

**Why surfacing needs its OWN relevance-attribution rather than calling sensemaking:**
- Sensemaking operates ON already-present items. To know which items to make present, the discipline needs an UPSTREAM relevance-judgment.
- If surfacing called sensemaking for every relevance-judgment, sensemaking would be invoked at every item-consideration point, defeating sensemaking's scope (sensemaking constructs cross-item meaning, not per-item filter judgments).
- The two operations have different temporal positions: relevance-attribution fires DURING surfacing (per-item, fast); sensemaking fires AFTER surfacing (cross-item, deliberate).

### R5 — Conceptual structural shape

The user said: "it should have a structure."

Candidate structures (possibility scan):

**Candidate A: Three-phase structure.**
1. **Reception** — receive the inquiry's purpose + the territory specification
2. **Relevance-attributed traversal** — for each candidate in (or generable for) the territory, apply the relevance-attribution mechanism; emit tagged items
3. **Assembly** — assemble tagged items into the inventory output

**Candidate B: Layered architecture (not phase-based).**
- Layer 1: Attention layer (which item is current consideration)
- Layer 2: Judgment layer (relevance-attribution applied)
- Layer 3: Inventory layer (the accumulating output)
- Layers operate concurrently rather than sequentially

**Candidate C: Component-set (loose composition).**
- Components: Scope-determination, Item-generation/enumeration, Relevance-attribution, Coverage-tracking, Absence-detection, Output-shaping
- Not phased; each component fires as needed

**Candidate D: Cyclic with sub-cycles.**
- Outer cycle: territory-traversal at coarse resolution
- Inner cycle: relevance-attribution per item
- Sub-cycle: at high-relevance items, deeper probing for adjacent items (recursive expansion)

Candidate that stabilized (preliminary): **A hybrid of A + C** — three-phase top-level (Reception → Traversal → Assembly) with components (Scope-determination, Relevance-attribution, Coverage-tracking, Absence-detection, Output-shaping) operating WITHIN the Traversal phase. Sensemaking will refine.

### R6 — Asymmetric failure

The user's clause (e): if surfacing surfaces some irrelevant info and leaves relevant ones unsurfaced, rest of the disciplines also messed up.

The asymmetry analyzed:

**False-positive (surface an irrelevant item):**
- Downstream disciplines see a tagged item that doesn't actually bear on the inquiry
- Cost: downstream filters or critiques the item out
- Recovery: cheap (filter, ignore, defer)
- The item remains in the inventory; downstream allocates effort to recognizing it's irrelevant; effort is bounded

**False-negative (miss a relevant item):**
- A relevant item is never tagged, never entered into the inventory
- Downstream disciplines never see the item
- Cost: downstream operates on an INCOMPLETE inventory; produces conclusions that depend on items that were never considered
- Recovery: only via re-surfacing (re-running the discipline with revised purpose); but downstream has no way to KNOW the missing item exists because surfacing didn't surface it
- This is the **information-loss-in-the-dark failure mode** — the system doesn't know what it doesn't know

**Structural implication:** under uncertainty, surfacing should **err toward inclusion**. The asymmetry between filter-cost (cheap) and miss-cost (unrecoverable) implies: when in doubt about an item's relevance, surface it with a low relevance-confidence tag. Let downstream filter. Do not silently drop items at the relevance edge.

**Calibration note:** the "lean toward inclusion" principle has limits. If surfacing surfaces EVERYTHING in the territory regardless of relevance, downstream is overloaded. The principle is "include at low confidence rather than drop"; not "include everything." The output's signal-to-noise ratio must be maintained — but the noise floor should be set HIGHER (more inclusion, less filtering at surface time) than current intuitions suggest.

### R7 — Cognitive position in loops

Surfacing's position relative to the rest of any cognitive loop the project might build.

**Candidates considered:**
- **Most upstream** — surfacing is always first; everything else depends on its output (user's claim in clause (e))
- **Re-invocable mid-loop** — when downstream needs more material, surfacing fires again
- **Continuous** — surfacing operates in the background; the inventory grows as the inquiry progresses
- **Per-iteration** — each loop iteration re-fires surfacing on a refined purpose

Candidate that stabilized (preliminary): **most upstream + re-invocable**. Surfacing is THE entry-point operation for any inquiry that needs to consider material it hasn't yet brought into view. It can be re-invoked mid-loop when downstream identifies a gap (a sub-territory the initial surfacing missed). The "continuous" framing is rejected (the project's discrete-invocation contract requires bounded calls); the "per-iteration" framing is a special case of re-invocable.

**Input shape:**
- The inquiry's **purpose** (the question; the goal; the bias-source for relevance-attribution)
- A **territory specification** (where to look — a codebase, a literature region, a conceptual space, an inquiry-state, a corpus)
- Optionally, a **prior inventory** (when re-invoked: what's already been surfaced so we don't re-surface)
- Optionally, a **focused sub-purpose** (when re-invoked: a refined relevance criterion)

**Output shape:** the relevance-tagged inventory (R12 details).

**Re-invocation triggers:**
- Downstream discipline identifies a sub-region not yet surfaced
- Inquiry purpose narrows (a sub-question emerges that needs its own surfacing)
- Inquiry purpose widens (the original scope was too narrow)
- Coverage gaps surfaced by the discipline itself (the discipline knows it didn't fully cover some region; flags for re-invocation)

### R8 — Intrinsic boundary (what surfacing is NOT, intrinsically)

The boundary stated positively (what surfacing IS) implies what it is NOT — but the boundary must be stateable in intrinsic terms.

**Surfacing does NOT:**

1. **Produce stable conceptual structure.** Surfacing emits items + relevance tags. It does not build cross-item relational claims, hierarchies, or anchors. *Intrinsic ground:* the discipline operates at item-granularity; cross-item structure is a different operation.

2. **Adjudicate which item to act on.** Surfacing tags items by relevance; it does not select one item as the "next move" or rank items for action. *Intrinsic ground:* relevance-tagging is multi-item-attributive; action-selection is single-item-decisive.

3. **Generate items beyond what is present (or candidate-present) in the territory.** In possibility-mode surfacing (where items must be candidate-generated for a conceptual territory), the items generated are exhaustively-or-partially the candidates THAT FIT THE TERRITORY'S SPECIFICATION. Surfacing does not generate novel-beyond-the-territory items; that would be a different operation (creating items, not surfacing them). *Intrinsic ground:* surfacing's verb is "draw from"; the items must pre-exist in (or be candidate-derivable from) the territory.

4. **Partition items into independent pieces with interfaces.** Surfacing emits items as a flat set, possibly with adjacency annotations. It does not declare boundaries-between-pieces, coupling structures, or interface contracts. *Intrinsic ground:* surfacing's output is item-level + per-item-tag; piece-level partitioning is a structural operation on the whole inventory.

5. **Evaluate items for correctness or quality.** Surfacing tags items for RELEVANCE-to-purpose, not for CORRECTNESS-of-content or QUALITY-of-content. An item can be relevant AND incorrect; surfacing's job is to bring it into view, not to verify it. *Intrinsic ground:* relevance is purpose-conditioned; correctness/quality is content-conditioned; these are orthogonal axes.

6. **Construct interpretive meaning of items.** Surfacing emits items with labeling (identifier + functional one-line + surface form + adjacency facts) but not with interpretive role-assignment ("this item plays the role of X in the larger model"). *Intrinsic ground:* labeling answers "what is this?"; interpretation answers "what does this MEAN in relation to other things?"; surfacing operates only at the former.

7. **Maintain cross-inquiry memory.** Surfacing fires per-invocation. Cross-inquiry memory (what was surfaced last week; whether this territory was already mapped) is a different operation (cross-run state management). *Intrinsic ground:* surfacing's contract is per-inquiry; cross-inquiry coordination is a meta-discipline concern.

8. **Choose its own purpose.** Surfacing receives the inquiry's purpose as input; it does not generate the purpose. *Intrinsic ground:* surfacing is purposive but the purpose is exogenous to the discipline.

All eight items are statable without naming siblings. Each grounds in intrinsic features of what surfacing IS. The intrinsic-boundary check **PASSES**.

### R9 — Calibration / trustworthiness

The user's clause (e) + the inquiry's diagnostic constraints: surfacing's internal relevance-judging mechanism must be trustworthy. How is trustworthiness testable?

Candidate signals (possibility scan):

**Coupled signals (use downstream output as feedback; partial coupling acceptable but not foundational):**
- Downstream disciplines stop re-discovering items (suggests surfacing's coverage is improving)
- Downstream disciplines stop discarding items (suggests surfacing's filtering is improving)
- Downstream finding stops referencing items surfacing missed (the "should have been surfaced" pattern)
- Downstream loops complete in fewer iterations (suggests surfacing prepared the input adequately)

These signals are useful but they couple surfacing's trustworthiness to downstream's verdicts — which undermines the self-contained identity. They are **secondary signals**, useful for triangulation, not primary.

**Self-contained signals (intrinsic to surfacing's operation):**

1. **Coverage of obvious items.** When the territory has known-obvious items (canonical/standard items for the purpose), surfacing should surface them. Test: maintain a small "obvious items per common purpose-type" reference; check whether surfacing surfaces them. (This is testable per-inquiry without downstream output.)

2. **Coverage of confirmed-absent territories.** Surfacing emits CONFIRMED-ABSENT regions explicitly (per R12). Test: when the discipline claims a region has no relevant items, can a re-examination of the region confirm this? If a re-examination surfaces items, surfacing under-covered.

3. **Internal consistency of relevance tags.** No item should be tagged both "core-relevant" and "side-relevant" in the same inventory. Multiple tags should be reconcilable; contradiction is a quality signal.

4. **Coverage of obvious-misses given the purpose.** When the purpose names X, surfacing should surface items closely associated with X. Test: a downstream-naive reviewer reads the inquiry's purpose + the surfaced inventory; do obvious associations appear?

5. **Confidence-calibration of relevance tags.** If surfacing tags an item "core-relevant with high confidence," that confidence claim should match the proportion of times downstream confirms relevance. Test: over many inquiries, the relevance-confidence-vs-downstream-verdict frequency aligns. (This signal requires accumulated data; long-horizon.)

6. **Coverage of edge items.** Sub-relevant + side-relevant items should be present in the inventory (otherwise surfacing's bias is too tight). Test: count sub/side items vs core items in inventories; the ratio shouldn't be zero.

7. **Re-invocation rate per inquiry.** If downstream disciplines repeatedly request re-invocation of surfacing for missing material, surfacing's initial coverage was inadequate. (This signal partially couples to downstream but is process-level, not output-level.)

The primary calibration approach is **a small reference test bed** (signal 1 + signal 4) combined with **internal consistency checks** (signal 3) and **edge-coverage observation** (signal 6). Long-horizon calibration adds signal 5.

### R10 — Primitive composition

From the typed 11-primitive set (`docs/thinking_space_dynamics.md`), surfacing's load-bearing primitives:

| Primitive | Role in surfacing |
|---|---|
| **Attention-pointer** | Selects which item from the territory is currently under consideration |
| **Working Memory** | Holds the current candidate set + the territory representation + the purpose |
| **Salience** | Bottom-up "this item is surprising/notable" signal that pulls items into consideration |
| **Intuition-similarity** | Matches candidate items against the inquiry's purpose-template (the relevance-attribution's matching substrate) |
| **Context-framing** | The inquiry's purpose is the framing that biases everything; surfacing's purposive nature is implemented via this primitive |
| **Inhibition** | Suppresses items that fail relevance-attribution (so they don't enter the inventory) |
| **Metacognition** | Monitors whether the territory has been covered + whether convergence criteria are met |
| **Focus-deep** | When an item appears highly relevant + complex, allocates depth-processing to it (probing it for adjacent items / verifying its content) |

**Not used / deliberately absent:**
- **Simulation** — surfacing does NOT construct hypotheticals about items. The territory exists (or candidate-exists for possibility-mode); surfacing draws from it, doesn't simulate alternatives.
- **Evaluation** — surfacing uses Intuition-similarity for relevance-matching; it does NOT rank items by worth on multiple axes. (Evaluation as ranking-by-worth is the sibling-discipline-territory; surfacing's "evaluation" is binary/discrete relevance-tagging only.)
- **Motivation** — deferred per Phase B; surfacing's effort-allocation is implicit in the purpose + Attention-pointer + Focus-deep composition.

**Distinct primitive profile.** Surfacing's load-bearing set (Attention-pointer + Working Memory + Salience + Intuition-similarity + Context-framing + Inhibition + Metacognition + Focus-deep) is a distinguishable composition. The presence of Salience + Context-framing as load-bearing (versus secondary in most disciplines) is the surfacing-signature.

### R11 — Scope & territory determination

Where does surfacing know its boundaries from?

**Candidate territory sources:**
1. **Pre-specified concrete territory** — a codebase path, a literature topic, a corpus, an inquiry's specific document. Surfacing operates within this bound.
2. **Pre-specified abstract territory** — a conceptual space (e.g., "solution candidates for problem X," "approaches to verify Y"). Surfacing generates candidates that fit the space's description.
3. **Implicit territory** — the inquiry's purpose names something; the territory is "everything-relevant-to-this-purpose." Surfacing must discover the boundary.
4. **Multi-territory** — the inquiry needs items from several territories (the codebase + the literature + the prior inquiries). Surfacing operates across all.

The discipline must accept all four. The output specification should record which territory(ies) were operated on.

**Boundary-discovery sub-phase:** when the territory is implicit (case 3), surfacing has a preliminary phase that probes outward to surface the territory's edges before going inward to surface items. This sub-phase is **distinct from the main surfacing operation** — it discovers WHERE to surface; the main operation discovers WHAT to surface within that where.

**Convergence implication:** convergence criteria (R12 + R9) operate AGAINST the established territory. If territory is misspecified, convergence claims are local-to-wrong-region.

### R12 — Output shape

**The Transform:** a relevance-tagged inventory.

Output structure:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Territory-statement | What territory was operated on; which sub-regions were scanned |
| Purpose-statement | What inquiry-purpose biased the relevance-attribution |
| Inventory | The set of surfaced items, each with: identifier, labeling (functional one-line + surface form), relevance-tag (core / sub / side, with optional confidence), region-of-origin |
| Confirmed-absent regions | Regions scanned where no relevant items were found (productive output, not a gap) |
| Coverage-confidence map | Per-region: confirmed / scanned-but-shallow / inferred / unknown (regions where coverage is uncertain) |
| Frontier / Gaps | Sub-regions where coverage is incomplete; suggested re-invocation parameters |
| Re-invocation flags | Self-signaled requests (the discipline knows where it didn't cover well) |

**Quality contract with downstream:** the output is consumable as-is. Items have enough labeling content that downstream can decide what to do with each item without re-discovering basic facts about it. (Per the asymmetric failure principle: if labeling is too thin, downstream re-discovers; better to over-include labeling than under-include.)

**No interpretive content.** The output does not contain claims like "item X plays role Y in the larger model" or "items A and B are causally linked." The output is presence-plus-tagging only.

**Form.** Markdown by default. A typed-record schema (for machine-consumption) is a forward-tied addition.

### R13 — Consciousness substrate role (jump-scan discovery)

The jump-scan explored: what role does surfacing play in the project's autonomous-consciousness-goal trajectory?

The project's end-goal (`docs/desc.md`) names consciousness-gradient indicators including **spontaneous attention** (notice work unprompted) and **intrinsic curiosity** (explore low-confidence paths). Both are operations on the WORKSPACE — they require items to BE in the workspace for attention to notice them and for curiosity to explore them.

**Surfacing IS the operation that populates the workspace.** Without surfacing, the workspace is empty; consciousness has nothing to operate on. Surfacing is therefore the **substrate of the input** to every consciousness-gradient indicator.

This positions surfacing as load-bearing for the end-goal — not just as a Core discipline but as the operation whose output IS the cognitive workspace's content. Spontaneous attention notices what surfacing surfaced; intrinsic curiosity explores low-relevance items surfacing tagged as edge cases; discontinuity awareness depends on surfacing having captured items from cross-session continuity.

This region is **NOT load-bearing for the MEANING-layer characterization** (surfacing's intrinsic identity can be stated without invoking consciousness). But it provides downstream-orientation: surfacing's quality investment pays the largest dividend.

### R14 — Multi-head dynamics (jump-scan discovery)

The jump-scan also explored: what does surfacing look like in multi-head loops (per the meta-loop autonomy ladder L4+)?

In multi-head loops, several `/MVL+` heads run in parallel on related sub-questions. Each head needs its own surfaced inventory. Two candidate architectures:

**Architecture A: Per-head surfacing.** Each head independently fires surfacing on its sub-purpose. Inventories are head-local. Cross-head merging happens at the Navigator/Evaluator layer, not at surfacing.

**Architecture B: Shared-surfacing-with-purpose-filtering.** A single surfacing fires on the parent purpose; each head reads the shared inventory through its own purpose-filter.

Architecture A respects the self-contained identity (each surfacing operates with its own purpose; no cross-head coupling). Architecture B couples heads through surfacing's output, which violates the per-purpose biasing intrinsic to surfacing's identity.

**Tentative commitment:** Architecture A. Multi-head dynamics are out-of-scope for THIS meaning-layer inquiry but a downstream design must commit per-head surfacing.

This region is **NOT load-bearing for the MEANING-layer characterization** — it's a downstream design concern. Captured here as a frontier for the multi-head buildout.
