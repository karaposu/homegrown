## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity A1: The 4-fold relevance taxonomy (FQ1)

**Ambiguity:** Is the user's "relevant + sub-relevant + side-relevant + core-relevant" a 4-type set (Interpretation B) or a 1-umbrella-plus-3-subtypes set (Interpretation A) or a 2-axis-grid (Interpretation C)?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Interpretation C (2-axis grid: depth × position) might be the right one. The user's enumeration appears 1-dimensional but could be 2-dimensional with implicit axes. The counter is: if relevance has 2 orthogonal axes (depth-into-question × position-relative-to-question), then the 4 terms map to grid cells, not to flat types.

**Why the counter-interpretation fails (structural grounds):** the 2-axis interpretation requires committing what "core" means (the depth axis) and what "side" means (the position axis) as orthogonal. But "core" and "sub" are clearly on the same axis (depth into the question); "side" is on a different axis (adjacency). And "relevant" doesn't fit either axis — it's the umbrella. The 4 terms therefore don't form a 2-axis grid; they form an umbrella + 3 subtypes where the 3 subtypes vary on different facets of how-they-relate-to-the-question (depth-into vs adjacent-to).

**Confidence:** HIGH (structural decomposition shows core/sub/side are not orthogonal axes; they're 3 facets of relating-to-the-question, with "relevant" as the umbrella).

**Resolution:** **Interpretation A: "relevant" is the umbrella; core-relevant + sub-relevant + side-relevant are 3 distinct subtypes.**
- Core-relevant: directly addresses the inquiry's central question
- Sub-relevant: addresses a sub-aspect of the central question
- Side-relevant: adjacent matter that bears on the question without being about it
- "Relevant" without qualification is the umbrella tag (an item is at least one of the three subtypes)

**What is now fixed?** The discipline's relevance-tagging vocabulary commits 4 levels: core / sub / side / (umbrella). The umbrella is the implicit OR of the three subtypes; the three subtypes are the granular tags.

**What is no longer allowed?** The discipline does NOT commit a 2-axis grid. It does NOT commit 4 flat parallel types. Items are tagged at one of the 3 granular levels OR at the umbrella level (when uncertainty about granularity exists).

**What now depends on this choice?** The output schema (R12) commits to 3 granular relevance-tag values + the umbrella value. The relevance-judging mechanism (R3) must operate at this granularity. Downstream consumers can filter by granular tag or use the umbrella.

**What changed in the conceptual model?** The relevance vocabulary is now structurally committed. The model gains specificity at the output-schema level.

### Ambiguity A2: The internal mechanism name and scope (FQ2)

**Ambiguity:** The mechanism is the discipline's signature internal capability. What is it called? Is it a layer / phase / continuous-operation / discrete-classifier?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** the mechanism should NOT have a separate name; it is just the discipline's per-item operation. Naming it separately reifies a sub-component that is actually just "what the discipline does at each item." The counter argues that the discipline's verb (surface-this-item-with-its-relevance-tag) IS the mechanism; naming the mechanism separately invents a non-existent sub-component.

**Why the counter-interpretation fails (structural grounds):** the user's clause (c) explicitly names the mechanism as a separate entity: "it has it's own relevance sense making like mechanism INSIDE i guess. but not exactly like sensemaking." The "inside" framing implies the mechanism is a sub-component of the discipline, not the discipline itself. Furthermore, the mechanism's SIMILARITIES to sensemaking (per-item judgment under context-framing + intuition-similarity matching) make it identifiable as a recognizable named operation, distinct from the discipline's whole. Treating it as un-named risks collapsing it into either the discipline's whole OR into sensemaking-proper, both of which are mis-attributions.

**Confidence:** HIGH (the user's framing + the structural identifiability argue for naming).

**Resolution:** **Name the mechanism "relevance-attribution"** (preliminary preference from Exploration confirmed; alternative "purpose-biased attention" rejected as too broad — it would also describe Salience-in-general). **Classify the mechanism as a sub-component (specifically: an OPERATION applied per-item during the Relevance-attributed Traversal phase).** It is NOT a layer (not a continuous background process); NOT a phase (not a temporal segment of the discipline's overall structure); NOT a discrete-classifier (not an isolated atomic predicate). It is a recognizable named operation — a "per-item relevance-attribution operation" — invoked iteratively during the Traversal phase.

**What is now fixed?** "Relevance-attribution" is the mechanism's name. Its structural classification is "per-item operation invoked during Traversal." Its primitive composition: Intuition-similarity + Context-framing + Inhibition (for clearly-irrelevant items) + Metacognition (for uncertain items, defaults to inclusion per asymmetric-failure-principle).

**What is no longer allowed?** Calling the mechanism "the discipline" (it is a sub-component). Calling it "an attention layer" (it is operation-typed, not layer-typed). Conflating it with sensemaking-proper (it operates at different granularity, per LBT2).

**What now depends on this choice?** The discipline's structural shape (R5) names "Relevance-attribution" as a component within Traversal. The MEANING-layer characterization commits this name. The downstream STRUCTURAL inquiry will detail the mechanism's operational steps.

**What changed in the conceptual model?** The mechanism is now a named, structurally-classified sub-component. Its boundary vs the discipline's whole is committed.

### Ambiguity A8: Asymmetric failure principle's operational form (FQ8)
