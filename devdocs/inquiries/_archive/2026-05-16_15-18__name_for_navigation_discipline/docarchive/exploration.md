# Exploration — Name for Navigation Discipline

## Territory Overview

**Territory.** The space of candidate names for the discipline currently called `/navigation`. The territory is conceptual (possibility mode): candidates must be generated and placed on a map by their fit to the discipline's essence.

**Mode.** Possibility.

**Entry-point.** Signal-first. The discipline's spec at `cognitive_harness/navigation/SKILL.md` and the conceptual framing at `docs/nav.md` already exist as anchoring signals — what the discipline IS gives strong direction to candidate generation.

**Resolution.** D3 (functional one-line per candidate + structural-adjacency facts: which dimension the candidate emphasizes, what it loses).

**Boundary** (from sources): the discipline operates on the output of a completed SIC inquiry plus the project's current state; produces a navigation map whose unit is a *route-card record* with fields (Direction, Goal, Type, Priority, Status, Blocked by, Purpose, Movement, Unlocks, Why-this-route-exists, Guidance mode, adaptive Guidance, Continuation note); routes are typed across a 16-type taxonomy (content-directed / process-directed / context-directed); the discipline ABSORBED the previously-separate `/wayfinding` discipline, inheriting DIAGNOSE as 16th type, reachability/gates check, and REVISIT sub-actions (RESURRECT / INVALIDATE / REVERT). `docs/nav.md` states "everything in navigation is concept" and that the discipline does two things — concept-mapping + concept-map-status-generation — and makes directions explicit.

**Surround-layer.** The 9-axis autonomy ladder uses *Navigator* (agent noun) as one of five execution roles. The discipline's slash command is `/navigation` (action noun). The terminology drift between Navigator (agent) and navigation (action) is itself a signal — pick one form consistently.

**Completeness-before-novelty.** Standard / obvious candidates first (current name + dictionary-direct alternatives); novel candidates after.

## Inventory

The candidate space partitions into 8 regions by what the name emphasizes.

### R1 — Status-quo

- **`/navigation`** (current). The user invokes via this name today. Recognizable in everyday English. Aligns with the autonomy-ladder *Navigator* role-noun. Implies steering toward a destination — which is what makes the user ask whether it's the right name, since the discipline ENUMERATES without selecting.

### R2 — Pure-enumeration

- **`/enumerate`** — operation-only; describes the verb the discipline performs. Loses the object (directions / routes). Recognizable but generic.
- **`/options`** — emphasizes the OUTPUT (the set of options). Plain. Loses the "next" temporal framing.
- **`/directions`** — uses the project's own term (route-cards have a "Direction" field). Plain. Loses the temporal "next" framing.

### R3 — Map / output-focused

- **`/direction-map`** — emphasizes the structured MAP output. Project-vocabulary-aligned (the discipline produces a "navigation map" today). Two-word; slightly less terse.
- **`/route-map`** — uses "route" (the discipline's unit: route-card). Project-vocabulary-aligned. Two-word.
- **`/move-map`** — uses "move" (a more generic alternative to "direction"). Two-word.
- **`/possibility-map`** — broader framing; emphasizes the SPACE of options rather than specific routes.

### R4 — Next-framing

- **`/next-moves`** — uses "next" (aligns with the user's framing in `cognitive_harness/next_question_to_ask.md`). Plain English. Two-word.
- **`/next-directions`** — uses both "next" and "directions" (project-vocab + user-vocab). Three-word; slightly bulky.
- **`/what-next`** — captures the user-facing question shape ("what should I do next?"). Very plain. Hyphenated.
- **`/next`** — terse to a fault; loses operational content.

### R5 — Action / move-focused

- **`/moves`** — single-word; loses "next" and "directions."
- **`/move-cards`** — emphasizes the unit (route-card record). Internal-implementation-flavored.
- **`/move-enumeration`** — full verb form; bulky.

### R6 — Role-aligned

- **`/navigator`** — uses the agent noun from the autonomy ladder; would align discipline name with role name. Same family as `/navigation`; semantic distinction is minimal (agent vs. action).

### R7 — Wayfinding-revival

- **`/wayfind`** or **`/wayfinding`** — was the previously-separate discipline that got ABSORBED into `/navigation`. Reviving the name would obscure the absorption: people seeing `/wayfinding` would expect the old single-direction-selection behavior, not the current enumeration-plus-diagnose-plus-revisit behavior. Reviving is regressive.

### R8 — Survey / horizon framing

- **`/survey`** — emphasizes broad scan; loses the specific "directions / routes" object.
- **`/horizons`** — poetic; implies future possibility space; loses operational specificity.
- **`/outlook`** — similar to `/horizons`; non-operational.
- **`/compass`** — implies orientation rather than enumeration; mismatch.

## Gaps and Recommendations

Frontier questions handed to downstream disciplines:

### To Sensemaking (S)

- What does "good name" mean? Multiple plausible interpretations: (a) most recognizable to a cold reader; (b) most precise to the discipline's essence; (c) most consistent with project vocabulary; (d) most aligned with user-vocabulary; (e) most distinctive from other disciplines.
- The discipline does FOUR operations (enumerate + gates + diagnose + revisit). Is the name supposed to cover all four, or just the dominant one (enumeration)? Different answers favor different candidates.
- Should the discipline's name align with the autonomy-ladder *Navigator* role-noun (favors `/navigator`) or with action-noun convention used by all other disciplines (`/sense-making`, `/innovate`, `/explore` — action nouns; favors keeping `/navigation` or a similar action noun like `/enumerate-directions`)?

### To Decomposition (D)

- The naming question decomposes into: (i) what is the discipline's verb-meaning? (ii) what is the discipline's object? (iii) what is the discipline's output shape? (iv) what is the discipline's role on the autonomy ladder? Different combinations of (i)-(iv) yield different names.

### To Innovation (I)

- Generate hybrid name candidates that combine dimensions (e.g., `/next-direction-map` combines next-framing + directions object + map output).
- Test mechanism-independence: do multiple mechanisms converge on the same name?

### To Critique (C)

- Adversarially test: which candidate survives the prosecution "is this name actually clearer than the current one"? Many candidates are slight variants; some don't meaningfully improve.
- Kill candidates that reuse already-claimed vocabulary (e.g., `/candidates` clashes with innovation/critique usage).

### Frontier observations

- The status-quo verdict (`/navigation`) has its own defenders: it's recognizable, it aligns with the Navigator role, it's already in use across cross-references. Sensemaking should test whether the rename's switching cost exceeds the name-improvement value.
- The user's note-to-self uses "next" repeatedly — strong relevance signal favoring R4 candidates.

## Self-Assessment

**PROCEED.** All checks pass. The candidate space is mapped at sufficient depth for Sensemaking to extract anchors. The single most load-bearing finding for downstream: **the question's apparent simplicity masks a multi-dimensional decision** — naming a discipline depends on which dimension (recognizability, precision, project-vocabulary alignment, user-language fit, role-distinctiveness) is weighted highest. Sensemaking's job is to surface those dimensions and weight them; Innovation's job is to generate hybrid combinations; Critique's job is to adversarially test which combination dominates.
