## Question

Is `/navigation` structurally the same discipline as `/explore`, differing ONLY in how its mapping is configured (paradigm + viewpoint + purpose + per-paradigm labeling extension) — OR are there genuine residuals (operations, modes, commitments) that `/navigation` requires which `/explore` cannot accommodate even with configuration, making `/navigation` a separate discipline rather than a configuration of `/explore`?

## Goal

A grounded verdict on the unification hypothesis, with explicit articulation of:

(a) **The hypothesis being tested:** `/navigation` = `/explore` with paradigm=Navigational + viewpoint=egocentric + purpose=routing + Navigational-paradigm-specific labeling extension. The hypothesis collapses `/navigation` into `/explore` as a configuration, not as a separate discipline.

(b) **The residual test:** for each operation, mode, or commitment that `/navigation` was specified to add (per the original `/navigation` finding's specialization treatment), determine whether it (i) is captured by paradigm/viewpoint/purpose configuration; OR (ii) is captured by per-paradigm labeling extension; OR (iii) is a genuine residual that `/explore`'s identity cannot accommodate. (c) **The verdict:** IF all residuals reduce to configuration → unification confirmed; `/navigation` collapses to configured `/explore`; replace `/navigation` discipline spec with paradigm-config additions to `/explore`'s Step 0 declarations + per-paradigm labeling extensions. IF any residual is genuine → unification rejected (or partial); `/navigation` remains a separate discipline OR the unification is partial (some operations configurable; others not).

(d) **The verdict's implications:** if unification confirmed, what are the concrete spec changes (to `/explore`, to `/navigation`, to `/MVL+`/runners)? if rejected or partial, what are the residuals and how should they be specified?
