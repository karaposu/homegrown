## (1) THE COMPOSING TWO-PART FIX, CONCRETELY [P4]

### HALF 1 — territory-bounding: get canon into the first surfacing (the 19-14 §6 fix, re-framed)

This half is **already designed** — it is the 19-14 seed_harvester §6 edit: repair "Surfacing brings the source's claims AND *the project anchors* into view" → *"…AND **the canon model the source bears on, surfaced fresh; the machinery is one column, never the whole axis**,"* plus the §9 #9 "machinery-only anchor axis" failure mode. **This dive does not redesign it — it re-frames its ROLE.** Its job in the ordering-problem picture is to ensure the **first (cold) surfacing fetches canon**, so that anything downstream has canon in context to work with.

**Honest local note:** for **harvests specifically**, this §6 edit does *both* jobs at once — it bounds surfacing's territory to fetch canon **and** re-specifies the anchor-axis as the canon model. That is why **harvests can be fixed by Half 1 alone** — the harvest protocol *knows* its anchor should be canon, so it hard-codes the re-anchoring at the surfacing-bounding step. No second step is needed for the harvest case. **Half 1 ships now and closes the spider problem.**

*Generators: (inherited from 19-14) Absence-recognition + Domain-transfer. Re-framed here by Lens-shift — same edit, new role (the canon-into-context half of a two-part structure).*

### HALF 2 — warm re-framing: articulate2 (the general half)

**What it solves that Half 1 doesn't:** Half 1 works for harvests because the harvest protocol hard-codes "anchor on canon." For a **general** traverse (not a harvest), nothing hard-codes the right anchor — the cold articulate_simple frames it from session context, and there is no warm step to correct it. Half 2 is that warm step.

**Concrete sketch:**
- **Position in the loop:** `articulate_simple → surfacing → **articulate2** → sensemaking → …`
- **What it IS (per the inversion below):** NOT a new discipline — a **second invocation of the existing articulate_simple discipline, with warm substrate** (the surfaced material, now including canon). The project doc says exactly this: *"articulate runs again as articulate2 after /surfacing returns project material"* (how_articulate_simple_should_be.md:50).
- **What it RE-RUNS:** the **anchor-identifying meta-question MQ2 (context-need)** at minimum — re-derive "what are the project anchors this bears on?" now that canon is actually in context. ★This is the dive's key design-input: the *documented* articulate2 mode "re-runs **only Rephrase** with new substrate" (how_articulate_simple_process_should_be.md:331) — but the anchor-framing lives in **MQ2**, not Rephrase, so a Rephrase-only articulate2 **would not fix the anchor**. Articulate2 must re-run the anchor-identifying MQs.
- **What it CONSUMES:** the surfaced canon (from surfacing-1).
- **What it PRODUCES:** a re-framed anchor / updated `_branch` framing that sensemaking-onward operate over — the machinery anchor replaced by the canon-model anchor, *derived* rather than hard-coded.
- **Minimal vs fuller form:** the **minimal** form is `a_s → surfacing → articulate2 → sensemaking` (no second surfacing — canon is already fetched; articulate2 just re-frames). The **fuller** documented form is **staged-surfacing**: `a_s → surfacing-1 → articulate2 → surfacing-2 → sensemaking` (re-surface once the frame is corrected). The minimal form suffices for the spider case; staged-surfacing is the documented general option when the corrected frame changes *what territory is relevant*.

*Generators: Absence-recognition (the missing warm-frame step) + Domain-transfer (the documented two-pass design → the ordering-problem fix).*

### The deadlock the halves break, and how they compose
The warm re-frame (Half 2) needs canon in context; canon enters via surfacing (Half 1's territory-bounding); surfacing's territory is set by the cold frame. **Half 1 breaks the deadlock by making the first surfacing fetch canon regardless of the cold frame; Half 2 then uses it.** They **compose, not compete.** Neither alone is the general fix: Half 1 alone re-anchors only where a protocol hard-codes it (harvests); Half 2 alone has no canon to re-anchor on.

## (2) ★ THE ASSEMBLY EMERGENT — the front of the loop is a two-phase bootstrap

Composing the halves reveals a reframe of the loop's **front**. The cold first pass — articulate_simple + surfacing-1 — is really a **BOOTSTRAP** whose only structural job is to **aim the first surfacing broadly enough** (including canon). The **real framing** is the warm articulate2. So:

> **articulate_simple has been doing double duty** — "cold bootstrap-aim" (point surfacing at roughly the right territory) AND "the real frame" (identify the project anchors) — when, being spec-barred from canon, it can structurally only do the **former**. The ordering problem is precisely **what you get when one cold step is asked to do both jobs.**

The healthy architecture separates them: **cold-aim → fetch → warm-frame.** articulate_simple = cold-aim (bootstrap); surfacing = fetch; articulate2 = warm-frame (the real one). This is why the project's own docs call articulate_simple the *"pre-context phase"* and articulate2 the *"post-context phase"* — the two-phase bootstrap is the latent architecture the docs already named; the spider case is its first concrete failure instance.

**★ Anti-inflation flag (explicit):** this is an elegant reframe, and elegance is a contamination risk. It rests on the **same** evidence as the diagnosis (no new claim), and it is a **design-hypothesis** pending the articulate2 build — not a proven architecture. It does **not** change what ships now (Half 1). Held at its true size: a clarifying lens on *why* the ordering problem exists and *what* articulate2 is for, not an independent result.

So articulate2 subsumes **half** of Half 1 (the re-anchoring), not all of it (the territory-bounding remains — the cold-first-surfacing bootstrapping problem).

## (4) THE OPEN SURFACING-NUANCE [P6]

The one place HA (surfacing-should-have) survives: should surfacing carry a **purpose-adequacy flag** — a check that asks *"does this given territory even contain what the purpose needs? if it looks mis-bounded for the stated purpose, flag it"* — and raise it rather than silently surfacing a territory that can't serve the purpose? In the spider case, such a flag might have caught "the anchor-space is all machinery, but the purpose is *seed-generation about traversal* — is machinery really the right anchor territory?" **Sketched as a candidate surfacing-spec addition; flagged OPEN.** It is a real design sub-question (it interacts with surfacing's draw-from-a-given-territory identity — does adding adequacy-checking violate that identity, or complete it?), not something this dive settles. It is the honest residue of HA.
