## Phase 2 — Adversarial Evaluation + Phase 3 Verdicts

### C1 — The verdict (over-built steelman)
**Prosecution:** a bare gut-call ("feels low/mid/high") yields the same first-pass labels with zero procedure; the rubric is ceremony, and heavier than the first-pass field 16-10 specified.
**Defense:** the gut-call *is* the fuzziness the user explicitly asked to remove; the rubric's value isn't out-computing the gut but *naming* the two things the gut implicitly weighs (impact, ambiguity) so two raters converge and the meta-loop can read the rating. 3 glance-questions ≠ heavier than a first-pass perception.
**Collision (purpose-fitness):** "if [procedure-instead-of-pure-gut] were left in, would the candidate still de-fuzz?" — a gut-call does NOT de-fuzz → the procedure earns its place. **SURVIVE** — caveat: contingent on D3 (booleans truly glance) and D1 (convergence, unobserved).

### C2 — The two-axis claim (likelihood smuggled-in / decorative)
**Prosecution:** likelihood-of-misreading is the rater's *confidence/difficulty*, not how *vital* closing the gap is — it conflates "hard to get right" with "important to get right." And under impact-gates (I=N→low regardless of L), likelihood is inert for half the space → not a co-equal "axis."
**Defense:** vitality = risk of building *wrong*, not importance-in-abstract. Risk *needs* both severity-if-wrong (impact) and probability-of-wrong (likelihood): a high-impact gap you'll certainly read right carries little risk; the same gap with many readings carries high risk. Likelihood is the second factor of risk, not foreign confidence. It is correctly *secondary* (active only under impact-YES) — that is what separates MID (depends, clear) from HIGH (depends, ambiguous); remove it and the mid/high distinction collapses.
**Collision:** the "difficulty-not-importance" charge dissolves once the frame is *risk-of-building-wrong* (not importance); "decorative" downgrades to "asymmetric/conditional," which the gate→escalate mapping correctly encodes. **SURVIVE** — caveat: the finding MUST state the risk-of-building-wrong framing explicitly, else likelihood reads as foreign. (Constructive: name the frame.)

### C3 — The booleans (false precision / "audit the gut" a rationalization?)
**Prosecution:** impact and ambiguity are continuous; forcing yes/no fabricates crispness the reality lacks ("multiple readings?" — 2 weak ones count?). "Audit the gut" just dresses an arbitrary gut answer in a yes/no costume — the arbitrariness remains.
**Defense:** the booleans don't claim *reality* is binary — they claim the *decision* is (16-10 fixed a discrete low/mid/high output; a discrete output needs discretizing questions). "Audit the gut" survives because its value is not removing judgment but *locating* it: "impact? yes / ambiguous? no → mid, reason attached." Two disagreeing raters can now see WHERE they differ (impact vs ambiguity) — a gestalt can't. That is decomposed judgment, not false precision.
**Substance-axis prosecution (apply literally):** borderline gap *"route grain — concept vs sub-concept?"* → Impact "build wrong/rework if guessed?" plausibly YES (grain drives structure); Ambiguity "genuinely-different readings?" YES → HIGH, both answers from the first-pass perception (no deep pass). Passes. Harder gap with *middling* impact → the rater must round; but rounding the GATE ("does the build depend — y/n") is more glance-stable than rounding "how much (1-10)."
**Collision:** the false-precision hit lands *partially* on middling gaps (a forced round). But stakes are LOW (the rating is attributive, low-confidence, reversible per 16-10 — a mis-round only nudges the meta-loop, doesn't gate) → low-stakes burden-of-proof = "innocent until proven guilty"; an occasional forced-round is the cost of the discrete output the project already chose. **SURVIVE** — caveat (REFINE-grade): acknowledge boolean rounding on borderline gaps + lean on the cheap-mis-rating nature. "Audit the gut" SURVIVES as legitimate (adds locate-the-disagreement value).

### C4 — The deferability cap (mis-filed third axis / hides high-impact?)
**Prosecution:** "can you stub it and resolve after building?" is about *sequencing* (do-it-later) — which the routelister NOT-list forbids as control-flow — not reversibility. And capping-at-low is dangerous: a high-impact, highly-ambiguous gap that happens to be stubbable gets forced to low → a hidden time-bomb.
**Defense:** deferability = a facet of the existing burden-of-proof richness (spec verbatim: *"reversibility, blast-radius, scope, fixability"*). "Stub now, fix after" = "is committing to a wrong reading now *reversible*" = low blast-radius-over-time — an attribute of THIS gap's cost-if-wrong, not an ordering of routes (no control-flow). The time-bomb refutes itself: the cap is conditioned on **"safely** stub/placeholder" — if a wrong stub silently corrupts everything, it is *not safely* stubbable (the corruption *is* the irreversibility), so deferability = NO and the cap never fires. "Safely" is load-bearing.
**Collision:** prosecution's real residue: deferability is *operationally* a separate 3rd question even if *conceptually* a reversibility facet. Defense holds because filing it as a CAP (only ever lowers vitality, never generates it) is structurally distinct from the two generating axes. **SURVIVE** — caveat: keep "safely" load-bearing in the boolean's wording; note deferability's dual nature (conceptually reversibility-facet; operationally its own glance-question).

### C5 — The mapping (coverage / MID / contradiction)
**Enumeration (8 combos; I=impact, L=likelihood, D=deferability):**
- I=N → **low** (4 combos, any L,D) · I=Y,D=Y → **low** (cap; 2 combos) · I=Y,D=N,L=N → **mid** (1) · I=Y,D=N,L=Y → **high** (1). **All 8 covered, mutually exclusive, no gap.**
**Prosecution:** is I=N ∧ L=Y ∧ D=N (peripheral but ambiguous + non-deferrable) wrongly low? **Defense:** if the build doesn't depend on it, ambiguity is harmless (be wrong, nothing breaks) → low is correct. No contradiction. MID = exactly (I=Y,D=N,L=N) = "depends, not deferrable, reading clear → deepen quickly, not urgent" — **well-defined.** Two routes to low (I=N *or* D=Y) both legitimately mean "don't worry now."
**Verdict: SURVIVE (clean — no caveat).**

### C6 — Lightness / glance re-test (LOAD-BEARING — does impact secretly need deep analysis?)
**Prosecution:** impact's sub-signals are coupling (*"if I change A, does B need to change?"* — tracing the dependency topology), reversibility, blast-radius, scope. Tracing coupling/blast-radius is *exactly* what the decompose discipline runs a whole process for → "answer impact at a glance" smuggles a decomposition pass into a first-pass field, contradicting 16-10 (D3 fails).
**Defense:** the sub-signals are *what to weigh*, NOT sub-questions to compute (innovation kept impact ONE boolean precisely for this). The glance form is *"would the build come out structurally wrong / need significant rework?"* — answerable from the SAME perception that surfaced the gap (you noticed the gap *because* you sensed the build leans on it). You don't trace the coupling graph; you sense load-bearing-vs-peripheral. The vocabulary explains *why*, it isn't a computation the rater runs.
**Collision:** the prosecution lands a REAL partial hit on worst-case gaps — honestly answering impact can shade from a glance into a few seconds' reflection. But: (a) 16-10 set confidence LOW — a first-pass, possibly-wrong impact answer is *acceptable*; the field doesn't demand a correct impact assessment; (b) asymmetric default — if unsure at a glance, default impact=Y and let likelihood/deferability sort it (graceful degradation). So glance-fit holds for the common case and degrades to "a few seconds," never to "run a decompose pass." **SURVIVE** — **strongest caveat in the set (REFINE-grade):** the finding MUST state that impact's sub-signals are *weighed-not-computed* and that a *first-pass, low-confidence (possibly-wrong) answer is acceptable* — otherwise a conscientious rater over-analyzes and breaks the glance constraint.

### C7 — Worth-it (earns its cost vs a bare gut label?)
**Prosecution:** 16-10 already made vitality low-confidence + attributive; the meta-loop only weakly uses it. A 2-axis/3-boolean rubric on an explicitly low-stakes signal is over-engineering — just write "low/mid/high, gut, low-confidence."
**Defense:** cost = ~3 yes/no questions (seconds), bounded *because* it reuses existing vocabulary (nothing new to learn). Benefit a bare label can't give: (a) cross-run consistency — vitality *accumulates* in the route-map across re-runs, so same-gap-rated-similarly matters; (b) meta-loop legibility — it sees *why* a gap is high (impact vs ambiguity) and chooses develop-vs-deepen.
**Collision (elegance/purpose-fitness):** "if [rubric-instead-of-bare-label] were left in, would the candidate still de-fuzz AND stay light?" — a bare label fails de-fuzzing (the user's explicit ask); the rubric de-fuzzes at 3 reused glance-questions = the *minimum* that de-fuzzes → not over-engineered. **SURVIVE** — caveat: worth-it holds ONLY at the 3-question size; promoting sub-signals to questions or adding a 4th axis crosses into over-engineering. **C7 depends on the lightness bound (P4) being enforced.**

> **The finding must carry a short "how to rate at a glance" usage-note:** (i) the frame is *risk of building on a wrong/unresolved reading* (not importance) — so impact AND likelihood both belong; (ii) impact's sub-signals (coupling/reversibility/scope) are *weighed, not computed* — answer from first-pass perception; (iii) a *first-pass, low-confidence, possibly-wrong* rating is acceptable (a mis-rating is cheap/reversible — it nudges, doesn't gate). 

With this note the assembly lands solidly **viable**. Without it, a conscientious rater could over-analyze (breaking glance, D3) or misread vitality as importance (making likelihood look foreign, C2). The usage-note IS the assembly-level constructive output → carry into the finding.
