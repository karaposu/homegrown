## PART 1 — The paper's functional claims (FACTs A-*), neural substrate quarantined

- **A-1 [core] The bridge thesis: attention and working memory are one system, not two.** *"attention and working memory are increasingly viewed as overlapping constructs"* (L10-11); the paper's whole point is that *"top-down modulation serves as a common neural mechanism underlying these two cognitive operations"* (L12-13). FACT. Functional residue (substrate stripped): **the machinery that decides what to attend to is the same machinery that operates working memory** — selection and holding are not separate subsystems.

- **A-2 [core] Top-down modulation = enhance relevant + SUPPRESS irrelevant, driven by goals.** *"top-down signals generate neural contrast by enhancing activity … for items that are relevant and suppressing activity for items irrelevant to task goals"* (L29-31). Glossary: top-down modulation *"may involve enhancement of task-relevant representations and/or suppression for task-irrelevant representations"* (L557-560). FACT. Functional residue: attention is **two dissociable operations — boosting the wanted AND actively pushing down the unwanted** — governed by the current goal.

- **A-3 [core] The same modulation operates at ALL FOUR stages of a WM episode.** Stages (Glossary L548-552): expectation, encoding, maintenance, retrieval. *"top-down modulation is engaged during both stimulus-present and stimulus-absent stages … expectation of an ensuing stimulus …, selection and encoding …, maintenance … and memory retrieval"* (L15-18). FACT. Functional residue: **one selection discipline runs across the whole life of a held item** — before it arrives, as it enters, while it is held, and as it is pulled back out.

- **A-4 [supporting] Expectation: a "filtering set" is pre-established before the item even arrives.** McNab et al.: greater control-region activity *"when participants were cued to expect distraction,"* interpreted as *"establishing a 'filtering set' to focus encoding resources only on the relevant information"* (L100-107). FACT. Functional residue: **you can pre-configure what to admit/reject before loading — a stance set in advance of encoding.**

- **A-5 [core] Early filtering prevents overloading a limited capacity.** *"optimal WM performance was dependent upon effectively filtering irrelevant information at early processing stages, presumably to prevent overloading a limited WM capacity"* (L121-124); *"limitations in attentional allocation … may serve as a limiting factor in the amount of information we can encode"* (L142-143). FACT. Functional residue: **because holding-capacity is small, the defense is to filter early — keep the irrelevant OUT before it consumes the scarce hold.** (Directly meets paper 2's rich-but-poor / reach≠grasp.)

- **A-6 [core] Retro-cues: re-prioritize what is ALREADY held, mid-maintenance, without retrieving or re-encoding.** *"Retro-cues differ from post-cues in that they do not prompt immediate retrieval … but instead provide information about the relevance of a given item for subsequent retrieval"*; they *"trigger top-down biasing mechanisms that operate upon representations being maintained in WM and consistently confer large benefits"* (L205-208). FACT. Functional residue: **you can re-weight the priority of already-loaded content in the middle of holding it, when new information about relevance arrives — a mid-flight re-focus, distinct from loading and from retrieving.**

- **A-7 [supporting] "Refreshing" — a minimal act of bringing a held item back to the focus.** *"merely to think back to a previously viewed item, i.e., to 'refresh' their current focus of WM"* (L214-215); ref 71 titles it *"Refreshing: a minimal executive function."* FACT. Functional residue: **a lightweight keep-alive — re-touch a held item to keep it in focus.**

- **A-8 [supporting] Distraction vs interruption during maintenance are different damage modes.** Distractors degrade holding *"especially when distractors are of the same category as memoranda"* (L258-259); and *"when maintenance was interrupted by a secondary task, the encoded items were not maintained but rather reactivated after the interruption"* (L267-269). FACT. Functional residue: **passive distraction (same-kind noise degrades the hold) and active interruption (a competing task evicts the hold, forcing later reactivation) are distinct failures.**

- **A-9 [core, the standout specific] The SUPPRESSION DEFICIT: holding can fail specifically because filtering-out fails, independently of boosting or storage.** Box 1: aging WM deficits are *"selective for deficits in the suppression of distracting information"* (L616-618) — enhancement and storage can be intact while suppression fails. FACT. Functional residue: **there is a failure mode that is purely "couldn't keep the irrelevant out," separable from "couldn't bring the relevant in" and from "couldn't hold it."**

- **A-10 [supporting] Retrieval = internally-directed selection, same act as external search.** *"selecting a target from WM and from the environment may be largely analogous acts of internally and externally directed selective attention"* (L297-298). FACT. Functional residue: **finding the right held item is the same operation as finding the right thing in the world — search turned inward.**

### Quarantined (DOESN'T-APPLY — neural substrate; import test bars these)
- All brain regions (PFC, parietal, IFJ/MFG/IFG, FFA, IPS, basal ganglia, default network); all methods (fMRI, EEG/ERP, rTMS/TMS, MEG); P1 amplitude, contralateral delay activity, functional connectivity, neural oscillations; the aging *neurology*. These are the paper's bulk (~70%) and are **out** — we have no neural substrate to map them onto. What survives quarantine is the functional residue A-1…A-10.

## PART 2 — Our meaning-commitments (FACTs for the mapping)

- **B-1 [core]** Working memory = a traverse's live in-context scratchpad (the **context window**); rich-but-poor (paper 2); transient. FACT (prior findings).
- **B-2 [core]** **Warming** — a navigation session loads terrain (codebase orientation / long-run + recent trajectory) to "see" before it moves. FACT (steering canon). This is an ENHANCEMENT operation (bring relevant in).
- **B-3 [core]** **reach≠grasp / the availability illusion** — vast reach, tiny live grasp; a session is fooled about how much it holds. FACT (paper-2 finding, load-bearing regulative import).
- **B-4 [supporting]** The **orchestrator / "the will"** — SUSTRALL's between-traverse selector of what to work on. FACT (canon).
- **B-5 [supporting]** The **open-directions index (ODI)** — ranks/holds prospective directions (option memory). FACT (prior finding).
- **B-6 [core]** **cold navigation** — a session that reads too little and makes tidy-but-wrong maps. FACT (steering canon). This is an ENHANCEMENT failure (under-loaded).
- **B-7 [supporting]** The memory **lifecycle stages** (encode→consolidate→retrieve→reconsolidate) are INTER-traverse (across sessions). FACT (paper-1 finding).
- **B-8 [supporting]** **Compaction / fresh perception** between steps — context is flushed/summarized. FACT (harness behavior + SUSTRALL).
- **B-9 [core]** The **import test** — real iff NAMES-what-we-do-unnamed OR RESOLVES-a-confusion-we-had; else decorative. FACT (the inherited gate).

## PART 3 — The mapping (possibility-items: HAVE / LACK / DOESN'T-APPLY / REVERSE)

### HAVE — the paper names something we already do
- **M-H1 [core]** A-1 (attention & WM are one system) ↔ **warming/selection is not separate from the context window** (B-1,B-2). We already treat "what to load" and "what's held" as tightly coupled; paper 2 already gave "context window IS working memory." So A-1 is *largely already ours* — candidate **decorative-or-confirming** (test in sensemaking: does it ADD that the *selector* (the will) is the same system, not just the substrate?).
- **M-H2 [core]** A-5 (filter early to avoid overloading small capacity) ↔ **reach≠grasp** (B-3) + **warming loads selectively** (B-2). We HAVE the constraint (paper 2) and the enhancement side (warming). A-5 restates the constraint — candidate confirming; its *filter-early* angle may add the suppression side (see M-L1).
- **M-H3 [supporting]** A-3 (one selection across stages) ↔ our selection *is* scattered across warming (pre), surfacing (encode), context-holding (maintain), retrieval — we DO select at each, but **have not named it as one cross-stage operation** (candidate LACK-of-a-name — see M-L2).
- **M-H4 [supporting]** A-7 (refreshing / keep-alive) ↔ **re-reading a file / re-stating a finding to keep it in context** — we do this ad hoc. Candidate confirming/minor.
- **M-H5 [supporting]** A-10 (retrieval = inward search) ↔ our surfacing/lookup is already "search turned inward" over canon/memory. Confirming.

### LACK — the paper names something we plausibly need but have no concept for
- **M-L1 [core — the strongest candidate] SUPPRESSION as a first-class operation, distinct from enhancement.** A-2 + A-9. We have **enhancement** (warming, loading, ODI-ranking = bring the relevant IN) but **no explicit concept of actively keeping the irrelevant OUT / pushing it down**. Our model of "loading" is *additive* (warm more); the paper says the other half — *suppression* — is dissociable and can fail alone (A-9, the suppression deficit). Candidate **LACK**: a named "keep-out / down-weight" operation and its failure mode. [external-anchor to test in sensemaking: does cold-navigation OR any canon already cover "too much irrelevant context," or only "too little relevant context"?]
- **M-L2 [supporting] The context-POLLUTION / distraction failure mode, distinct from cold-navigation.** A-8 + A-9. **cold navigation** (B-6) = under-loaded (enhancement failure). We appear to LACK a name for the *opposite*: a traverse **over-polluted** by irrelevant/tangential context that degrades or derails it (suppression failure). Two orthogonal failure axes: too-little-relevant vs too-much-irrelevant. Candidate **LACK**.
- **M-L3 [supporting] The pre-set "filtering stance" (expectation stage).** A-4. We warm by *loading* terrain; we do not explicitly *pre-configure a reject-stance* ("expect and refuse these distractions") before a traverse. Candidate LACK — but may be premature (mechanism-flavored).
- **M-L4 [supporting] Mid-flight re-prioritization of already-held content (retro-cue).** A-6. When new information mid-traverse changes what matters, do we **re-weight the already-loaded context** without reloading or concluding? We do a weak version (later disciplines re-weight earlier material), but have no named "retro-cue" re-focus. Candidate LACK-or-decorative (test: is this real, or just "the pipeline moves on"?).

### DOESN'T-APPLY — does not transfer
- **M-DA1** The entire neural-substrate apparatus (quarantined above). DOESN'T-APPLY.
- **M-DA2** The consciousness angle is absent here (unlike paper 2); the aging *neurology* (Box 1) doesn't apply, though its *functional* lesson (suppression can fail alone) survives as A-9.
- **M-DA3** The stimulus-present/absent perceptual specifics (P1, receptive fields) — DOESN'T-APPLY.

### REVERSE — what WE have that the paper doesn't cover
- **M-R1 [supporting]** Our selection is **inter-traverse** (the orchestrator picks across sessions; the ODI holds prospective directions across runs). Paper 3 is entirely **intra-episode** (one WM task). So our "attention" has a cross-session scope the paper never addresses — a place where paper 3 UNDER-covers us.
- **M-R2 [supporting]** We **externalize** working memory (files); the paper's WM is purely in-head. So our "maintenance" can be durable-by-snapshot (paper-2 result) — beyond the paper.
