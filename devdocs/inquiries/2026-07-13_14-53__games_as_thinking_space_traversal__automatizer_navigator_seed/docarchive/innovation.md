## P1 — THE CLAIMS, PRECISELY (principal candidates, finding-ready)

**Claim A (the domain claim):** *Games are a genuine traversal domain, not a metaphor for one. A game presents a real terrain — its state space of reachable positions, builds, and routes — and a real mover, the player; playing IS working through that space toward a purpose. Four bounds keep the claim honest: game terrain is AUTHORED (a designer guarantees a path exists; thinking-space has no designer), win conditions are FIXED (ventures define their own purposes), state is LEGIBLE (games show a score; "meaningful traversal" is an open question precisely because thinking lacks one), and runs REPEAT (a game restarts identically; a question never re-poses).*

**P1 Inversion-candidate (mandated: "games are NOT a domain — playing is maze-solving inside someone else's finished thought; real traversal creates its terrain by moving"):** the inversion BITES and then IMPROVES the claim. Its true half: the FORMAL space is pre-authored, and canon's terrain is not. Its failure: the player's traversal runs over their EPISTEMIC terrain — the knowledge-frontier — which is genuinely unauthored and genuinely grows by moving (fog-of-war lifts; mechanics get discovered; speedrunners find new terrain in twenty-year-old games the designers never knew existed — sequence breaks surprise their own authors). **Refinement adopted into Claim A:** *the analogy runs at the epistemic layer — the player's discovered terrain — not the formal layer; the authored-terrain disanalogy applies to the formal space only.* (Depth-check: system-level — it states WHERE the whole mapping holds.) *(5-test: passes; the refined claim survives its own strongest objection by absorbing it as a layer distinction.)*

**Claim B (the automatizer claim):** *A good game automatizer is a good navigator/orchestrator — for the decision-preserving family. Game automation comes in an ordered family (execution → routing → scheduling → orchestration); the claim holds where automation climbs that ladder while keeping or creating decision layers, and fails at the family's own counterexample — the bot-that-plays, which deletes the decision layer: what game communities ban as cheating and idle games experience as death.*

**The convergent-discovery statement (the frame):** *The project's canon derived a law from reactor physics: the gap between yield and uptake is a decision-slot, and automation that auto-fires the next turn deletes the steering. Game design, watching millions of players, arrived at the same law independently: automate the executing and the game deepens; automate the deciding and the game dies. Two derivation paths, no contact, one law — games are a mass-scale empirical confirmation site for a principle canon reached by reasoning.*
