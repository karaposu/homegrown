## Finding

### 1. The paradigm, named and adjudicated (I2)

Your instinct was right, and it can now be said precisely. The new scene-description paradigm is better — **for what the pair could not teach** — and its advantage decomposes into four nameable elements: a **failure-arc** (characters err, consequences unfold, the correction arrives); **multi-agent partiality** (several searchers, each holding a fragment); **proportion-between-findings** (the thing the divers lose — the parable's müvazene); and **config-dependent visibility** (the telescope's gift: what you see depends on how you look). Three of these co-require each other's form — an arc needs agents erring in time; partiality needs agents plural; proportion needs findings plural — which is why they cannot be grafted onto the potter or the sonographer without making a different scene. The fourth grafts anywhere. And the old pair keeps its own virtue the parable-form lacks: mechanism-density, every prop mapping to machinery. So: not "parables beat mechanism-scenes" — **element-rich narrative scenes beat single-practitioner scenes exactly where the project is many-sessioned**, which is where traversal memory lives.

### 2. The scene — the night-survey observatory (I1)

You have looked through a telescope once: everything dark, then one bright point. Now put many nights behind it. A mountain observatory: the sky above is vast and mostly empty — the masses in it are rare, scattered, unlit. Each night one astronomer points the instrument and exposes a plate. **What appears depends on the config** — chase the far bright giants and a near, dim body slides through unseen. Some nights are survey sweeps [the digest-read]; some are pointed follow-ups on yesterday's oddity [the topic-read]. The light arriving tonight is old — every plate shows an object as it *was* [every finding shows the project as it was when written]. Clouds eat whole nights; the plates already taken are not eaten. And every plate goes into the **shared catalog** — positions, exposures, and **gaps known as gaps** [the frontier]. The catalog aims the next night's pointing [the warm-up]. Between seasons the astronomers **grind their own lenses** by what the sky taught them [the specs, improved by findings]. No one has seen the sky whole. The catalog, read together at dawn, is the closest thing the mountain has to an open eye.

**The arc (transplanted from the parable).** One astronomer's plates keep catching comets. He concludes the sky's essence is comets; his colleagues' nebulae are "smudges of my comets." He re-reads their plates to fit — draws comet-lines between unrelated lights — and, chasing the far and bright, never notices the near dim body crossing his own plates. In time he half-believes the deep sky is empty. No hero corrects him. **The dawn reading does:** the catalog holds his plates AND theirs in one reckoning, each find in its own kind, in proportion. He points differently the next night.

### 3. The contrast — the treasure-dive, as it was written

A classical parable (from the Risale-i Nur; rendered compactly and secularly — in the source, the open-eyed one is the Quran): *Several men dive to the sea's floor for a treasure holding every kind of jewel. In the dark, one man's hand finds a long diamond — and he concludes the treasure IS diamonds; when his friends speak of other stones, he decides theirs are mere engravings on his. Another has found a round ruby, another a different stone, and each believes his own kind is the treasure's essence and the others' are ornament. Proportion is lost; balance goes. To keep their fragments central they bend into forced interpretations — and some, in the end, deny the treasure entirely. Only an eye already open — one that encompasses the whole — sees each jewel in its place and shows the treasure in order.*

Read what the divers LACK: no logbook, no dive-lines, no surface conference — **no shared memory at all.** That is why the fragment swallows the whole; the demotion move ("your finds are decorations of mine"), the forced readings, and the final denial are what a many-searcher practice becomes WITHOUT the machinery this project builds. The open-eyed diver is the limit no single searcher reaches — what the observatory's catalog-plus-dawn-reading approaches over many nights, no one night owning it. *(This scene replaces echolocation as the corpus's contrast: echolocation showed memory fading; the divers show what the fading costs when there are many of you.)*
