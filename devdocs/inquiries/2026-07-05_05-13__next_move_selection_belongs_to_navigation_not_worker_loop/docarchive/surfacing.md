## The Grounding FACTs (verbatim / near-verbatim from the SUSTRALL canon)

- **FACT-1 — the "one revolution" sequence puts SEE + DECIDE *after* the worker's PROBE.** Canon: *"probe — a worker loop runs one inquiry to a finding; **see** — the navigational session enumerates the field of directions over the finished work; **decide** — the orchestrator makes a loop-control decision and **selects the next route**; dispatch — the next session starts."* So "what should I work on next" = the **decide/select-the-next-route** step = the **orchestrator**, occurring *after* a worker probe finishes. The worker's job (probe) is one earlier step and is over by then. [core]

- **FACT-2 — the worker's control scope is per-cycle only.** Canon (the components): *"Loop runners — the hands… each executing one inquiry at full depth. **Control scope: per-cycle only (conclude-or-iterate within one inquiry).**"* The worker decides only conclude-or-iterate *within its own inquiry* — nothing across inquiries. [core]

- **FACT-3 — the navigational session SEES but does not CHOOSE, and it runs over FINISHED work.** Canon: *"The navigational individual session — the eyes. A fresh, context-isolated session, warmed on the terrain… running the enumerator (routelister) over a **finished cycle or territory**. It produces the typed field of directions. Two load-bearing boundaries: **it never chooses ("Navigation sees; it does not choose")**, and it is a session, not a standing document — fresh perception each time."* [core]

- **FACT-4 — the orchestrator is the Selector; selection is its move; traversal memory is its organ.** Canon: *"The orchestrator — the will. The cross-inquiry controller… Its repertoire: the closed seven-move loop-control vocabulary… **route selection among what the eyes enumerated**… Its organ: traversal memory."* And the correspondence table: *"Orchestrator | Selector + Runner (+ Evaluator at L4) + Memory."* [core]

- **FACT-5 — "one enumerator, two controllers": the two decision scopes are per-cycle (runner) and cross-inquiry (orchestrator).** Canon (closing commitment): *"one enumerator, two controllers — routelister enumerates; **the runner (per-cycle) and the orchestrator (cross-inquiry) decide**; the seven loop-control moves are controller decisions, never an enumerated menu."* "What to work on next" is a **cross-inquiry** decision → the orchestrator, not the per-cycle runner. [core]

- **FACT-6 — the dividing line is the INQUIRY BOUNDARY.** Canon (the bootstrap jump): *"automation lives below the inquiry boundary (six disciplines auto-chain within an inquiry); **above it, every function is human — cross-inquiry traversal** lives in the user's head."* The worker loop is *below* the inquiry boundary; next-move-selection is *above* it. ## The WHY-Reasons (possibility items — why next-move-selection is navigation/orchestrator, not the worker loop)

### WHY-1 — Whole-field VISIBILITY  ·  relevance: **core** · confidence: HIGH
Choosing the next move requires seeing the *whole field* of directions. A worker traverse is heads-down in **one** inquiry with **one** topic — it structurally cannot see the field. The navigational session is *deliberately* fresh + context-isolated + warmed on the terrain (FACT-3) precisely so it *can* survey the whole field over finished work. You cannot select across options you cannot see; the worker can't see them. [inference grounded in FACT-3]

### WHY-2 — CONTROL-SCOPE (canon-defined)  ·  relevance: **core** · confidence: HIGH
The canon *assigns* scopes: the worker runner's control scope is **per-cycle only** (FACT-2); cross-inquiry selection is the **orchestrator's** move (FACT-4, FACT-5). This isn't a judgment call — it's the architecture's stated allocation. "What to work on next" is cross-inquiry by definition, so it is definitionally not the worker's to make. [FACT-2, FACT-4, FACT-5]

### WHY-3 — SEES-vs-CHOOSES separation  ·  relevance: **core** · confidence: HIGH
Even the layer that *sees* the whole field (the navigational session) is barred from choosing — *"Navigation sees; it does not choose"* (FACT-3). Choosing is reserved for the orchestrator (FACT-4). So the architecture puts a wall between enumeration and selection, and **both** sit *above* the worker. The worker is two roles removed from selection (it neither sees the field nor chooses over it). [FACT-3, FACT-4]

### WHY-4 — FRESHNESS / anti-tunnel-vision  ·  relevance: **core** · confidence: MED-HIGH
A worker deep in a problem has *biased, narrow* perception — that's what depth costs. The navigational session is a **fresh session, not a standing document — "fresh perception each time"** (FACT-3), specifically to avoid inheriting the worker's tunnel-vision into the next-move decision. Letting a heads-down worker pick the next move would import exactly the bias the isolation exists to shed. [inference grounded in FACT-3]

### WHY-5 — DISPATCH-ORDER (the question was already answered upstream)  ·  relevance: **core** · confidence: HIGH
By the time a worker traverse is running, "what to work on next" has **already been answered** — the orchestrator's decide/select + dispatch (FACT-1) is *why this worker has this topic*. So a worker asking "what should I work on next?" is re-asking a question resolved one layer up, one step earlier in the revolution. The worker's job is to *execute the answer*, not re-derive it. [inference grounded in FACT-1]
