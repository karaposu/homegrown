## Phase 5 — Conceptual Stabilization

### SV6 — Stabilized Model

**Yes — I understand, and you're right on the architecture's own terms. Here's why, and what my prior finding got wrong.**

1. **Why next-move-selection is not the worker loop's job.** The canon's revolution runs *probe → see → decide → dispatch*: a **worker** runs one inquiry to a finding (probe); the **navigational session** enumerates the field over the finished work (see); the **orchestrator** selects the next route (decide); then the next session is dispatched. "What should I work on next?" is the **decide/select** step — it happens *after* a worker's probe, one layer up. Five canon-grounded reasons make it the navigation/orchestrator's job, not the worker's: the worker can't **see** the whole field (it's heads-down in one topic); its **control scope is per-cycle only** by canon; even the eyes only **see, never choose** (choosing is the orchestrator); the eyes are deliberately **fresh/isolated** to keep worker tunnel-vision out of the next-move; and by the time a worker runs, "what next" was **already answered** — that's *why* it has its topic. The worker's job is to execute the answer, not re-derive it.

2. **What my prior finding got wrong (named plainly).** I wrote that "the hard steering/selection job belongs to push." That mislocated steering on **two counts**:
   - **See vs decide.** Surfacing an aging direction is an *enumeration* (a "see") — it can put a direction in front of the selector, but it is not *selecting*. I called a see-function "the selection job."
   - **Layer.** I framed pull and push as "two reads over the index" — reads a worker performs. But selection is a *cross-inquiry* decision, *above* the inquiry boundary. A worker-loop read is the wrong layer for it.

3. **What the prior finding still got right (bounded correction, both ways).** Its *diagnosis* holds: pull's "feeds the steering layer" claim does over-reach, and the index (the store) is the valuable substrate. What's wrong is only the *prescription* — "add push as a second worker-loop read to do the steering." So this corrects one recommendation, not the whole finding.

4. **Where pull and push actually belong.** The index's consumers split by **layer**, not by pull-vs-push-as-two-worker-reads:
   - **Below the inquiry boundary (the worker loop): pull.** A worker, at the start of its already-assigned inquiry, queries the index and enriches its chosen topic. Correctly in-layer. Its inability to answer "what should I work on next" is **not a defect** — that question isn't its job.
   - **Above the inquiry boundary (navigation + orchestrator): the "push" function, already provided by the architecture.** The navigational session — fresh, isolated, warmed, running over finished work — enumerates the whole field *including* the aging directions parked in the index; the orchestrator then **selects** the next route using traversal memory. So "surface aging high-value directions uninvited" is not a new worker-loop interrupt — it's just **making the open-directions index one of the inputs the eyes enumerate.** The organ already exists; the index feeds it.

**The one-line correction:** "what should I work on next?" is the orchestrator's *decide* step over the field the navigational session *sees* — both above the inquiry boundary. My prior finding put that job inside a worker-loop read ("push"); the honest fix is to feed the index into the eyes, and to stop asking the worker loop (via any read, pull or push) to steer.
