### The relevance verdict + axis argument (K1)

**Prosecution:** (a) the open-weights vulnerability (frame-premise c) — the self-contradiction argument assumes provider lock-in. (b) Is "governance rises with capability" actually true, or just plausible? The four precedents (aviation/finance/medicine/nuclear) are real but SELECTED — survivorship bias (industries that DIDN'T grow a governance layer aren't cited). (c) The axis-orthogonality could be overstated (a model could become more inspectable as it gets smarter — interpretability research).

**Defense:** (a) adopted — the open-weights caveat stated, and shown to SHIFT not obsolete the edge (open weights commoditize the engine → the governance layer becomes the differentiator). (b) genuine — the precedents are softened from "proof" to "strong recurring pattern," and the mechanism (more autonomy + more stakes → more demand for accountability) is given as the *reason* the pattern holds, not just the pattern. (c) conceded as a real erosion vector: interpretability could make models more inspectable — but model-internal interpretability is still first-party (the fox-henhouse problem) and not operator-owned/portable; the edge narrows to *neutral, external, portable* auditability. 

**Verdict: REFINE.** The open-weights caveat; precedents as pattern-not-proof (with the mechanism); the interpretability erosion vector named.
