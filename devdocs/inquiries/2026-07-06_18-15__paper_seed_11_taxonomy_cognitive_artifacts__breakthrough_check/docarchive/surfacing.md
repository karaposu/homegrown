## Part 1 — PAPER FACTs (Heersmink 2013; the taxonomy + distinct sub-claims)

- **A-1 [core] — Cognitive artifact = FUNCTIONAL KIND.** "human-made, physical objects that functionally contribute to performing a cognitive task"; a functional kind "defined purely by its function" (Carrara & Vermaas; Kornblith: "what makes two artifacts members of the same kind is that they perform the same function"). NOT defined by intrinsic properties nor designer intent — "established by the intentions of the user and by how it is used." Improvised artifacts count (the rented DVD on the desk becomes a mnemonic by use).
- **A-2 [core] — The 3-level taxonomy.** family → genus → species → token (Fig. 1). Family = all cognitive artifacts, by function. Membership is **monothetic** (one property determines membership: function at family; informational property at genus/species). Upward deduction valid, downward not.
- **A-3 [core] — The two GENERA.** REPRESENTATIONAL (info-structures *about* the world — "representational information") vs ECOLOGICAL / non-representational (info-structures *as* the world — "ecological information"). "Exhibiting an information-structure is thus a necessary and sufficient condition for being a cognitive artifact."
- **A-4 [core] — REPRESENTATIONAL species (Peirce's trichotomy).** ICONIC (isomorphic/similar to object — maps, blueprints, graphs, diagrams), INDEXICAL (direct causal connection — thermometers, compasses, weathervanes, measuring instruments), SYMBOLIC (meaning via shared convention/rules — words, sentences, numerals, formalisms, **tables**). A token may combine all three; classify by the **predominant** ground.
- **A-5 [core] — ECOLOGICAL species.** SPATIAL (info encoded into LOCATION — car keys on a spot, washed-vs-unwashed dishes, portals-left-open / prominent desktop positions) vs STRUCTURAL (info EMERGES from manipulating a structure — rearranging Scrabble tiles to prompt word recall, rotating Tetris zoids). Structural is more dynamic than spatial.
- **A-6 [core] — EXOGRAM vs ENGRAM (Donald 1991).** Exograms (external representations) differ from engrams (biomemory): storage capacity "far exceeds" biomemory, reformattable, transmissible across media, malleable; engrams less flexible. "Because exograms have such different functional and representational properties… they have the capacity to complement the shortcomings and limitations of engrams… this is arguably why we have developed exograms in the first place."
- **A-7 [sub] — ARTIFACT vs TECHNIQUE vs NATUREFACT.** technology/artifact = intentionally designed physical object; technique/skill = a method/procedure (Hutchins's "internal artifacts" — the Micronesian navigator's perceptual strategies — are better seen as INTERNALIZED cognitive techniques, not artifacts); naturefact = a natural object used for cognition (stars for navigation) — not intentionally made. Intentional control over content/function is larger for artifacts → "significantly more variety" → deeper integration ("functionally and informationally malleable").
- **A-8 [sub] — The ARTIFACT-CENTERED (non-anthropocentric) METHOD.** Categorize by the artifact's OWN informational properties, NOT by the cognitive process it serves (contra Norman/Brey/Kirsh, who "start with a cognitive agent and then categorize artifacts on the basis of the cognitive processes to which they contribute"). "I take as my point of departure the specific properties of cognitive artifacts and then categorize them on the basis of those properties, not on the properties or goals of the agents."
- **A-9 [side] — "THE WORLD IS ITS OWN BEST MODEL" / 007 principle (Brooks/Clark).** "Why create an expensive representation of the world, when you can use the world itself as a model?" Ecological artifacts embody this; "evolved creatures will neither store nor process information in costly ways when they can use the structure of the environment… as a convenient stand-in."
- **A-10 [side] — Genera not mutually exclusive.** An alphabetized bookcase = representational (titles) + ecological (spatial order); the two "are thus not mutually exclusive."
- **A-11 [side] — Interpretation / consumption.** For a genuine triadic relation, an agent must interpret the representation *qua* representation (understand the sign→object relation); representations "are psychologically efficacious in that they cause beliefs"; misinterpretation → false beliefs → hindered performance.
- **A-12 [sub] — The paper's own purpose.** It answers Clark's "Holy Grail" call for "a taxonomy of different types of external prop, and a systematic understanding of how they help (and hinder) human performance"; it is explicitly "an important first step" — a classificatory scaffold, not a mechanism or a practice.
- **A-13 [context] — SAME AUTHOR as paper 14.** Heersmink 2013 (this taxonomy) is the earlier classificatory substrate under Heersmink 2015 (paper 14, the extended-mind "stance-namer" already absorbed as confirming-plus). Cites Clark & Chalmers, Hutchins, Kirsh, Sutton, Donald — the same situated-cognition canon paper 14 drew on.
