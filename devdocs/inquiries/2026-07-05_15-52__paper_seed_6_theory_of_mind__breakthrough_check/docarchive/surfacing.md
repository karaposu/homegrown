## PART 1 — FACTs surfaced from paper 6 (the territory's own claims)

### The Theory-of-Mind concept
- **A-1** [FACT · core] **Theory of Mind = inferring others' mental states.** "the ability to make inferences about others' mental states" (L4–5); we "explicitly model and respond to other people's mental states: their knowledge, intentions, beliefs, and desires" (L20–22). Modular, rapid, automatic, universal, with a "stereotyped developmental sequence" (L38).
- **A-2** [FACT · core] **ToM develops in graded stages of increasing difficulty.** joint attention + protodeclarative pointing (~18mo, L40) → pretend play / decoupling pretend from reality (L52–54) → desire (age 2, L55–57) → **first-order false belief** (3–4: others can hold beliefs "that differ from the child's own", L59–66) → **second-order false belief, "belief about belief"** (6–7: "understand that other people can also represent mental states", L70–73) → **faux pas recognition** (9–11, L106–108).
- **A-3** [FACT · core] **Subtle deficits show ONLY on the most advanced tasks.** "Performance on the most developmentally advanced theory of mind tasks is an index of how severe a person's theory of mind deficit is. Subtle theory of mind deficits can only be picked up with the most developmentally advanced tasks." (L128–132).
- **A-4** [FACT · core] **The physical-vs-mental representation distinction (the false-photograph test).** Autistic children / young children CAN represent physical representations but not mental ones: on the false-photograph test they are "not fooled into thinking that the photograph will show a table with nothing on it" — "they can understand physical representations, such as photographs and pictures, but not mental representations" (L98–105). So the deficit is specific to modeling MINDS, not representations in general.
- **A-5** [FACT · core] **The faux pas requires TWO mental states + an affective read.** "one has to represent two mental states: that the person saying it does not know that they should not say it and that the person hearing it would feel insulted or hurt… both a cognitive component and empathic affective component" (L110–114).

### The methodological crux — the working-memory dissociation
- **A-6** [FACT · core] **An apparent ToM deficit was really a working-memory failure in disguise.** Dorsolateral (DFC) patients "had difficulty only on versions of the tasks that placed demands on working memory" (L14–15). "when the story was in front of the subjects, so that there was no memory load, patients made almost no errors" (L332–333). "Only memory load predicts their performance, indicating that working memory limitations may provide the best explanation of their results" (L402–405).
- **A-7** [FACT · core] **The prior study was CONFOUNDED — it hadn't controlled for the working-memory load.** Price et al.'s "deficit" in DFC patients: "these patients could have failed on this task because of working memory limitations rather than because their theory of mind was impaired" (L230–233). This study's fix = two conditions, **memory-load** (story hidden, must remember, L576–581) vs **no-memory-load** (story stays in front, L582–584), dissociating the two capacities.
- **A-8** [FACT · supporting] **The Yes-bias control.** DFC patients always said "Yes"; the authors controlled by checking the follow-up "who committed the faux pas" — a bias would fail it, real recognition would pass it (L438–454). (A second dissociation-of-confounds move.)

### The affective / substrate content
- **A-9** [FACT · peripheral] **"Hot" vs "cold" ToM.** OFC patients' faux-pas failure = "problems connecting their theory of mind inferences with an understanding of emotion" (L493–495); amygdala + OFC are for "the 'hot' aspects of theory of mind… interpreting the valence and significance of others' actions" (L497–499). Empathy intact; the integration of empathy WITH mental-state attribution is the deficit.
- **A-10** [FACT · peripheral] **ToM is a distributed circuit, not a localized module.** "not a unitary module but a collection of inferential abilities… a distributed circuit involving many regions" (L539–545). Substrate detail (OFC/DFC/Brodmann areas 8,9,11,46; SPECT/PET/MRI; patient lesions) = the ~70% quarantined bulk.

## PART 3 — Mapping possibility-items (three directions + reverse)

### Candidate A — CROSS-SESSION THEORY OF MIND (the seductive reframe; INFLATION-watch)
- **M-A1** [HAVE, leaning DECORATIVE · core] **cold-navigation ≈ a "read the record, miss the mind" failure.** The false-photograph structure (A-4) maps eerily onto B-1: the navigation session reads finding.md (a *physical* representation, the photograph) and "understands the local words" but fails the *mental* reconstruction ("does not understand the project"). BUT: cold-navigation **already names this failure**, and its prescribed fix (warming, B-2) is about the **terrain/world**, not about modeling the worker as an **agent**. So the ToM reframe re-describes a failure we already have — and where it *differs* (mind-modeling vs terrain-knowledge), it's not clearly a gap we actually suffer. → strong INFLATION candidate; adjudicate in Sensemaking.
- **M-A2** [HAVE, leaning DECORATIVE · supporting] **first-order false belief ≈ superseded findings.** Representing that "the worker (at write-time) believed X, but X is now known false" (A-2) maps onto B-5 (`SUPERSEDED_BY` / staleness). BUT we **already have** the relationship type + reconsolidation for this; the false-belief lens is an elegant re-labeling, not a new operation or a resolved confusion.
- **M-A3** [DOESN'T-APPLY · peripheral] **second-order false belief / faux pas / the two-mental-states + affect (A-5, A-9).** A solo harness has no social partner whose feelings it must model; the "hot"/empathic component (A-9) has no referent (no affect). Quarantine — like paper 4's off-domain apps.

### Candidate B — THE DISSOCIATION METHOD (the methodological residue; bounded-real watch)
- **M-B1** [LACK?, leaning BOUNDED-REAL · core] **an apparent competence failure may be a grasp failure in disguise — control for context-load before diagnosing.** A-6/A-7 is a diagnostic: DFC patients LOOKED like they had a high-level (ToM) deficit, but removing the working-memory load revealed it was a *context* failure, not a *competence* failure. Mapped: when a traverse/session appears to REASON badly (bad navigation, missed what matters), the failure may be a **grasp failure** (the relevant thing wasn't loaded — the availability illusion, B-3), not a competence failure. The **method**: put the info "in front of" the session (warm it / remove the grasp-load) and re-test — if the failure vanishes, it was grasp, not competence. This extends reach≠grasp (B-3) from the *self-perception* side to the **observer/evaluation** side (B-6) — a genuinely different angle + a concrete procedure. → the real candidate; test hard in Sensemaking.
- **M-B2** [HAVE / near-DECORATIVE · supporting] **graded-difficulty probes reveal subtle deficits (A-3).** "Subtle deficits can only be picked up with the most developmentally advanced tasks." Maps faintly onto the quality-assessment idea that harder probes reveal subtler failures — but this is close to generic ("harder tests find subtler bugs"); likely decorative.
- **M-B3** [HAVE / DECORATIVE · peripheral] **the Yes-bias control (A-8)** ≈ generic "control for response bias." Decorative — good experimental hygiene, not domain-specific to us.

### REVERSE — what WE have that paper 6 doesn't cover
- **M-R1** [REVERSE · supporting] Our sessions don't (need to) model *each other as agents with beliefs about beliefs* — they share a common artifact store and a single project goal. What looks like "theory of mind" for us is mostly **terrain-understanding** (warming) + **record-currency tracking** (superseded-by), not agent-modeling. This is the ground of the inflation charge against Candidate A.
- **M-R2** [REVERSE · supporting] Our evaluation layer (B-6) already exists; what A-6/A-7 adds (M-B1) is one *specific diagnostic* it may lack, not a new layer.
