## Finding-Ready Pieces (sharpened)

### Q1 — The use-picture (consolidated)
Two reads on two layers: **below the inquiry boundary**, a worker starting its assigned inquiry pulls related open directions → the offer-menu (absorb-while-warm / inform-the-finding / mark-done). **Above the boundary**, the navigational session reads the index as one enumeration input → aging open directions enter the typed field the orchestrator selects over. Traversal-memory roles: option-memory **spent** (pull→probe), **composed** (eyes→field), **reconciled** (mark-done→state). Caveat carried: the eyes-consumption is committed by the corrected findings; the steering canon holds only the "future navigation memory files" hook — cashing it is Q6's second condition.

The ODI is exactly this loop, made **shared and cross-traverse**: one index instead of per-inquiry `_route.md` files, so step 2 stops being "open the right inquiry folder" (per-session sweep) and becomes "read the one index." A spec that reads an artifact is easier to define, test, and warm than one that performs a sweep-and-reconstruct procedure. ## Piece-Level Inversions

- **Q5 inverted — "it IS a breakthrough on the new bar: unloading the eyes is the era's currency, and the ODI unloads."** Tested: the currency argument proves too much — any clerical improvement (better file naming, faster greps) also "unloads the eyes" and is obviously no breakthrough; magnitude and class matter. The class difference stands: the precedent removed a *discipline execution* from the eyes; the ODI removes *bookkeeping around its outputs*. The era's breakthrough-shaped event is the navigational session existing and running; the ODI is a step that makes that event easier — which is precisely the frontier classification, not the breakthrough one. **KILLED.**
- **Q4 inverted — "the unload is illusory: the sweep just moved into the loop; total work is conserved."** Tested: work is not conserved, three ways. (1) *Warm vs cold:* the filing traverse already holds its directions in context — appending them costs ~nothing; the cold eyes reconstructing them re-pays context acquisition every time. (2) *Paid once vs per session:* the append happens once per traverse; the sweep would be re-paid by every navigation session over the same history. (3) *State vs investigation:* "which are still open?" as recorded state is a read; as investigation it's a re-derivation. Moving work below the boundary to where it's warm, incremental, and automatable is the bootstrap's entire point — calling that "relabeling" would also indict the routelister precedent, which the objection's own benchmark accepts as a win. **KILLED.**
