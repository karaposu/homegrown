## Thin artifact

### Paper FACTs (quoted verbatim; relevance-tagged)

- **A-1 [core] Body as cognitive artifact.** Hand gestures are cognitive artifacts — Norman's def: *"designed to maintain, display, or operate upon information in order to serve a representational function"* — affording *"the special cognitive advantages claimed to be associated exclusively with enduring (e.g., printed or drawn) diagrammatic representations."* Cognitive artifacts *"not only assist in solving cognitive tasks but also change the nature of those tasks."* FACT (paper).
- **A-2 [core] Evanescent vs enduring; temporary persistence.** Gesture diagrams are *"visible and vivid during production and then, like speech, gone from the perceptual field"*; enduring diagrams are culture's *"external symbolic store"* (Donald). But gesture has *"temporary persistence"* — *"while not permanent like a printed diagram, this temporary persistence makes the gestured diagram more than evanescent"*; it persists *"as long as the explanation at hand remains the focus of attention"* then *"is erased in the progression to something new."* FACT (paper).
- **A-3 [core] THE STRIKING CLAIM — anchor not even necessary.** *"This persistent artifactual material is in fact not even necessary."* Participants are *"cognitively maintaining the diagram through time, across segments in which there is no pointer to the space, no physical realization of a referent in that space, and no referring speech."* Goldin-Meadow (comment): *"the grid isn't there… the diagram is only there because we believe it is."* FACT (paper).
- **A-4 [core] External working memory / offloading.** The virtual diagram *"functions… as an external working memory"*, enabling *"sustained reference to multiple individuals during a stretch of talk"*; *"distribution of information across these modes of representation lowers the overall load on any one of them"*; experiment (Goldin-Meadow comment): people *"remember more items when they gesture"* — gesture *"freed up cognitive resources."* FACT (paper).
- **A-5 [supporting] Three intrinsic properties.** (1) virtual reality (imagined; anchor not necessary); (2) temporal structure — *"gesture, like language, is forced to linearize when it reaches its representational limits"*; *"what comes first can be assumed to be conceptually active for some period"*; (3) bodily origo — *"a deictic zero for the I/here/now, and a grid center for spatial oppositions including high/low, left/right, and forward/back."* FACT (paper).
- **A-6 [core] Sentential vs diagrammatic (Larkin & Simon).** Sentential = *"elements appear in a single sequence"* (linear search); diagrammatic = info *"indexed by two-dimensional location,"* searched via *"the computationally easy process of change of attention to adjacent locations."* *"Spatial properties… directly preserve certain logical properties of the set of relationships"* — structure *"not directly available in the coded linguistic structure."* FACT (paper).
- **A-7 [core] Representational determinism (Zhang).** *"Different representations of a common abstract structure can cause dramatically different cognitive behaviors."* Intrinsic representations allow relations to be *"efficiently read off from the display"* without being *"inferred from symbolic descriptions"*; the diagrammatic mode *"not only affords but requires specification of values on multiple dimensions."* FACT (paper). **The sharpest genuine-import candidate.**
- **A-8 [supporting] Composite signals + distributed cognition.** Speech (symbolic) + gesture (indexical) + visual (iconic) → *"a larger whole message"*; *"made public and publicly made"*; distributed ACROSS PEOPLE (the "third arm" — a 2nd speaker completes a 1st's diagram) and across a speaker's sub-systems; *"no individual can influence the internal processing of another except by putting mediating artifactual structure in the environment of the other"* (Hutchins & Hazlehurst). FACT (paper).
- **A-9 [supporting] Recipient design / other-anticipating.** *"Participants… actively work to secure the orientation of a hearer… and design the current action… for the particularities of the current addressee"*; *"people's primary cognitive orientation is as other-anticipating interactants rather than as stand-alone information processors"*; cognition *"externally coupled"* and *"inherently social."* FACT (paper).
- **A-10 [peripheral] Editing during production; baptismal pointing.** *"Speakers employ systematic mechanisms for editing gesture diagrams during the course of production"*; "baptismal" pointing creates a node *"as if the referent were already there."* FACT (paper).
- **A-11 [supporting] Distributed-through-time.** *"Distributed through time so that the products of earlier events can transform later events"* (Hutchins). FACT (paper).
- **A-12 [peripheral for us] The kinship / Lao-kin-terminology thesis.** Ethnogenealogy; lun1 generations; sibling birth-order skewing; sibling-exchange rules; *"hand gestures reveal speakers' conceptualizations of kinship structure which are of a different nature to and not necessarily retrievable from the accompanying linguistic code."* FACT (paper) — the paper's actual domain.
- **A-13 [core for adjudication] Peer commentary — how load-bearing are the paper's OWN claims?** Goldin-Meadow (gesture eases the SPEAKER's own burden; "the grid isn't there"); Kelly (neuro integration; mutual disambiguation); **Rehbein (CRITICAL): *"it is difficult to see the theoretical point of the paper. What does Enfield want to show?"*** (theoretical thinness; thin/solitary empirical base); **Rendall (SKEPTICAL): the gestures *"appear to be largely iconic and completely redundant with the semantic content of their speech"*** — merely "tracking, reiterating, or emphasizing"; the rich diagrams "clearly… fleshed out by Enfield." Enfield's REPLY rebuts: filiation is never lateral yet the lateral axis IS exploited for sibling/collateral relations — *"No information relating to this distinction is encoded in the linguistic system."* FACT (paper).
