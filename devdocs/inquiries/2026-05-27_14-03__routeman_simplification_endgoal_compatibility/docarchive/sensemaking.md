### Ambiguity 1 — What does FULL COMPATIBILITY require?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Full compatibility requires that every architectural surface have a designed output shape that works with the committed shape. Anything undesigned = not-fully-compatible.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** This interpretation conflates "compatible inputs" with "fully designed system." The nav-session-level aggregation output is a SEPARATE design concern. The committed shape's worker-level output is fully compatible AS AN INPUT to nav session aggregation; the aggregation OUTPUT is downstream and not yet designed. Calling this "not fully compatible" forces the priors to have designed something out-of-their-scope — over-reach.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Resolution:** Full compatibility means: (a) the committed shape's outputs are PARSEABLE by all 3 integration surfaces; (b) the read-patterns each surface needs are SATISFIABLE from the committed shape's content fields; (c) no structural commitment in the priors CONTRADICTS or PREVENTS a future design at downstream layers.

**What is now fixed?** The compatibility test is INPUT-SIDE: do the worker outputs support what each surface needs?

**What is no longer allowed?** Claiming non-compatibility because downstream OUTPUT layers aren't yet designed.

### Ambiguity 2 — What does EASY EXTENSIBILITY require?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Easy extensibility means the committed shape needs ZERO modification when end-goal capabilities ship. Any field addition or restructure = not-easily-extensible.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** This interpretation assumes a frozen schema. The project's history shows iterative refinement (00-51 → 13-23 amendment within hours). Some future refinement is expected. The real test of extensibility is whether future additions are ADDITIVE (extend with new fields/sections in ways downstream consumers can ignore) vs DESTRUCTIVE (require existing downstream consumers to change). The committed shape's stable 11-field schema with ADDITIVE-friendly group structure is extensibility-positive: future fields can join a group or create new groups without breaking parsers.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Resolution:** Easy extensibility means: (a) future capabilities can be added by extending the schema additively (not restructuring); (b) downstream consumers that worked with prior schemas continue to work with extended schemas via existing parsers; (c) infrastructure-conditional revival paths (LAYER-2 audit, /intuit) have substrate in place to be activated when their prerequisites land.

**What is now fixed?** Extensibility tested by additive-friendliness, not by zero-modification.
