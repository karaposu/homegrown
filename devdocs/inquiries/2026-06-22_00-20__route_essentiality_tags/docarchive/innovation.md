## Phase 2 — Generate

### Inversion (Framer — MANDATORY, the central commitment) 🔑

**Central assumption:** "the right fix is to ADD one essentiality axis that decomposes Priority's hidden coreness."

**L1 inversion — "Don't add a field; SHARPEN Priority to mean coreness."** (component-level)
- *Steelman:* Priority already exists. Redefine "Priority = can-the-goal-land-without-it" — zero new fields, zero bloat, and the user's "can't tell which are essential" is solved by making the existing field mean essentiality. The lean-index just shed columns; adding one is the wrong direction.
- *Why it fails (structural + measured):* Priority is assigned for **heterogeneous real reasons** authors genuinely want to express — R4 is MED because it's "light build" (effort/risk), R10 is MED because it activates "the moment real money flows" (phase). Forcibly redefining Priority to pure coreness doesn't delete those signals — it **evicts them with nowhere to go**, so they get crammed back into Priority anyway (re-creating the overload) or vanish (information loss). You cannot resolve an overload by renaming one of its fused components; the others still need a home. And 153 existing routes were rated under the current meaning — a silent redefinition reinterprets all of them. **So sharpen-Priority doesn't remove the need to decompose; it hides it.** → Inversion REJECTED.
- *Refinement it yields (kept):* the decomposition must **explicitly state what Priority RETAINS** (pressingness/salience) so the evicted signals keep a home. This becomes the "loudness vs load-bearing" framing below.

**L2 inversion — depth-check to system-level: "Maybe the map shouldn't carry an importance SCALAR at all — only describe each route's goal-relation."**
- *System-level reframe:* routelister's identity is to **describe each route's relation to the goal, never to rank the field** (§1.3). "Can the goal land without this route?" is the **purest goal-relation descriptor** — arguably MORE identity-aligned than Priority, which smuggles in "salience"/attention (a half-disposition). 
- *Fertile result:* essentiality isn't merely an *addition* — it is **the goal-relation attribute routelister should have led with**; Priority is the incremental-evolution proxy that blended coreness with urgency. This **strengthens** the verdict: the user has identified the field the schema was missing.
- *Existence-axis check:* could the importance-axis count be ZERO? No — authors demonstrably need *some* goal-relation signal (every WHY supplies one). *Identity-axis check:* the importance signal fundamentally consists of **coreness (goal-dependence) + pressingness (urgency/phase) + salience (attention)** — three things one Priority scalar fused. The decomposition names coreness and lets Priority keep the rest.

### Absence Recognition (Generator — both levels)
- **Patch-level:** missing a clean "is the goal load-bearing on this route?" attribute (confirmed-absent in surfacing — lives only in WHY prose).
- **Redesign-level (from scratch):** if routelister were designed from scratch around "describe each route's goal-relation," the **first** attribute would be coreness, not salience. Priority-as-salience is the incremental artifact.
- **Redesign-level (already-present-in-different-form):** essentiality is **already computed, in narrative form** — every WHY says "load-bearing human-in-the-loop" (R6), "off the money-loop critical path" (R13), "biggest leverage point" (R3). **The field formalizes a hand-curated practice the project already runs — it does not invent a new judgment.** (The honest framing: lift the latent, don't add the novel.)

### Domain Transfer (Generator — native + different)
- **Native (project management): the CRITICAL PATH.** A task is on the critical path if the project's completion depends on it; off-path tasks have slack. **core = on the critical path to the goal · supporting = has slack · peripheral = off the network.** The phase-qualifier = *which phase's* critical path. This is the cleanest source — it gives the exact vocabulary and the "can the goal land without it?" test.
- **Native (software): MoSCoW (Must/Should/Could/Won't).** "Must" ≈ core, "Could" ≈ peripheral — BUT MoSCoW is a **ship-DECISION** method (a disposition). The transfer's lesson: **take the coreness distinction, leave the ship-decision behind** — exactly the Selection-creep line. Confirms attributive-only.
- **Different field (medicine/architecture): vital-organ triage / load-bearing wall.** core = the structure collapses without it. Reinforces the universal test.

## Crystallizing Output

### The NAME: **Loudness vs Load-bearing**

> **Priority measures how LOUD a route is** (how much it stands out — salient, pressing, big, risky). **Essentiality measures how LOAD-BEARING it is** (how much the goal *rests on it* — can the goal land without it?). **The user's pain is that the map only showed loudness, and they were trying to read load-bearing off it.** R4 is the proof: *quiet* (light build → Priority MED) but *load-bearing* (no money loop without it). One scalar can't say both; so say both.

The principle: **the route-map already computes load-bearing — in every WHY ("off the critical path," "the load-bearing human-in-the-loop") — but only displays loudness. Lift the latent load-bearing judgment into one attributive token.**

### (c) The glance-rubric (crisp, domain-agnostic, one question)

> **Essentiality** — one glance, one question: **"Can the goal land without this route?"**
> - **core** — *no.* The goal fails or is incomplete without it. (It's on the critical path.)
> - **supporting** — *yes, but weaker.* It helps; the goal survives without it. (It has slack.)
> - **peripheral** — *yes.* Off the critical path — optional, exploratory, nice-to-have.
>
> Optional, on **core** routes only: a **`@<goal-phase>`** qualifier when coreness activates only in a later goal-phase (e.g., `core · @real-money`). Default (no qualifier) = this phase. *Never a "do it now" — it describes which phase the goal rests on the route, not when to act.*

### (a) The axis in a real record — R4, the smoking gun (essentiality decoupled from Priority)

```
### R4 — Funding / escrow-hold
- tags: core · goal-advancing · DEVELOP
- Move:  route the Launcher budget into a reversible ESCROW_HOLD through the settlement spine.
- Lands: money is held and reversible — ready for R3 to release.
- Priority: MED · Confidence: HIGH · Essentiality: core
- Why:   the money loop cannot land without a hold to settle from — a LIGHT build, but load-bearing.
```
**The win:** `Priority: MED` (it's light, not urgent) sits beside `Essentiality: core` (the goal needs it) — the two now read **independently**, dissolving the exact "idk if it's essential" confusion. Reading Priority alone, R4 looks skippable; the essentiality token says it isn't.

### (b) The phase-qualifier in a real record — R10 (essential-but-later)

```
### R10 — The dispute path
- Priority: MED · Confidence: MED · Essentiality: core · @real-money
- Why:  absent today; the goal can't land at the real-money phase without it —
        core, but its coreness ACTIVATES at that phase (not the pilot).
```
**The win:** `core` (the goal genuinely rests on it) + `@real-money` (not this phase) — "essential but later," with no disposition.

| **essential** | `core · @<later-phase>` | the qualifier marks essential-but-not-now (R10) |
