## Finding Summary

- **Your instinct is the design's own law — that's why superseded material is the view's CHEAPEST class.** Full superseded texts never enter a view: the size policy sends them to the one-line tier FIRST, and the synthesis traverse reads the assembled view, never raw folders. Each dead end costs one tagged line.

- **The honest cost, corrected under critique:** ~32% of the corpus is superseded (128 of 403 findings — a third of any topic's story is its dead ends). At rendered width the original "7%" was under-sold: tag-first lines wrap, so the worst case is **~10–15% of a view** — brought down by adopting the **short tag** (`[superseded → <folder-basename>]` instead of the full path).

- **Why keep even those lines — the asymmetry that decides:** with the trail, the risk is mild anchoring on a dead framing — bounded to one tagged line, and the disqualifying evidence sits in the critique step's own input. Without the trail, the risk is **re-deriving a killed design and presenting it as new** — a whole loop's cost, and structurally invisible, because nothing in the run can test its "fresh" idea against material that isn't there. **Bounded-and-evidenced versus unbounded-and-invisible.**

- **The one-day-old proof (mechanism stated honestly):** the paradigm-sweeper's design turned on re-testing a same-day kill — possible only because the kill was VISIBLE to that inquiry. This session had that visibility for free through warm context; **the view is the artifact designed to give COLD sessions the same visibility.**

- **The boundary your question was feeling for: seeds are trail-free; territories are trail-full.** A generation-dive should not be biased by the graveyard — and it isn't: the seed-block is trail-free by design (divergence-pinning, forward-looking). The trail belongs only where history is the subject: synthesis territories and consolidations.

- **Three one-line refinements land with the build:** superseded one-liners live under their own neutral heading (**"The closed trail (superseded — see supersessors)"**); each line LEADS with its tag; and the **register rule** is stated once — view-text is functional (labels, pointers, how-to-refresh), persuasive phrasing stays in findings (the colorful "kept so they stay dead" teaches humans here, not machines there).

- **The current-only dial: not needed.** The view's head (the newest or consolidation summary) already IS the pure-current read; the closed-trail heading makes the separation visible. Dropping the trail would save ~10% of lines by deleting the vaccine.

## Finding

### 1. What actually enters a view (the premise, dissolved)

A view is built by the size policy's cut-order, and superseded findings are its FIRST compression target: they render as one tagged line each — title, date, one bullet, pointer — never as full text. And the synthesis traverse's territory is the assembled view, not the inquiry folders. So the thing the question worries about (deprecated inquiries eating context in the traverse run) does not exist in the shipped design; what exists is a short, tagged index of the dead ends. Measured scale: about a third of the corpus is superseded, so the densest concept (routelister, 67 hits) would carry ~21 such lines — roughly 10–15% of a ≤300-line view at honest rendered width, with the short-tag form adopted to keep it near the low end.

### 2. Why those lines earn their place (the asymmetry)

Weigh the two ways a synthesis run can fail. **With the trail:** the run might lean slightly toward a dead framing. That risk is bounded — one line per dead end, led by its disqualifier — and the evidence against the dead claim travels INTO the run's own critique step (tags don't vanish from an anchored run's input; what the trail guarantees is that the catching material is present). **Without the trail:** the run can re-derive a killed design and call it new. That failure costs a whole loop, and it is invisible — no step can test a "fresh" idea against absent material, and a consolidation that cannot see what it supersedes is GUARANTEED to silently absorb commitments it never re-tested (the exact failure the house's re-test mandate exists to catch). Bounded-and-evidenced versus unbounded-and-invisible: that asymmetry is the whole answer.

The proof is one day old. The paradigm-sweeper's central design move was re-testing a same-day kill ("a tenth discipline would duplicate it") — which produced *confirmed-but-frame-revised* instead of a naive re-invention. That re-test was possible only because the kill was visible in the inquiry's territory. This session had such visibility for free, through warm context; the view exists to give cold sessions the same.

### 3. What the lines buy, graded

1. **The relitigation vaccine (load-bearing).** Dead ends that stay visible stay dead. The house already runs on this — the north star lists its killed frames "so they stay dead"; this week's own route-maps exclude routes as "relitigation." A synthesis blind to the graveyard becomes its next resident.
2. **The silent-absorption catch.** A consolidation must re-test — or explicitly inherit, with reasons — the commitments of everything it supersedes. It needs the LIST, at exactly the view's grain: one line and a pointer each. (The counter — "re-test from the supersessor's own text" — partially works and is credited: the supersessor states what it changed; it cannot state what it silently absorbed, which is the failure the mandate targets.)
3. **The WHY's index.** The current design is unintelligible without its dead ends: "log choices, not outcomes" only means something against the rejected logbook. The one-liners index the WHY; the pointers carry the depth.

### 4. Where the trail does NOT belong

Your worry is exactly right for one place: **generation**. A dive exploring family F should not be biased by the graveyard — and it isn't, because the seed-block is trail-free by design: it pins the family's commitments and points forward. The rule in one line: **seeds are trail-free; territories are trail-full.** Clean inputs for generating; full history for synthesizing.

### 5. The three refinements (landing with the build)

1. **The closed-trail subsection** — superseded one-liners live under their own heading in the view's history region: `## The closed trail (superseded — see supersessors)`.
2. **Tag-first, short-tag lines** — `[superseded → <folder-basename>] title · date · first bullet · pointer` (the disqualifier read first; the basename keeps the line short; the full path lives in the pointer).
3. **The register rule, stated once in the protocol** — view-text is functional (labels, pointers, "regenerate for current"); persuasive or evaluative phrasing stays in findings. (Found when the colorful heading was prosecuted as a possible steering-instruction to machine readers.)

Its kernel survived twice: the mitigation stack (tag-first; the closed-trail section) and the seeds/territories boundary (generation DOES get trail-free inputs — via seed-blocks, already shipped).

**Kills:** the current-only dial (the view's head already serves it); trail-stripping (the asymmetry); the colorful heading in the artifact (the register rule — persuasion stays finding-side); the 7% claim as-worded (corrected, not defended).
