**Strongest counter-interpretation:** the point-type mismatch — thinking-space's points are QUESTIONS; approach-space's points are WAYS/answers; different point-types cannot chart each other; the whole bridge is decorative.
**Why the counter partially STANDS and improves the model (structural grounds):** the counter is right against "chart" — a chart re-coordinatizes a region of the same space, and these are different spaces of different point-types. But it does not sever the relation; it forces the correct one: **a practice-shaped question is a point of the territory, and engaging it OPENS INTO its approach-space** — the approach-space is the question-point's internal structure at answer grain (fiber-like: base = questions; over each practice-question, its space of ways). "Chart" survives re-homed: **the paradigm map is a chart of the approach-space** (that was always its correct referent).
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Fixed:** sweep's dual-level nature. **No longer allowed:** "sweep is not a movement" (too strong) and "sweep is just another position-move" (too weak). **Model change:** the categories gain a clean example of why level-tagging matters.

**The definition (the asked-for relation):**

> **Thinking-space is the territory of questions an inquiring system walks. Each practice-shaped question opens into its own approach-space, whose named attractors are paradigms. Paradigms are thereby the REFERENCE FRAME of thinking-space movement: every move is typed by what it changes relative to the current paradigm chart —**
> **· in-place production** (new instances within one paradigm; nothing about the position changes — the innovation mechanisms mostly operate here),

> **· a family jump** (several commitments change; you land at another attractor),
> **· chart acquisition — the paradigm-sweep** (a cartographic act: no position changes; the map of the ways comes into existence),
> **· chart replacement — the paradigm shift** (the axes and criteria themselves are replaced; positions under the old chart no longer translate — incommensurability, in movement terms).
