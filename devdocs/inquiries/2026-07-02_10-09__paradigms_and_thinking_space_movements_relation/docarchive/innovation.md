### Q2 — The definition, final wording (principal)

> **Paradigms are what thinking-space movements are measured against.** Thinking-space is the territory of questions an inquiring system walks. Each practice-shaped question opens into its own approach-space, whose named attractors are paradigms — and that structure is the movement system's **reference frame**: every move is typed by what it changes relative to the current paradigm chart. **Within the chart:** *in-place production* (new instances inside one paradigm — the innovation mechanisms mostly power this), *an axis step* (one commitment changes), *a family jump* (several commitments change; you land at another attractor). **On the chart:** *chart acquisition* — the paradigm-sweep (nothing about your position changes; the map of the ways comes into existence) — and *chart replacement* — the paradigm shift (the axes and success-criteria themselves are replaced; positions under the old chart no longer translate, which is what incommensurability means in movement terms). **Around the steps:** routelister's engagement verbs are the typed steps of the base walk (question to question); the seven loop-control moves are not steps but steering decisions about the walk; MTTP patterns are composed itineraries of many steps; and the thinking-space primitives are the walker's anatomy, executing every act at every level. **This relation types movements; it never scores traversals** — the walk's quality stays the canon's deliberately-open question.

**Inversion (mandated: "'reference frame' is physics cosplay — say 'paradigms are the biggest moves'"):** the plain alternative is FALSE under the settled model — paradigms are not moves at all (that is exactly the user's "paradigms are top" confusion the model repairs: the top MOVES are sweep and shift, which act ON the paradigm structure). And "reference frame" does non-decorative work: it says movements are TYPED BY what they change relative to the chart. The cosplay charge wins one concession: **lead with the plain gloss** ("what movements are measured against"), then introduce the frame term. **Rejected with the lead-order refinement.** *(5-test: survives.)*

### Q5 — Sweep and shift natures (principal)

Sweep: **a real step of the base walk whose product is cartography of an approach-space** — within the space it maps it changes nothing (enumerate-not-select IS fiber-position-preservation by design). Shift: **chart replacement** — after it, "where you were" has no address in the new chart (incommensurability, stated as positions-not-translating; the success-criterion moves with the chart, which is why post-shift comparisons feel impossible).

**Inversion (nature-commit reversed: "shift is just the biggest family jump"):** a family jump lands at another attractor IN THE SAME chart (the old position still has an address; distance is measurable). A shift replaces the chart — the old position isn't far away, it's UNADDRESSABLE. Different in kind, not size; collapsing them would lose exactly the incommensurability phenomenon Kuhn named. **Rejected.** *(Survives.)*

### Q6 — The cube, told as a story (content-production; mandated inversion)

> You hold the cube and work one face — slice moves, vertical and horizontal. That's movement INSIDE a system: every move you consider is one your current method makes legal. *(Your slice-move picture was right — that IS what within-paradigm movement feels like.)*
> Now you turn the whole cube in your hands to "see it differently." It feels like a perspective change — but nothing about the cube's state changed. That's a SYMMETRY: re-viewing, not shifting. *(Your "not sure if this is accurate" pointed at exactly this joint — a lens move masquerading as a shift.)*
> The real paradigm shift on a Rubik's cube is switching your **solving method**: from beginner layer-by-layer to CFOP or Roux. Same cube, same physical territory — but a different commitment-bundle: different decomposition of the solve, different move-vocabulary, different intermediate goals, even a different definition of "good" (fewest moves vs fastest fingers vs ergonomics). Mid-solve, your layer-by-layer "progress" doesn't even TRANSLATE into Roux terms — that untranslatability is incommensurability, in your hands. And learning that CFOP and Roux exist at all — reading the map of methods before committing to one — is the paradigm-sweep.

**Inversion (mandated: "drop the cube — repaired analogies confuse"):** the repair IS the lesson — the symmetry-limit teaches lens≠shift better than prose could, and the user's own doubt located it (keeping their authorship of the correction). External-fact check: layer-by-layer/CFOP/Roux/ZZ are real methods with real communities and real criterion differences — verifiable, low-risk. **Rejected; the story keeps the user's image with the one repair.** *(Survives.)*
