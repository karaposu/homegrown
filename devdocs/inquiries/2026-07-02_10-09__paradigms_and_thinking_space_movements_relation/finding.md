## Finding Summary

- **The definition:** **paradigms are what thinking-space movements are measured against — within a practice-question's approach-space.** Thinking-space (the canon's territory of questions) contains practice-shaped questions; each opens into its own approach-space, whose named attractors are paradigms; and that structure is the movement system's **reference frame**: every move inside an approach-space is typed by what it changes relative to the current paradigm chart. The frame then LOCATES every other movement kind around that core.

- **The step-ladder (inside an approach-space), ordered by what changes:** **in-place production** (new instances within one paradigm; zero coordinates change — the innovation mechanisms mostly power this rung) → **axis step** (one commitment changes) → **family jump** (several commitments change; you land at another attractor). The user's "downwards less dimensional" is exactly right here: many → one → zero coordinates.

- **The cartographic triple (acts ON the chart, not in it):** **chart acquisition** — the paradigm-sweep (your position doesn't change; the map of the ways comes into existence); **chart extension** — a new axis or family joins the live chart, old positions keep their addresses (discovered during this inquiry's adversarial pass — e.g., Domain Transfer importing a dimension the chart lacked); **chart replacement** — the paradigm shift (the axes and success-criteria themselves are replaced; positions under the old chart no longer translate — that untranslatability IS incommensurability, in movement terms).

- **The five-category Movement Frame** ("one theory to merge them all," delivered as one frame rather than one line): **anatomy** (the eleven thinking-space primitives — the walker's organs, executing every act) · **steps** (the ladder above; plus routelister's engagement verbs as the typed steps of the BASE walk, question to question) · **cartographic acts** (the triple) · **steering decisions** (the seven loop-control moves — decisions ABOUT the walk, not steps of it) · **itineraries** (the MTTP patterns — composed multi-loop journeys). Sorting rule for edge cases: *what is the act's direct object (a concept / a position / a chart / the walk)? and is it a decision or a step?*

- **The user's intuitions, scored:** slice-moves-as-paradigm-work — **right**. "Less dimensional downwards" — **right** for the locomotive rungs. "Paradigm shift = completely changing your perspective" — **repaired at exactly the joint their doubt pointed at**: merely re-orienting the cube is a SYMMETRY (what changes, if anything, is your *noticing* — a walker-level act, like the innovation discipline's Lens Shifting); the real shift is **switching solving methods** (layer-by-layer → CFOP → Roux): same cube, different commitment-bundle, different definition of "good." "Paradigms are top" — repaired to: **paradigms are the FRAME; the top moves are the ones that act ON the frame.**

- **The arc closes on the real case:** the naming failure that started this chain is, in frame vocabulary, **hours of rung-1 iteration (in-place production inside the default paradigm) where a rung-3 family jump was needed** — and the sweep exists to make rung-3 moves cheap: you cannot jump to a family you have never seen on a map. This diagnosis is falsifiable (it inherits the naming case's designated test).

- **The compliance rule:** **this theory types steps; it never scores walks.** The traversal-quality question stays the canon's deliberately-open one. One legal bridge, discovered when the strongest counter partially won: the canon itself PLANS "operational placeholder v1" signals — so the rung vocabulary is **offered** (raw material, no weights, no formulas) to the future `devdocs/spec/meaningful_traversal.md` when it is drafted.

- **Four space-words, one table:** representation space (what the cognitive primitives operate over, within one act) · thinking-space (the territory of questions) · approach-space (what a practice-question opens into) · answer-space (what a configuration-question opens into — the Horizontal Dive pattern's object). The last two are siblings — two question-shapes, two opened-into structures — which is also why Paradigm-Sweep and Horizontal Dive are sibling patterns with disjoint gates.

## Finding

### 1. Why this inquiry exists

Two findings this morning settled what a paradigm IS (a coherent, generative bundle of commitments relative to a practice) and how to enumerate a practice's paradigms (the meta-facet kit and probes). The user then asked the unifying question: how does all of that relate to **movement** in thinking-space — the canon concept from `docs/canon/what_is_meaningful_traversal.md` — given that the innovation discipline's mechanisms are already "known moves," and offering a Rubik's-cube intuition with an honest "not sure if this is accurate." This run read both canon documents at source for the first time in the chain (`docs/canon/thinking_space_dynamics.md` and `docs/canon/what_is_meaningful_traversal.md`) and built the relation from there.

### 2. Getting the spaces straight (the relata)

The canon itself uses "thinking space" at two different grains. `thinking_space_dynamics.md` describes the **within-act apparatus**: eleven typed cognitive primitives (attention-pointer, working memory, intuition-similarity, and so on) operating over a representation space — the machinery of a single cognitive act. `what_is_meaningful_traversal.md` describes the **cross-inquiry territory**: "thinking space as the territory the loops traverse," with a topology of question-clusters, regions, and dead zones — its points are QUESTIONS. Add the paradigm findings' per-practice **approach-space** (points are WAYS of doing) and the user's question involves three different objects.

Their relation (corrected under adversarial pressure — the first candidate, "approach-spaces are local charts of thinking-space," died on a point-type mismatch, since a chart re-coordinatizes the SAME space and these spaces have different point-types):

> **Thinking-space is the territory of questions. Each practice-shaped question OPENS INTO its own approach-space** (the space of ways to do that practice, whose named attractors are paradigms). **The paradigm map is a chart OF that approach-space** — "chart" re-homed to its correct referent. **The thinking-space primitives are the walker's anatomy** — organs that execute every act at every level, not a place at all.

The general principle behind "opens into": **every question opens into its own answer-structure**. Practice-questions open into approach-spaces; configuration-questions open into answer-spaces (the object of the Horizontal Dive + Vertical Refinement + Synthesis pattern); a bare fact-question opens into a thin verification-structure. And an approach-space exists as soon as the practice admits genuine choice — a brand-new practice's approach-space is simply **attractor-sparse** (few or no named families yet; paradigm formation is a community's slow sedimentation inside a space that pre-exists the sediment — which is exactly what the frontier entry-type on paradigm maps describes).

| Space-word | What it is | Its points | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| representation space | what the primitives operate over, within one cognitive act | representations | `thinking_space_dynamics.md` |
| **thinking-space** | the territory the loops traverse | questions (clusters, regions, dead zones) | `what_is_meaningful_traversal.md` |
| **approach-space** | what a practice-question opens into | ways of doing (families / attractors = paradigms) | the paradigm findings |
| answer-space | what a configuration-question opens into | positions along answer-axes | the Horizontal Dive pattern |

### 3. The definition

> **Paradigms are what thinking-space movements are measured against — within a practice-question's approach-space.** Every move there is typed by what it changes relative to the current paradigm chart:
> **Within the chart —** *in-place production*: new instances inside one paradigm; nothing about the position changes (the innovation mechanisms mostly operate here). *An axis step*: one commitment changes. *A family jump*: several commitments change together; you land at another attractor.
> **On the chart —** *chart acquisition* (the **paradigm-sweep**): no position changes; the map of the ways comes into existence. *Chart extension*: a new axis or family joins the live chart; old positions keep their addresses. *Chart replacement* (the **paradigm shift**): the axes and success-criteria themselves are replaced; positions under the old chart no longer translate — which is what incommensurability means in movement terms.
> **Around the steps —** routelister's engagement verbs are the typed steps of the BASE walk (question to question); the seven loop-control moves are not steps but STEERING decisions about the walk; the MTTP patterns are composed ITINERARIES of many steps; and the thinking-space primitives are the WALKER'S ANATOMY, executing every act at every level.
> **This relation types movements; it never scores traversals** — the walk's quality remains the canon's deliberately-open question.

Two notes on the wording. The scope clause ("within a practice-question's approach-space") is load-bearing: base-walk steps are typed by their engagement verbs, not by paradigm charts — the earlier draft's "every move" overclaimed and was corrected. And the definition is not circular: paradigms are grounded independently of movement (the membership test — commitment-bundle + generative capacity — from the prior finding), so typing moves against them bottoms out.

### 4. The Movement Frame (the "one theory," as one frame)

| Category | What it is | Members | Why not a "step" |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anatomy** | the walker's organs | the 11 thinking-space primitives | organs execute moves; they aren't moves |
| **Steps** | position acts in a space | the 3-rung ladder (approach-space); engagement verbs (base walk) | — these ARE the steps |
| **Cartographic acts** | acts on a space's CHART | sweep (acquire) · extension · shift (replace) | they change map or basis, not position |
| **Steering decisions** | decisions ABOUT the walk | the 7 loop-control moves | decided, not stepped (the loop-control finding's decide-vs-enumerate split) |
| **Itineraries** | composed step-sequences | the MTTP patterns | many steps with a shape |

**The sorter** (for edge cases, with the two prosecuted examples): *what is the act's direct object, and is it a decision or a step?* REFRAME (an engagement verb) acts on ONE CONCEPT's framing — a base-level step, not a chart-act (downstream cascades don't reclassify the act). DIFFERENT-APPROACH (a control move) is the boundary DECISION to make a family jump — the jump itself is the step that follows. The frame's lines hold because each was paid for by a prior adjudication.

**Placements worth naming:** the innovation mechanisms MOSTLY power rungs 1–3 (transforms on a seed within or between families) — but **Domain Transfer and redesign-level Absence Recognition can act cartographically**, importing or exposing an axis the chart lacked (chart extension). Lens Shifting lives at the walker level: a re-viewing act that can change your noticing without moving anything — see the cube's re-orientation below.

### 5. The Rubik's cube, repaired (the teaching story)

You hold the cube and work one face — slice moves, vertical and horizontal. That is movement INSIDE a system: every move you consider is one your current method makes legal. *Your slice-move picture was right — that is what within-paradigm movement feels like.*

Now you turn the whole cube in your hands to "see it differently." It feels like a perspective change — but nothing about the cube's state changed. That is a SYMMETRY. What changed, if anything, was your **noticing** — a walker-level act (attention, salience), the same family as Lens Shifting. *Your "not sure if this is accurate" pointed at exactly this joint: re-viewing is not shifting.*

The real paradigm shift on a Rubik's cube is switching your **solving method** — from beginner layer-by-layer to CFOP or Roux. Same cube, same physical territory, but a different commitment-bundle: a different decomposition of the solve, a different move-vocabulary, different intermediate goals, and a different definition of "good" (fewest moves vs fastest execution vs ergonomics). Mid-solve, your layer-by-layer "progress" doesn't even translate into Roux terms — incommensurability, in your hands. And learning that CFOP and Roux *exist at all* — reading the map of methods before committing — is the paradigm-sweep. (The methods pass the paradigm membership test cleanly: bundles, generative, exemplar-anchored, community-named, plural and composable.)

### 6. The diagnosis idiom, on the real case

In frame vocabulary, the AI-naming failure that started this chain reads: **hours of rung-1 iteration** — in-place production inside the default naming paradigm — **where the situation needed a rung-3 family jump** (to the translingual-metaphor family), which only the human eventually made. The sweep exists precisely to make rung-3 moves cheap and deliberate: you cannot jump to a family you have never seen on a map. This is the canon's "stuck-in-a-rut" failure mode translated into step vocabulary — a diagnosis, not a score. It is falsifiable: give the generator the map (make jumps cheap) and outcomes on comparable tasks should change — exactly what the chain's designated first instance tests.

### 7. The compliance line (and the one legal bridge)

The canon deliberately declines to formalize traversal quality ("not a commitment to any specific formula"; premature formalization named as a failure mode). The rule this theory lives by: **type steps, never score walks** — no rung-weights, no stuck-detector formulas, no signal combinations. The strongest counter to this rule partially WON during adversarial testing: the canon's own roadmap *plans* "operational placeholders v1" for its signals. The resolution keeps the boundary and opens the one legal door: the rung vocabulary is **offered as raw material** to the future `devdocs/spec/meaningful_traversal.md` (for example, consecutive-same-rung-iterations as a coverage-adjacent placeholder candidate) — the offering names no weights, no thresholds, no combinations; that spec does its own adjudication when it is drafted.

### 8. What was deliberately NOT concluded

- **"Paradigms are the biggest moves"** — killed: paradigms are not moves at all; they are what moves are measured against (this was the precise repair of "paradigms are top").
- **One linear ladder over everything** — killed: steering, itineraries, and anatomy are category-different from steps; a single line would re-merge distinctions prior findings paid for.
- **"Approach-spaces are local charts of thinking-space"** — killed by the point-type mismatch (its kernel survives as opens-into, with "chart" re-homed to the paradigm map).
- **"Shift is just the biggest family jump"** — killed: a jump lands at another attractor in the SAME chart (the old position keeps an address); a shift replaces the chart (the old position becomes unaddressable). Different in kind, not size.
- **A rung-based stuck-detector, here** — excluded on the compliance rule; its legal descendant is the offering.
- **Dropping the cube** — rejected: the repair IS the lesson, and the user's own doubt authored it.

## Reasoning

**Why "reference frame" carries the definition.** The alternative ("paradigms are the biggest moves") is false under the settled model — and its falseness is the exact repair of the user's "paradigms are top": the top MOVES (sweep, extension, shift) are the ones that act ON the paradigm structure; the structure itself is what all approach-space moves are typed against. The physics-cosplay charge won one concession: the plain gloss leads ("what movements are measured against") and the frame term follows.

**Why the frame has five categories, not one line and not two buckets.** Each non-step category exists by a prior, separately-paid-for adjudication: steering was split from enumeration by the loop-control finding; itineraries have their own open catalog (MTTP); anatomy has its own canon document. A taxonomy that un-splits adjudicated distinctions is amnesia, not parsimony. The frame's lines survived two concrete straddle-prosecutions (REFRAME; DIFFERENT-APPROACH) via the two-question sorter.

**Why chart extension had to be added.** The prosecution asked where Domain Transfer's new-axis imports live, and neither existing cartographic act fits: not acquisition (the chart existed), not replacement (old positions still translate — no incommensurability). Extension slots between jump and replacement exactly where the address-preservation criterion says it must — and the paradigm map was ALREADY committed as "challengeable and extendable"; the theory had simply not named extension as a movement kind. The pass's best catch.

**Why the cube stays.** The repair is the lesson: the symmetry-limit teaches lens≠shift better than prose, the noticing-form locates walker-level acts (and Lens Shifting with them), and the user's own doubt authored the correction — the analogy is kept with their authorship intact.

**Significant kills** are in Finding §8. The one the reader should not miss: "shift is just the biggest family jump" — collapsing kind into size would have lost incommensurability, the phenomenon that makes shifts consequential.
