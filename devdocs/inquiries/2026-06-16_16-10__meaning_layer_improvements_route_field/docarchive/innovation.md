### Framers

**Lens Shifting.** Under the **"a new field"** lens → schema growth. Under the **"promote the existing depth-signal"** lens → not really new. Under a **third lens — "a per-route Definition-of-Ready"** → the whole thing reframes: the develop-vs-deepen choice is a *readiness gate*, and the field is the *readiness checklist*. (The crispest name.)

**Constraint Manipulation** (both directions).
- *ADD* "the field must never imply ordering" → forces a **pure perception** (a meaning-gap map + vitality); the meta-loop supplies all ordering. Keeps it identity-clean.
- *REMOVE* "low default confidence" → if the list were *authoritative*, it'd be a **hard** DoR (a contract you MUST satisfy) = a gate = a *decision* → violates enumerate-don't-decide. So the **first-pass / low-confidence framing is load-bearing**: it keeps the field a **prompt, not a gate** — a *soft* DoR.

**Inversion** (challenging "it's a good within-concept addition"; depth-iterated).
- *L1 (component):* "the field is a within-concept addition" → invert → "it's an **inter-concept dependency / over-engineering in disguise**." Grain of truth: if a sub-concept later becomes its OWN DEEPEN route, the field's item now references a separate route → edging toward inter-concept.
- *L2 (system-level + existence-axis):* the inversion and the thesis resolve via the depth-signal precedent — the field is within-concept **as long as its items describe the target's own sub-parts, not pointers to separate route-identities.** The moment a sub-concept becomes its own route (after you engage it), the cross-route relationship is captured by the **existing re-run loop** (routelog ↗ + re-run, the prior finding's mechanism), NOT by the field encoding a dependency. **Existence-axis: the number of inter-concept edges the field encodes must be ZERO** — it lists sub-parts, never edges. So the contrarian collapses into a *design rule*: the field lists the target's own gaps (within-concept, zero edges); cross-route follow-through is the re-run loop's job, not the field's.
