## The Six Pieces (finding-ready)

### (1) The corrections-frame

> Your note lands on the grounding's §1 in three places, and the record should be fair in both directions. On "the maps burn every night": the finding's own parenthetical already said *"the archive stays — storage remembers everything; it is the READING that must be shaped like the traversal"* — so the fact was never wrong, but the vivid line overclaims, and you are right that it misleads: the artifacts (the traverse md files) persist; what is redone each morning is the *assembled reading* of them. Second, your sentence — **"traversing the thinking space is for uncovering a certain shape in solution space that fits the problem shape in particular way and conditions"** — is recorded as your working position on the points-vs-shape seed (the traversal-ontology dive stays open; this feeds it). Third, two annotations now ride the four requirements: R-DEFINE ("name what you've mapped") carries your doubt — *not sure if required*; and R-HOLO's working form is corrected from whole-map-in-view to a **rolling horizon** — recent parts plus next places to go. The requirements stand; their map-clothing is what this inquiry replaces.

### (2) The pair — the sonographer and the potter

> **The sonographer (the read half).** You have seen this scene: the ultrasound room, the probe on skin, the grainy screen. The organ is real and it is there — but dark; she sees only the current slice on the screen, one step's visibility at a time. So she moves the probe *by what just appeared* — an edge suggests itself, she angles toward it. No single pass shows the form; **the form exists only across many passes from different angles**, assembled. And here is the part that matches your correction exactly: **the slices are saved — the assembly is not.** The images persist on the machine (the md files); the three-dimensional understanding she built while scanning must be rebuilt by whoever reads the study tomorrow. She scans because a physician asked a question; the scan is done when the question is answered — not when the whole body is imaged.
>
> | counterpart | in the scene |
> |---|---|
> | problem space | the clinical question the scan must answer |
> | thinking space | the space of probe-angles and passes |
> | solution space | the anatomy — the shape that is there to uncover |
> | traversal memory | the saved slices (persist) vs the assembled reconstruction (rebuilt each reading) |
> | one traverse | one angled pass |
> | meaningful vs chaotic | the systematic sweep guided by what appeared vs waving the probe at random |
>
> *Break-points, aloud:* the shape pre-exists and does not grow when scanned; nothing is built — reading only.
>
> **The potter (the write half).** Hands on clay, no blueprint — a commission: it must hold water, fit the kiln, sit well in two hands. *The use-constraints are the problem shape, and the vessel must fit them in a particular way, under conditions.* Each pass of the hands is one traverse: the wall rises, the form answers back, and what the clay just did tells her where the next pressure goes. Nothing is ever final — **the clay stays workable**: a wall can be thinned, a curve reopened, the whole thing collapsed and rethrown (supersession, in material form). And between sessions the clay dries — left alone it stiffens and blurs her last intentions — so she rewraps it wet and, next morning, rewets and re-centers before working on (the warm-up, in material form). Chaos here has a feel: off-center wobble, where every pass makes it worse.
>
> | counterpart | in the scene |
> |---|---|
> | problem space | the use-constraints the vessel must fit (hold water, fit the kiln, serve the commission) |
> | thinking space | the working of the clay — where hands can go next |
> | solution space | the vessel-forms latent in this clay |

>
> *Break-points, aloud:* the potter roughly knows what she is making (discovery weakened — the commission bounds it); the clay does not grow new clay.
>
> **The pairing.** These are the two halves of one practice — the read half and the write half — and the project does both every day: reads like the sonographer at morning, works like the potter till night. *The slices are hers to keep; the reconstruction she must build again each morning — and the clay remembers only what her hands last left it.* One preserved discreteness, in both: one pass, one move — the point-reading's true part living inside the shape-reading.

### (3) Knitting — yours, honored

> Your candidate, and it earned its place: **each stitch is one traverse** — the most discrete, countable move of any scene here. **Frogging is supersession in yarn**: rows unraveled on purpose so the shape can be re-made — the reopen-move no rigid material allows. The cloth's bend when you hold it up — proportions lost until it's laid flat and smoothed — is not the flaw you suspected: it is the **between-session blur**, embodied; laying it flat IS the warm-up. And the hat must fit a head, in weather — the fit-clause, homely and real. The honest flaw is different from the one you named: real knitting usually **follows a pattern** — the maker knows the target from row one, which weakens the very thing you asked for (a shape unknown until you move). Freeform knitting exists, but an analogy that needs a niche variant has stumbled. Third place, with its two donated mechanisms credited.

### (4) The donors — four short scenes, one property each

> **Bonsai** — the only scene where the terrain grows when you walk it: pruning spawns growth; the form is negotiated with a living thing over seasons, working recent growth plus intention, never a whole-plan. **Echolocation** — the memoryless contrast: pings in the dark, and the impression fades entirely; nothing persists to re-read. That is a traverser without artifacts — the picture of what this project would be with no md files at all. **The locksmith** — the fit-clause, literal: the key's shape is *defined* by the lock's, under tension, in particular conditions. **Molecular docking** — the same clause at nature's scale: the candidate must fit the binding site under pH and temperature. Both donate vocabulary to your ontology sentence, not scenes to live in.
