## Question

From `_branch.md` (two distinct points the user judged missing from the enduring-edge finding):
1. **Coverage-by-control.** Providers' smarter models traverse thinking space well *in one shot* for near-distribution tasks (prompt a game, design something) because the answer sits near their training data. But the real value of *structured* traversal is **coverage** — for things like science. Thinking space is infinite with multiple solution-approximations; an *uncontrolled* traversal is randomized and you just hope it converges; a *controlled* one is **brute force + engineering**.
2. **Self-improvement.** The project is trying to achieve self-improvement — it runs to improve itself, hopefully producing an edge over pure thinking models. *But this is a hope.*

**Goal:** add both, tied to real project assets, honest about each one's reach — without claiming the coverage edge universally (the user conceded one-shot tasks to models) and without laundering the self-improvement hope into a confident claim.

## Finding Summary

- **Item 1 (coverage) is not a new edge — it is the top edge's killer application, and it makes the whole governability thesis concrete.** The prior finding argued governability is the durable axis but stayed abstract about *why anyone wants a controlled traversal*. The answer: **to survey an open space instead of sampling it once and hoping.** A model **samples** — one high-quality jump to a point near its training distribution (perfect when the answer IS a point, like "make a game"); structured traversal **surveys** — systematic, recorded coverage of a space. For an infinite space, a sample is a *point* with no idea what was missed; surveying gives *coverage*.
- **The decisive distinction maps to a domain rule: point-tasks vs map-tasks.** *Use the model for point-tasks* (near-distribution, one good answer suffices). *Use the harness for map-tasks* (open, coverage-demanding, where you must be able to *prove* you covered the space — science, R&D, regulatory due-diligence, safety cases). The variable is the task's shape, not the model's IQ. This **sharpens** the prior finding's domain-blind "harness loses on per-answer quality" caveat into a decision rule (loses on points, wins on maps), it does not contradict it.
- **Coverage and provenance are two faces of one edge: verifiable systematic coverage.** Coverage you can't verify is just a claim. So the top edge sharpens to **"auditable, portable, and exhaustive provenance" — defensible coverage of an open space.** This is what science / regulation / due-diligence actually need (you must *prove* you covered it, not just produce a good answer). Coverage is provenance's killer app — folding it in (rather than adding a 7th edge) prevents double-counting.
- **The un-absorbable core (this survived the hardest attack): an operator-owned, external coverage criterion.** The strongest objection is that a future agentic model could emit its *own* verifiable search trace — so it can "prove" coverage too. The honest answer narrows the edge: a model can emit a trace, but it **structurally cannot be the one that defines and grades what counts as "covered"** — that's the self-reference trap at the criterion level. The edge is that the coverage criterion (explfine's declared territory + the operator's frontier) is **external to and ungameable by the model**. A single model call would have to both define and grade its own exhaustiveness; an external harness doesn't.
- **"Brute force + engineering" = precision-over-scale at the search level.** Control downgrades the requirement on the model from *genius-in-one-jump* to *adequate-per-step* while the SYSTEM guarantees coverage. Uncontrolled = randomized hope-for-convergence; controlled = engineered coverage. (This is the project's precision-over-scale thesis applied to search-completeness, and the algorithm-family verdict — controlled derivative-free search over an infinite, non-stationary space — recast as a competitive advantage.)
- **Honest bound on "coverage":** it means *declared, bounded, recorded, resumable* coverage of a *scoped territory* with an explicit frontier of what's deliberately not covered (exactly explfine's claim) — NOT literal completeness of infinity. The value is *defensibility of the declared scope*. The real-world precedent: a **systematic review (PRISMA)** outranks a brilliant expert's opinion *for evidence*, not because the expert is dumber, but because its search strategy is declared and its coverage is defensible. The harness is the systematic review; the model is the expert.
- **Item 2 (self-improvement) is a meta-edge / call-option above the durability tiers — and its honest core is *attemptability*, not the payoff.** Don't claim self-improvement (a hope); claim what's real now: the project is **structurally positioned to attempt** cross-run, domain-specific self-improvement, because it has the persistent substrate (recorded traversals to improve against), the metacognition theory (an explicit, editable self-model), and explfine(self). A *stateless* flagship structurally cannot self-improve across runs without the provider retraining it. **The present asset is attemptability (real, and rare versus stateless models); the payoff — that it actually produces an edge — stays a hope.**
- **Self-improvement is a low-marginal-cost call option.** The expensive precondition (the persistent, theorized, recorded substrate) is **built anyway** for provenance and coverage; the only *extra* cost is an improvement-evaluation loop. So the option is nearly free: worst case you lose the option, never the position (the Tier-1 edges still stand); best case it compounds. It is a *meta-edge* — it acts ON the other edges (its most plausible first target: improve the project's own coverage — "self-improve the surveyor"), not beside them. It does NOT sit in a durability tier (those rank *static* edges; this ranks *improvability* — a different axis).
- **Amendment shape: fuse-and-sharpen (item 1) + add-one-category (item 2), anti-bloat preserved.** No new tier-edge is created; the edge count is essentially unchanged. Precision is added to the top edge and the relevance verdict; one new category (the meta-edge) is added above the tiers.

## Finding

*Context: the prior finding argued the project stays relevant against ever-smarter models because it competes on governability (inspectable, persistent, steerable thinking across calls), not capability (thinking-per-call), and governability's value rises with capability. The user added two points it missed. Both extend that spine.*

### 1. Coverage is the killer app of the top edge — sampling vs surveying

The prior finding named governability as the durable axis but didn't say what controlled traversal is *for*. Item 1 supplies it: **you control a traversal in order to survey an open space systematically, rather than sample it once and hope it converged.**

- A model **samples**: it produces one high-quality output drawn from a distribution shaped by its training. When the target is a *point* near that distribution — "make a game," "design this" — the sample IS the deliverable, and the harness is pure overhead. (This is the user's concession, and it's correct.)
- Structured traversal **surveys**: it moves through a space systematically and records where it went. When the target is a *map* of an open space — a scientific question, an exhaustive option-analysis, a safety case — a single sample gives you a point and *no idea what you missed*; surveying gives coverage.

The vivid form: a model is a brilliant **lottery ticket** (one draw, maybe the jackpot, but you never learn the other numbers); the harness is a **census** (slower, systematic, and you can show exactly who you counted and who you didn't). For science you need a census, not a lucky ticket.

### 2. The domain rule: point-tasks vs map-tasks

This sharpens the prior finding's vague "harness loses on per-answer quality" into a decision rule:

- **Point-tasks** — near-distribution, one good answer suffices (a game, a design, most everyday asks). *Use the model.* The harness is overhead.
- **Map-tasks** — open, coverage-demanding, and you must be able to *prove* you covered the space (science, R&D, regulatory due-diligence, safety cases, systematic review, patent landscaping). *Use the harness.* Sampling can't survey.

The variable is the *task's shape* (does it need a point or a map?), not the model's intelligence. So the prior caveat is qualified, not contradicted: the harness loses on points, wins on maps. This also names the project's go-to-market: **map-tasks** — the domains that must defend their coverage.

### 3. The fused top edge, and its un-absorbable core

Coverage you can't verify is just a claim. Provenance is the *record*; coverage is what the record is *of*. They are two faces of one edge, so the prior top edge sharpens:

> "auditable, portable provenance" → **"auditable, portable, and *exhaustive* provenance"** = verifiable systematic coverage (defensible exhaustiveness).

The hardest objection — and its answer — defines the edge's true core. **Objection:** a future agentic model with tool-use could run its own systematic search and emit a verifiable trace, so it can prove coverage too. **Answer:** a model can emit a trace, but it *structurally cannot be the party that defines and grades what counts as "covered"* — if the model both does the search and certifies its own exhaustiveness, that's the self-reference trap (the grader is the graded). The durable edge is that the **coverage criterion is operator-owned and external to the model**: the operator declares the territory (explfine's declared coverage) and the frontier (what must be covered, what's deliberately parked), and the harness shows the search met it. A single model call can't provide that — it would have to define and grade its own exhaustiveness. (This is the same third-party-neutral logic the prior finding used for provenance, now at the criterion level.)

### 4. "Brute force + engineering" — precision-over-scale at the search level

The user's phrase is exact. Control **downgrades the requirement on the model from genius to adequacy**: you no longer need it brilliant enough to land the answer in one jump; you need it adequate per-step while the *system* guarantees the coverage. Uncontrolled traversal is randomized hope-for-convergence; controlled traversal is engineered coverage. This is the project's own precision-over-scale thesis (the harness supplies what the model lacks) applied to *search-completeness*, and the algorithm-family verdict (controlled derivative-free search over an infinite, non-stationary space of multiple solution-approximations) recast as a competitive advantage.

**Honest bound.** "Coverage" never means conquering infinity. It means *declared, bounded, recorded, resumable* coverage of a *scoped territory*, with an explicit frontier of what's deliberately left. The value is *defensibility* — you can show what you covered, what you parked with reasons, and what you left — not totality. The real-world precedent is medicine's **systematic review (PRISMA):** it outranks a brilliant expert's opinion *as evidence* precisely because its search strategy is declared and its coverage is defensible and reproducible — not because the expert is less smart. A systematic review covers a *declared scope*, not all knowledge, which is exactly the honest claim here. **Why this is Tier-1** (value rises with capability): a smarter sampler still hands you a *point*, never a *map*; as problems get more open, the demand for surveying rises.

### 5. Self-improvement — a low-marginal-cost call-option meta-edge

The project's stated aim includes improving itself. The honest treatment splits it:

- **Attemptability (real, and rare).** The project is *structurally positioned to attempt* cross-run, domain-specific self-improvement: it has the persistent substrate (recorded traversals to improve against), the metacognition theory (an explicit, editable model of its own thinking), and explfine pointed at its own harness. A *stateless* flagship structurally *cannot* self-improve across runs without the provider retraining it — it has no persistent self between releases. So the present, defensible claim is: *"we can attempt domain-specific recursive self-improvement, and a stateless model can't."*
- **Payoff (hoped).** That the attempt actually produces an edge over pure thinking models. **This is a hope** — kept load-bearing, never sold as a plan.

It is a **meta-edge**, not a tier-edge: it acts ON the other edges (a working loop would raise them all over time — its most plausible first target is improving the project's own coverage, i.e. *self-improve the surveyor*), so it sits *above* the durability tiers, not inside them. Putting it in a tier would miscategorize it — the tiers rank *durability versus models*; self-improvement is about *improvability*, a different axis.

And it is a **low-marginal-cost call option.** The expensive precondition — the persistent, theorized, recorded substrate — is *built anyway* for the provenance and coverage edges; the only *extra* cost is an improvement-evaluation loop (a way to measure whether a proposed change actually improves traversal, with safe rollback). So the upside is nearly free: worst case, you lose the option but keep the position (the Tier-1 edges stand); best case, it compounds. The project's other edges are its *position*; self-improvement is its *option on a better position.*

## Reasoning

**Why coverage is the top edge's killer app, not a 7th edge.** Coverage and provenance are inseparable: a survey you can't verify is just a claim, and a record of nothing is empty. Folding coverage into provenance (sharpening it to "exhaustive provenance") rather than listing it separately prevents double-counting and explains the domain in one move — coverage is *why* you'd pay for provenance in science. It also concretizes the prior finding's abstract governability thesis: governability matters *because* it's what lets you survey instead of sample.

**Why the coverage edge narrowed under fire.** Innovation proposed "provable coverage" as the un-absorbable core. Critique's strongest attack: a future agentic model could emit its own verifiable trace. The honest narrowing — the edge is not "we have a record" (a model can have one too) but "the coverage *criterion* is operator-owned and external to the model" (a model can't neutrally define and grade its own exhaustiveness — the self-reference trap). This is the edge's true, defensible core, and it matches the prior finding's third-party-neutral logic.

**Why self-improvement is a meta-edge, not a tier-edge, and why it stays a hope.** The durability tiers rank static edges versus models; self-improvement is a different axis (improvability), so it sits above them. And it is genuinely a hope (the user said so) — but not nothing: the *attemptability* is real and rare (a stateless model structurally can't attempt cross-run self-improvement), and the option is low-cost (the substrate is built anyway). Presenting it as a claimed edge would breach the prior finding's name-every-vulnerability discipline; presenting only the hope would miss the real present asset. The attemptability/payoff split holds both.

**Significant narrowings (critique).** *"Provable coverage"* → operator-owned external criterion (a model can emit a trace; it can't be its own coverage judge). *"Zero-marginal-cost call option"* → low-marginal-cost (the substrate is free; the improvement-eval loop is extra). *"Coverage"* → declared, bounded, resumable coverage of a scoped territory (not infinite completeness). *PRISMA precedent* → mapped at the search-strategy/declared-scope level (it reinforces bounded-coverage, doesn't break on infinity). Each narrowing moved an exciting claim to its defensible core — the prior finding's discipline applied to its own extension.

**Significant kills.** *Coverage as a standalone Tier-1 edge* — killed (double-counts provenance). *Coverage as the project's whole purpose* — killed (governability is broader; coverage is the killer app, not the definition). *Self-improvement in a durability tier* — killed (wrong axis: unproven ≠ eroding). *Dropping the bounded-coverage honesty* — killed (returns the infinity over-promise). *The search-engine analogy* — killed (the lottery/census + systematic-review images are sharper).
