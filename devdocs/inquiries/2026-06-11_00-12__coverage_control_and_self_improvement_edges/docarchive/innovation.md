## Phase 2 — Generate (7 mechanisms × 3, compact)

### 1. Lens Shifting (Framer)

- **1G — the search-completeness lens.** Coverage is *search completeness as a product*: a model gives you the best answer it sampled; the harness gives you the best answer **plus the proof you searched the space**. "Best sampled" vs "best, and here's what else we ruled out." One sharp framing for the fused top edge.
- **1F — the "lottery vs census" lens (focused).** A model is a brilliant *lottery ticket* — one draw, possibly the jackpot, but you never learn the other numbers. The harness is a *census* — slower, systematic, and you can show exactly who you counted and who you didn't. For science you need a census, not a lucky ticket. Vivid for sampling-vs-surveying; one image. Adopted.
- **1C — the "but a genius doesn't need a checklist" adversary (contrarian).** "A smart-enough model just IS the coverage — it considers everything implicitly." Answer (the fusion): implicit consideration is **unverifiable** — "I thought about the alternatives" is unfalsifiable; only an explicit, recorded frontier lets anyone CHECK the coverage. The edge isn't being-more-thorough (a model can match that); it's **being able to PROVE the thoroughness** — and a model proving its own coverage is the self-reference trap. Adopted — restates coverage as *provable* coverage, the un-absorbable core.

### 2. Combination (Generator)

- **2G — coverage × the value-curve.** Coverage is Tier-1 by the SAME logic as provenance: as model capability rises, a smarter sampler still hands you a *point* (a better lottery ticket), never a *map* — so the value of surveying rises with capability, just like governance. Coverage slots into the value-curve cleanly. Adopted.
- **2F — the point/map rule × the go-to-market.** The domain rule isn't just analytic — it's a market map: point-tasks (consumer, creative, one-shot — concede to models) vs map-tasks (science, R&D, regulatory due-diligence, safety cases, patent landscaping, systematic review — the project's market). One line names where to sell. Adopted.
- **2C — self-improvement × coverage (the recursive tie).** The meta-edge's best *attempt-target* is improving the project's own COVERAGE (explfine(self) surveying the project's own thinking space for better disciplines) — so the two new edges connect: if self-improvement works, the thing it most plausibly improves first is the coverage edge. One line; keeps item-2 grounded in a concrete (if hoped) first target.

### 3. Inversion (Framer; depth-checked)

- **3G — the "when does coverage NOT matter" inversion.** Invert "coverage is the edge" → when is it worthless? When the task has ONE right answer near-distribution (the model nails it) OR when nobody needs to PROVE the search (internal brainstorming). This confirms the domain rule by drawing its boundary: coverage matters when the space is open AND someone must defend the search. Absorbed (sharpens the domain rule into two conditions, not one).
- **3F — the meta-edge inversion (focused, adopted).** Invert "self-improvement is a hope" → what's the WORST honest version? That it never pays off and the project is just a fancy harness. Resolution: even in the worst case, the *attemptability* delivered something — the recorded substrate that MAKES the attempt possible is also the provenance/coverage asset (the same corpus). So self-improvement is a **free call option**: you build the substrate for the Tier-1 edges anyway, and self-improvement is the upside you get for free on top. **Adopted — the meta-edge costs nothing extra (the substrate is already being built for provenance/coverage), so it's a zero-marginal-cost call option** — the strongest honest framing.
- **3C — the "providers self-improve faster" adversary (contrarian, adopted).** "Providers retrain models constantly — they self-improve faster than your harness ever could." Answer: providers improve the ENGINE on THEIR cadence and THEIR objectives; the project's self-improvement is **operator-owned, domain-specific, and continuous between provider releases** — it improves YOUR traversal of YOUR space, which the provider has no signal for and no incentive to optimize. Different target, different owner. Adopted into the attemptability framing.

### 4. Constraint Manipulation (Framer; both directions)

- **4-ADD (generic).** Each new edge gets its **honest-status tag** (coverage = present-and-grounded-but-machinery-partly-in-build; self-improvement = attemptability-real / payoff-hoped) — carrying the prior finding's name-every-vulnerability discipline.
- **4-ADD (focused).** The coverage edit names its **buyer + the proof-artifact** (science/regulatory/safety; the artifact = the recorded frontier "here's what we covered, parked, and ruled out").
- **4-REMOVE (generic).** Drop the bounded-coverage honesty (claim full coverage)? → over-promise (infinite space); the prior finding's killed "more thorough" trap returns. Fails.
- **4-REMOVE (contrarian).** Drop the meta-edge (keep only coverage)? → the user explicitly asked for self-improvement; and 3F shows it's a free option. Keep.

### 5. Absence Recognition (Generator; both levels, bidirectional)

- **Patch-level:** (i) **the falsifiability inversion** — coverage's deepest value is that it makes "did we miss something?" a CHECKABLE question (a model can't tell you what it didn't consider; the frontier ledger can) — one line, sharpens the provenance fusion. (ii) **the resumability property** — recorded coverage is *resumable* (pick up the survey where it stopped); a model's sample isn't (you re-roll the lottery) — distinct sub-property of the coverage edge.
- **Redesign-level:** none — the fuse-and-sharpen + add-category shape holds.
- **Bidirectional (already-present):** this very session is a *coverage* artifact — the inquiry chain surveyed the edge space, parked some (the speculative corpus edge), and recorded the frontier; the edge is being demonstrated, not just claimed. Grounding line.

### 6. Domain Transfer (Generator; native source included)

- **6-native (the project's own).** explfine = "declared coverage of a territory" IS the coverage edge, already canonical; the competitive claim is just explfine pointed outward. One grounding sentence.
- **6-different (systematic review vs an expert opinion).** In medicine, a *systematic review* (PRISMA — declared search strategy, inclusion/exclusion, what was found and excluded) outranks a brilliant expert's opinion *for evidence*, precisely because it's *defensible and reproducible*, not because the expert is dumber. The harness is the systematic review; the model is the expert. **The single best real-world precedent for coverage-as-an-edge** — adopted as item-1's anchor (medicine already pays a premium for defensible coverage over brilliance).
- **6-different (search engine vs an oracle).** REJECTED — the lottery/census + systematic-review images are sharper.

### 7. Extrapolation (Generator)

- **7G — the end-state for coverage.** At the limit, the project is the **"systematic review engine for open problems"** — point a controlled traversal at a scientific/strategic question and get a *defensible map* (covered / parked / ruled-out with reasons), not a lucky answer. One end-state line for the go-to-market.
- **7F — the self-improvement value if it lands.** If the meta-edge pays off, it compounds the coverage edge first (better disciplines → better surveys), then the rest — the highest-payoff path is "self-improve the surveyor." Adopted (ties 2C's recursive note to the value claim).
- **7C — the meta-edge failure path (kept honest).** If self-improvement never pays off, the project is still a defensible-coverage + provenance product (Tier-1 stands) — so the downside is bounded (you lose the option, not the position). The call-option's floor. Adopted.
