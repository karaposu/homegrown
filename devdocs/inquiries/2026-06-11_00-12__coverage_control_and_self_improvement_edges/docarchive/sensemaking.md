- KI1 — **Coverage is the answer to "why would anyone want a *controlled* traversal at all?" — it makes the governability thesis concrete.** The prior finding argued governability is the durable axis but stayed abstract about the USE. Item-1 supplies the use: you control the traversal *in order to* survey an infinite space systematically rather than sample it once and hope. **Coverage is governability applied to exploration.** This isn't a new edge; it's the prior finding's spine given its killer application.
- KI2 — **The decisive distinction is SAMPLING vs SURVEYING, and it maps onto a domain axis: POINT-TASKS vs MAP-TASKS.** A model samples brilliantly — for a task whose answer is a *point* near its training distribution (make a game, design a thing), one good jump IS the deliverable, and the harness is pure overhead. For a task that needs a *map* of an open space — science, exhaustive option-analysis, safety cases, novel research where the answer ISN'T near-distribution — sampling gives you a point and no idea what you missed; surveying gives coverage. **The variable isn't model IQ; it's whether the task needs a point or a map.** This sharpens the prior finding's domain-blind "harness loses on per-answer quality" into a decision rule: *model for points, harness for maps.*
- KI3 — **Coverage and provenance fuse into one edge: VERIFIABLE SYSTEMATIC COVERAGE ("defensible exhaustiveness").** They're two faces: provenance is the RECORD; coverage is what the record is OF. The fusion matters because it answers the strongest counter to coverage (an agentic model could self-impose a systematic search): yes, but its coverage is *unverifiable* — a model self-reporting "I considered the alternatives" is the self-reference trap. Only an external, recorded frontier makes coverage *checkable* ("23 regions explored, 5 parked, 2 dead-ended, with reasons"). So the top edge sharpens from "auditable, portable provenance" to **"auditable, portable, and *exhaustive* provenance" — defensible coverage of an open space.** This is exactly what science / regulation / due-diligence / safety-cases need (you must PROVE you covered it, not just produce a good answer).
- KI4 — **"Brute force + engineering" is precision-over-scale at the SEARCH level — and it ties coverage to committed canon.** The user's phrase is profound: control *downgrades the requirement on the model from genius to adequacy*. You no longer need the model brilliant enough to land the answer in one jump; you need it adequate per-step while the SYSTEM guarantees coverage. This is the precision-over-scale thesis (the harness supplies what the model lacks) applied to search-completeness, and it's the algorithm-family verdict (controlled derivative-free search over an infinite, non-stationary space) recast as a competitive advantage. Item-1 is therefore not a new claim — it's the competitive face of three committed theses (governability, precision-over-scale, the algorithm family).

So the present, defensible statement is: *"we can attempt domain-specific recursive self-improvement, and a stateless model can't"* — the payoff stays a hope, but the *positioning* is real and rare.
- KI6 — **Self-improvement is a META-EDGE, not a tier-edge — it acts ON the other edges, not beside them.** The Tier-1/2/3 tiers rank static edges by durability vs models. Self-improvement is a different axis: the ability to improve one's own edges. Putting it in Tier-3 (transitional) would mis-categorize it — it's not *eroding* (Tier-3's character), it's *unproven* (a different status). It sits ABOVE the tiers as a call-option: near-zero value if the hope fails, compounding/largest if it succeeds. The project's tiered edges are its *position*; self-improvement is its *option on a better position.*
- KI7 — **The amendment respects anti-bloat: item-1 FUSES (sharpens the top edge + adds one domain axis), item-2 ADDS one category (the meta-edge).** No new Tier-1/2/3 edge is created (coverage folds into provenance; self-improvement sits above the tiers). The prior finding's edge COUNT is essentially unchanged; what's added is precision (the top edge's exhaustiveness face; the point-vs-map domain rule) and one new category (the meta-edge). This is the same discipline the scorecard-enrichment used: sharpen + add-a-category, not inflate the core list.

## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

#### Ambiguity A1: Is coverage a new edge, a sharpening of the top edge, or a reframe of the project's purpose?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** it's a reframe — the project's PURPOSE is "systematic coverage of open spaces," which is bigger and clearer than "governability."

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** governability is broader than coverage — provenance serves audits, controllability serves steering, sovereignty serves ownership, even for tasks that aren't coverage-demanding. Declaring coverage the *purpose* would narrow the project and orphan its non-coverage edges. But coverage IS more than a bolt-on edge: it's the *killer application* of the top edge (provenance) — the use that most concretely demonstrates why control matters, and the one that names the go-to-market (map-tasks). So it's bigger than an addition, smaller than a purpose-reframe.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** coverage **sharpens the top edge** (provenance → "auditable, portable, *exhaustive* provenance" = verifiable systematic coverage) **and sharpens the relevance verdict** (the point-vs-map domain rule — the project's strongest niche is map-tasks like science), **but does not replace the governability frame**. It's the killer app, not the purpose. **No longer allowed:** coverage as a 7th standalone edge (double-counts provenance); coverage as the whole purpose (narrows the project).

#### Ambiguity A2: What is the domain rule — and does it contradict the prior finding's quality caveat?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** saying "the harness wins for science" contradicts the prior finding's "the harness loses on per-answer quality to a smart model."

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the two are about different things. The prior caveat is about *per-answer quality on a point-task* (a smart model's single answer beats the harness's overhead). The new rule is about *coverage on a map-task* (where there is no single answer — you need the space surveyed). They QUALIFY each other, they don't conflict: **the harness loses on points, wins on maps.** The variable is the task's shape (does it need a point or a map?), not the model's IQ.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** the **point-tasks-vs-map-tasks domain rule** — *use the model for point-tasks (near-distribution, one good answer: a game, a design); use the harness for map-tasks (open, coverage-demanding, must-prove-you-covered-it: science, R&D, audits, safety cases).* This SHARPENS the prior caveat (names when the harness loses vs wins), doesn't contradict it. **No longer allowed:** claiming the harness wins universally; treating the quality caveat as a blanket loss.

#### Ambiguity A3: What does "coverage" honestly mean for an infinite space?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** "brute force + engineering" promises completeness — but you can't exhaustively cover an infinite space, so the claim over-promises.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the edge is not *total* coverage of infinity — it's **declared, bounded, recorded** coverage of a *scoped territory* (exactly explfine's actual claim: "declared coverage of a territory," with the frontier marking what's parked/unexplored). The value isn't completeness; it's **defensibility** — you can show precisely what you covered, what you parked with reasons, and what you left at the frontier. A sampler can't show any of that. So "brute force + engineering" means *systematic, recorded, resumable* coverage of a bounded scope, not conquering infinity.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** coverage = **defensible exhaustiveness** — declared, bounded, recorded coverage of a scoped territory (with an explicit frontier of what's not yet covered), NOT literal completeness of an infinite space. The edge is that you can *prove and resume* your coverage. **No longer allowed:** implying total/infinite coverage; "more thorough" vagueness (the prior finding's killed trap).

#### Ambiguity A4: How does self-improvement enter — and at what honesty?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** add it as a strong edge — the project's whole self-improvement aim is a major differentiator.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** as an *achieved edge* it's a hope (the user said so), and over-claiming it would breach the prior finding's name-every-vulnerability discipline. But it's not *nothing*: the honest split is **attemptability (real + rare) vs payoff (hoped)** — the project is structurally positioned to ATTEMPT cross-run self-improvement (persistent substrate + editable metacognition theory + explfine(self)), which a stateless flagship structurally cannot; the payoff (an actual edge) stays hoped. And it's a different KIND — a meta-edge (acts on the other edges), not a tier-edge.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** self-improvement enters as a **meta-edge / call-option above the tiers** — *present asset:* attemptability + rarity (positioned to attempt domain-specific recursive self-improvement a stateless model can't); *hoped payoff:* it actually produces an edge; *variance:* near-zero if it fails, compounding/largest if it succeeds. Explicitly NOT Tier-1/2/3 (those rank durability; this is improvability). **No longer allowed:** self-improvement as a claimed/likely edge; placing it in a durability tier.

#### Ambiguity A5: Does the amendment bloat the prior finding?

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** neither adds a Tier-1/2/3 edge. Coverage FUSES into the existing top edge (sharpening it) and adds ONE domain axis to the relevance verdict; self-improvement adds ONE new *category* (the meta-edge) that sits above the tiers, not a new tier-entry.
