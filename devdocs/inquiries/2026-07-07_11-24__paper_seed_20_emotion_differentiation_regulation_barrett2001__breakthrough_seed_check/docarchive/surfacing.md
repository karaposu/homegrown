## (A) SEEN facts — the paper's claims (relevance-tagged)

- **P-A [CORE] — differentiation predicts regulation.** Emotion differentiation (granularity — finely distinguishing anger/sadness/nervousness vs lumping as "feeling bad") predicts emotion regulation: high NEGATIVE differentiation → MORE negative regulation, esp. at high intensity (~:170, and the result :291-316). Relevance: DIRECT (the paper's thesis).
- **P-B [the transferable structure] — a fine-grained CONCEPT carries its action-repertoire.** VERBATIM (:116-124): differentiators have *"more highly activated discrete emotion knowledge... The emotion knowledge includes the abstract cause... its relational context... its expressive modes... and sequences of action to take to enhance or reduce the experience (i.e., plans of emotion regulation)."* And (:133-142): *"discrete emotion concepts provide a wealth of information regarding the behavioural repertoire... If this information is highly accessible in those who generate differentiated conscious emotional experiences... individuals with highly differentiated emotion experience should be at an advantage in regulating their emotions."* → **fine differentiation → ACCESSIBLE action-repertoire → regulation.** "Knowing what you're feeling" IS "knowing what to do about it." Relevance: DIRECT (the transfer candidate).
- **P-C — the monitoring→control (differentiation-flavored).** VERBATIM (:89-92): *"Knowing how we feel helps to inform us about the significance of the immediate situation, to work out what we should do next, and to indicate what, if anything, we should do about changing how we feel."* Relevance: SUPPORTING (maps to the owned monitoring→control link).
- **P-D — the asymmetry / conditional nuance.** Differentiation→regulation held ONLY for NEGATIVE emotions (positive differentiation UNRELATED, :326-336), and MOST at HIGH intensity ("where the press for emotion regulation is greatest", :143-144). → differentiate where regulation is PRESSING, not everywhere. Relevance: SUPPORTING (maps to the decision-vs-label test).
- **P-E — the accessibility language.** The action-knowledge is "highly accessible" in differentiators (:138), "easily accessible due to repeated use" (:406). Relevance: PERIPHERAL (echoes paper 19's accessibility / the availability illusion, but here it's action-repertoire accessibility, not confidence).
- **P-QUARANTINE — the emotion content + apparatus.** Feelings, negative/positive valence, intensity, "emotional intelligence"; the 14-day diary/RIR protocol, N=53, the differentiation indices (inter-emotion correlations), the OLS regressions, Figure 1. The harness has NO emotions → HEAVILY non-transferable. Only the abstract differentiation→action structure is a candidate. Relevance: PERIPHERAL (the paper's scientific content).
