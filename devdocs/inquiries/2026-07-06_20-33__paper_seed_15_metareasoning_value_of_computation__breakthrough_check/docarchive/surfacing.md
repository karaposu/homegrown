## A — Paper FACTs (Russell & Wefald 1991; quoted/located against the file)

- **A-1 — metareasoning defined (line ~200):** "deliberation concerning possible changes to the computational state of the agent." The paper's subject is *reasoning about which computation to do next*.
- **A-2 — the two principles of rational metareasoning (~271–284):** (1) "**Computations are to be treated as actions**, and are therefore to be selected among on the basis of their expected utilities." (2) "The utility of a computation is derived from its expected effects, consisting of: (a) the passage of time...; (b) **the possible revision of the agent's intended actions** in the real world."
- **A-3 — THE VOC CORE (~281–284):** "**The ability of a computation to cause the agent to take a different course of action**, that has been revealed by the computation to be superior to the agent's original intention, **is the fundamental source of positive utility for computations.**" And the sharp corollary (footnote, ~326–329): "**there can be no utility to computations whose sole possible outcome is to increase confidence.**" [THE load-bearing principle for the import test.]
- **A-4 — net value = benefit − time-cost (eq 2 ~443; eq 18 ~703):** `V(Sj) = U([Sj]) − U([default action])`, decomposed to `V = Δ(Sj) − TC(|Sj|)` (estimated benefit minus time cost).
- **A-5 — the ideal-control STOP-RULE (~489–493, also Good [15]):** "Step 1. Keep performing the available computation with **highest expected net value, until none has positive expected net value.** Step 2. Commit to the action preferred according to the internal state resulting from Step 1."
- **A-6 — the REGRESS problem → compile/hardwire (~236–266):** "no computation can be executed until a computation has been executed to decide on it"; metareasoning itself has costs, so one must "back off from insisting on optimal control of all reasoning" — "Decisions at various points in the hierarchy can be **hardwired or made by default**, approximate decision methods can be used." The meta-policy is compiled/learned, not deliberated per-step.
- **A-7 — meta-greedy + single-step (myopic) assumptions (§5.3):** tractable approximations — consider single primitive steps (depth-limit-1 on the meta-level) and evaluate partial computations as if complete. "a fixed meta-meta-level policy of a depth limit of 1."
- **A-8 — qualitative stopping behaviour (§5.5 ~922–943):** stop when best and second-best are "too far apart" (further computation won't flip the choice) OR "too close together, and the variance... is small" (won't reveal a significant difference); continue under "uncertainty... and considerable overlap." Sharp line: "many algorithm designers **erroneously assume that the goal of deliberation is to identify the best action, rather than to maximize net expected utility.**"
- **A-9 — bounded optimality + REGULATIVE PRINCIPLES (~1182, ~1315–1317):** the goal is "bounded optimality"; a full theory should provide "a set of **regulative principles** to define and guide this convergence process, **replacing the standard axioms of perfect rationality.**"
- **A-10 — the MACHINERY (reverse-lens quarantine candidate, LARGE):** numeric utility-estimate joint distributions `pj(u)`; the propagation-function transformation (eq 24); the coherence/martingale condition (~622–625: for a rational agent `E[future estimate] = current estimate`); the intrinsic-utility/time-cost formalism. All presuppose a numeric utility function `U` and an evaluation function `Û`.
- **A-11 — the SEARCH APPLICATIONS (quarantine candidate):** DTA* (decision-theoretic A*), MGSS* (game-playing), alpha-beta, subtree-independence, the "meta-greedy barrier," Eight Puzzle, Othello, backgammon, anytime-algorithm composition via performance profiles. Concrete-level; domain-specific.
- **A-12 — composition of anytime algorithms (~1247–1265):** given subsystems with performance profiles, "construct the optimal apportionment of resources" — "a natural generalization of the task of a compiler for a language with procedural abstraction." Composition of computations under a time budget.
