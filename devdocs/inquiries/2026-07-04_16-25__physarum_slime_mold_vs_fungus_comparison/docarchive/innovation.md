## The Six Finding-Ready Pieces

### Piece Q1 — What Physarum is (honesty-bounded)

The same rule the prior dives held governs this one: real mechanism informs the mapping where the science is solid, the model carries where it is thin, and contested claims stay out. And here the "contested" clause earns its keep, because Physarum attracts a specific overclaim — that the slime mold *thinks*. It is cited here for its behaviours, not a mind.

*Physarum polycephalum* in its foraging (plasmodial) stage is a single enormous cell — many nuclei, no internal walls, one continuous bag of streaming cytoplasm. So it is, if anything, *more* one-body than the mycelium, which is many walled filaments forming one organism. It is motile: its cytoplasm oscillates back and forth, the whole mass can crawl, and it can withdraw entirely from one place and relocate to another. It forages by thickening the tubes that carry productive flow and letting unproductive ones wither. It has no brain; the pattern emerges from flow and local chemistry. And it has a striking experimental record — it finds the shortest path through a maze between two food sources, it grew a transport network comparably efficient and robust to Tokyo's actual rail system when food was placed at the cities, and it leaves an extracellular slime trail where it has already been and avoids it, using that trail as an external memory to keep from re-searching ground.

### Piece Q2 — The head-to-head, and the real conclusion

Laid against the mycelium as an analogy, the differences are clean:

| | Physarum | Mycelium |
|---|---|---|
| structure | one cell (more unified) | many hyphae, one organism (more multibody) |
| motion | flows, migrates, can fully withdraw and relocate | grows, extends, the body largely stays |
| memory | two — internal tubes (live) + external slime trail (persistent) | one — the network (body = path-memory), fused |
| allocation | thicken/prune tubes + migrate the mass | reinforce cords + abandon/recycle regions |
| grounding | demonstrated computation (maze, Tokyo rail) | foraging, no sharp experimental proof |

The conclusion is not that one wins. It is that **both are whole-architecture scenes — each holds the search-move, a record, and allocation — but they weight the concerns differently.** The mycelium is strongest where the record is a *persistent, accumulated body*; Physarum is strongest where the work is *active reallocation, re-decision, and an external memory.* They are two variants of the same kind of scene, each vivid exactly where the other is faint.

### Piece Q3 — Physarum partly fixes the mycelium's over-integration (CENTER)

The prior finding left one honest wrinkle in the mycelium: it *fuses* memory and body into one structure, where the project keeps its external corpus separable from the live context of any one traverse. Physarum resolves that wrinkle on the axis that matters — because Physarum keeps **two** memories, not one. Its internal tube network is the live flow, the current allocation. Its external slime trail is a record left *in the world*, persisting where the organism has been, read back to avoid re-searching. That is precisely the project's own arrangement: a written corpus and a route-index kept *outside* any live traverse. On the memory-separation axis, then, **Physarum is a better map than the mycelium** — it shows a brainless forager holding an external, separable record distinct from its live flow, which is exactly what the mycelium could not show.

The fix is real but partial, and the boundary must be stated or the picture inflates. Physarum's slime trail records *where it has been* — a been-there, anti-redundancy trail — not *what it found and how much each find is worth.* So it maps the **anti-redundancy function** of the project's record (don't re-traverse settled ground), not the whole weighted, proportion-kept archive. And avoiding where you have been is not re-reading the whole to correct an over-weighting; so this external memory, like the mycelium's internal one, still does not supply proportion-correction. The wrinkle is resolved on the *separation* axis and left open on the *proportion* axis — which is why the pairing rule still holds (Q6).

### Piece Q4 — Join, not replace or separate; the convergence becomes a constellation (CENTER)

So where does Physarum stand relative to the mycelium? It **joins** as a complement — it does not replace it, and it is not a separate spare.

Replacement fails on inspection: Physarum trades rather than dominates. It loses the mycelium's persistent-body-as-record (it retracts and migrates — its body is transient, where the mycelium *builds and keeps* a body that is itself the accumulated trace) and it loses the multibody richness the user cared about (one cell, against many hyphae). A scene that gives up two real mappings cannot take the other's place. And "keep them separate, pick one per occasion" fails too, because both map real and distinct properties of the project: it genuinely *accumulates a persisting corpus* (the mycelium's strength) and it genuinely *reallocates live and keeps an external anti-redundancy memory* (Physarum's strength). Drop either and you lose a true part of the picture.

What survives is that both belong, because each names a strength the other lacks — the same standard by which the prior finding kept two multibody models. **Physarum is the active-computation, re-decider, external-memory scene; the mycelium is the accumulator, persistent-record scene.** The convergence-form the prior finding left as a pair — the mycelium for the live intra-system view, humanity for the cross-session view — becomes a small **constellation**: on the live side, the mycelium *and* Physarum, splitting the labour; on the cross-session side, humanity still.

One discipline comes with this, or the family bloats: a constellation earns its size only if every scene carries a distinct, nameable load. The mycelium carries persistent-accumulation; Physarum carries active-reallocation, external memory, and a mechanism existence-proof; humanity carries mortal-independent-heads and the architecture-scale proof. Add a fourth organism only against that test — and drop any scene that stops carrying its own load. Non-sycophancy cuts both ways here: Physarum is admitted because it carries two things the others lack, not because it is the newest arrival.

### Piece Q5 — What Physarum credits, and what it costs

The credit Physarum brings, beyond the external memory, is a slice of grounding. The Tokyo rail is real evidence that a decentralized, brainless network can compute a near-optimal, robust resource-distribution structure — the kind of existence-proof the mycelium's plain foraging never supplied. But it should be placed at the right tier. It grounds *the mechanism* — that reallocation-by-local-flow produces good structure — at lab scale, on a single task. It does not ground *the whole architecture* the way the humanity analogy does, where an actual civilization actually resolves mysteries and cures disease. So Physarum imports **mechanism-grounding, a real but lesser tier than humanity's architecture-grounding.**

And the costs, plainly, without protecting the mycelium or the newcomer. Physarum loses the persistent-body-as-record: it flows on, where the project (like the mycelium) *keeps* what it builds. It loses the multibody richness: one cell is a thinner picture of many fronts than many hyphae. Its external memory, for all that it fixes the separation, is an anti-redundancy trail, not a weighted archive. These are not fatal — they are exactly why it joins rather than replaces.

## Assembly Check — The Emergent

Reading the pieces together, one structure emerges that no single piece states, and it is grounded in the project's own files.

**The record-layer splits into two functions — and the project already runs both.** The earlier findings treated "the record" as one thing. Physarum forces a distinction the mycelium had blurred: there is the record as *accumulated body of findings* (the mycelium's network — what has been learned, kept and added to) and the record as *anti-redundancy trail* (Physarum's slime — where has already been searched, so don't search it again). These are different jobs. And the project keeps both, in different artifacts: the findings corpus is the accumulated body; the persistent route-index — the `_route.md` an inquiry leaves behind — is almost literally the slime trail, an external record of where the inquiry-chain has already gone, read back to avoid re-traversing settled ground. That near-literal match is the sharpest single mapping this dive produces, and it grounds the over-integration fix in something the project actually has.

Scope it honestly (the same discipline as Q3): the `_route.md` records concepts and routes, not spatial position, so the match is functional, not literal-literal — an external persistent record used to avoid re-traversal. Within that scope it is strikingly close.

So the emergent is a refinement of the family's record-layer: **it has two functions — accumulate-findings and avoid-redundancy — and the two organism-scenes each picture one** (the mycelium the accumulation, Physarum the anti-redundancy trail). This is offered to the family and, as a candidate, to the spec-slot; it is not a redefinition of the record-layer, which remains the record-layer.
