Recorded as a coverage observation, NOT a recommendation — the next inquiry proposes and grades.

This matches the project's own architecture (no orchestrator-seat; the Selector chooses locally per turn) — the requirement: *the meaningful shape EMERGES from local rules + a meaningful code, not from a global blueprint.*

## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

#### Ambiguity A1: What FORM does "list the components" take?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** a flat enumerated list of resembling-parts — the user said "list," so just list them.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** a flat list can't do the job the user actually needs — he called the current analogy "bad" and wants better, which requires GRADING, and grading requires knowing which components are load-bearing (their absence makes an analogy misleading) vs decorative. A flat list treats "shows fuzzy boundaries" as equal to "shows the landscape changing as you search" — but only the second's absence makes the bird's-eye actively bad. The form must encode load-bearingness.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** a **SCORECARD** — MUST-HAVE (absence ⇒ actively misleading) / HIGH-VALUE (absence ⇒ impoverished but usable) / VOCABULARY POOL (raw material, not a requirement). **No longer allowed:** a flat undifferentiated feature list.

#### Ambiguity A2: What are the MUST-HAVES (the load-bearing requirements)?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** all the rich properties are essential — merging, drift, limits, seams, emergence, the five signals — promote them all to MUST.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** promoting everything makes the bar unmeetable (no single source shows all of them) and stalls the hunt; the MUST set should be exactly the properties whose ABSENCE made the bird's-eye "bad." Those are: **(1) fit-search** (not random growth, not optimization to a fixed peak); **(2) a NON-STATIONARY landscape** (the shape changes as you search — the candidate's static map fails this; canon's load-bearing match); **(3) interleaved layers with back-pressure** (not sequential phases — the candidate's own contradiction); **(4) local horizon / no global simultaneous visibility** (the bird's-eye's false promise); **(5) a pre-structured, non-uniform space** (clusters + dead zones, not a blank canvas). The rest enrich but their absence doesn't mislead.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** the five must-haves above. **No longer allowed:** promoting refinements to must-haves; a must-list so long no analogy passes.

#### Ambiguity A3: How does the catalog treat many-dimensionality?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** require the analogy to REPRESENT the many dimensions (else it's lying about the space).

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** representing N>3 dimensions visually is impossible; demanding it rules out all analogies and contradicts the user's own "an analogy will be hard." The real defect of the bird's-eye isn't too-few-dimensions — it's the FALSE PROMISE OF GLOBAL VISIBILITY (you can see the whole shape from above). The honest requirement is therefore must-have #4: *no global simultaneous visibility* — the analogy must evoke a searcher with a LOCAL horizon in a space too large to see whole. Dimensionality is handled by dropping the whole-visibility promise, not by counting axes.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** many-dimensionality enters as the **local-horizon / no-global-visibility** must-have (a dropped false promise), not as a represent-N-dimensions demand. **No longer allowed:** requiring literal high-dim representation; treating dimensionality as a dimension count.

#### Ambiguity A4: Clouds merge but crown-shyness holds seams — which does the analogy require?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** pick one — either regions merge (clouds) or they hold boundaries (crown-shyness); an analogy can't show both without confusion.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** thinking space demonstrably does BOTH, and the corpus already names WHEN: routelister's individuation — **same concept-identity's manifestations merge; distinct identities hold seams.** A README and an implementation of "auth" merge into one identity (clouds); "auth" and "logging" hold a seam (crown-shyness). The analogy must show both, governed by sameness — and that this IS the individuation judgment is itself an enriching insight (the merge/seam decision is where the searcher decides "same concept or different?").

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** a HIGH-VALUE requirement — *the analogy shows BOTH merging (within-identity) and seam-holding (between-identities), governed by the sameness judgment (individuation).* **No longer allowed:** forcing a choice between merging and seams.

#### Ambiguity A5: Does the catalog name candidate analogies, or strictly stay requirements-only?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** strictly requirements-only — naming slime mold or watershed edges into proposing the analogy (violating the sequencing bound).

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** a SCORECARD's natural validation is to score known items against it — and the user himself supplied candidates (the bird's-eye; the three exemplars). Pre-SCORING named phenomena against the catalog (the bird's-eye fails 3 must-haves; watershed and slime mold pass the hard ones) DEMONSTRATES the scorecard works without CHOOSING the analogy — the choice (synthesis, trade-offs, the actual metaphor) stays the next inquiry's. The line: scoring-for-validation is in; selecting/synthesizing is out.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** include a **coverage observation** — named phenomena pre-scored against the must-haves as scorecard VALIDATION, explicitly deferring the choice; plus the note that a COMPOSITE (several analogies each carrying some requirements) may beat one master metaphor. **No longer allowed:** proposing/choosing the analogy; suppressing the validating pre-score out of over-caution.
