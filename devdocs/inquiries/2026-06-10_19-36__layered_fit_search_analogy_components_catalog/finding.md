## Finding Summary

- **The deliverable is a SCORECARD, not a flat list** — because your real need is to make "this analogy is bad" into "it fails must-haves X, Y, Z." Three tiers: **MUST-HAVE** (absence makes an analogy *actively misleading*, the way the bird's-eye is), **HIGH-VALUE** (absence makes it impoverished but usable), and a **VOCABULARY POOL** (raw material for the hunt, not requirements).
- **The five MUST-HAVES, each paired with the sin it forbids** (a reviewer catches a bad analogy faster by spotting one forbidden sin than by confirming five virtues):
  1. **Fit-search** — *forbids: aimless growth OR optimization to a fixed known peak.* Probe: *is the search fitting to a goal/constraints, not expanding toward nothing and not climbing to a pre-known summit?*
  2. **Non-stationary + path-dependent landscape** — *forbids: a static map.* Probe: *does the territory reshape as the searcher moves — and does the path taken change what's reachable next?* (Two probes, one must-have.)
  3. **Interleaved layers with back-pressure** — *forbids: sequential layer-completion (the relay).* Probe: *do meaning/structural/process co-revise each other, or does one finish before the next starts?*
  4. **Local horizon** — *forbids: global simultaneous visibility (the bird's-eye's false promise).* Probe: *can the whole space be seen at once, or only a local neighborhood?*
  5. **Pre-structured, non-uniform space** — *forbids: a blank canvas.* Probe: *does the space already have clusters and dead zones before the searcher arrives?*
- **Your candidate's flaw, diagnosed — and it's the scorecard's calibration:** the bird's-eye map fails #3 and #4 outright (the green→yellow→blue *relay*; the *bird's-eye* whole-shape promise) and #2 on path-dependence (it says "the shape changes" but renders it as a static picture). The salvageable keepers: layers-as-regions, traversal-as-fit-search, the shape mutating, and the back-pressure itself.

### 1. Why a scorecard, and what the tiers mean

"List the components" under-specifies the FORM. A flat list can't do the job the word "bad" implies — judging an analogy needs to know which components are *load-bearing* (their absence misleads) versus *decorative*. So the catalog is graded:

- **MUST-HAVE** — absence makes an analogy *actively misleading* (it teaches something false about traversal), the way the bird's-eye does. A candidate failing any must-have is "bad."
- **HIGH-VALUE** — absence makes the analogy *impoverished but still usable* (it just shows less).
- **VOCABULARY POOL** — named phenomena that are *raw material* for the hunt, not requirements themselves.

Each must-have ships as a **(virtue ⊕ forbidden-sin) pair with a yes/no probe**, because spotting one sin is faster than confirming five virtues. (The sins disqualify independently but aren't a partition — one bad analogy can commit several.)

### 6. The dimensionality worry, reframed

You worried thinking space is many-dimensional, so an analogy is hard. Many-dimensionality enters as a dropped false promise, not a dimension count. (And the cleanest dodge, for the hunt: pick a time-based source whose dimensionality is unbounded by nature.)

## Reasoning

**Why a scorecard rather than a list.** The user called the candidate "bad" and wants better — both are grading acts, and grading needs load-bearingness. The must/high-value/pool tiering is what converts a felt "this is bad" into "it fails must-haves 2, 3, 4."

**Why these five are the must-haves and not more.** A must-have is a property whose ABSENCE actively misleads (teaches something false), not merely whose presence enriches. The five are exactly the bird's-eye's instructive failures plus the canon's load-bearing commitments. Promoting every rich property (merging, drift, limits) to must-have would make the bar unmeetable and stall the hunt — so those are high-value.

**Significant kills.** *A flat feature list* — killed: can't grade. *Path-dependence as a sixth must-have* — killed by folding it into #2 (keeping the must-list minimal; it's a sub-clause with its own probe, not a separate axis). *Collapsing #2 and #4* — killed: they fail differently (a film of a changing blob still shows global visibility; a local-horizon walk on a static terrain still misses non-stationarity). *Dropping the high-value tier* — killed: loses the individuation and DNA-limits insights, the richest exemplar contributions. *Requiring literal high-dimensional representation* — killed: impossible; the real defect is the global-visibility false promise.

**Self-reference handling.** The catalog analogizes the system's own traversal, with a risk of self-flattering requirements. Guards: every must-have is a yes/no probe checkable against any candidate; the rubric is calibrated on a known-bad case (the bird's-eye must fail the must-haves the diagnosis names); and the analogy choice is deferred, not smuggled — the finding proposes no analogy.
