## Question

His voice note, verbatim in substance: as a human traversing the thinking space, I focus on one thing, then another — and at some point I *"just kind of remember there was something related to that; maybe I should go back and check it out, and merge these contexts."* We do this a lot. The question: this process that remembers there was something relevant — **is the main process SCANNING all the past things, or does each past thing have a SUBPROCESS in our brain that, when the current context is relevant to it, goes and NOTIFIES the current context?** He added: *"this difference is really important."*

## Finding Summary

- **The honest answer to the literal dichotomy is: neither exactly — but his second option is the better approximation, and loosely read it is essentially right.** The real architecture is **resonance**: the past doesn't watch — it's shaped so the present lights it up.
- At reminding time there is **no scan** (nothing visits the past items one by one) and **no active per-item watcher** (no little process burning effort per memory). The current context itself is the query; a past trace fires when its stored form overlaps what the present is carrying. **The watching is structure, not computation** — the encodings and links do the waiting, for free.
- **His (b) is credited on two counts:** receptivity really is per-item (each past thing has its own firing conditions), and the direction really is notification (past→present, unbidden). Its single miss is the watcher's nature — read loosely ("each past thing has its own firing conditions") his (b) IS this view; the correction targets only the literal reading ("it just GOES and notifies" — an agentive subprocess), and that literal reading matters because it misdirects design: it would have us build N daemons, when what resonance calls for is structured writing.
- **Scanning is real too — as the second mode, downstream.** Deliberate search exists (costly, attention-paying, used when cues are unreliable or stakes are high). The two modes form a **pipeline, not a rivalry**, and his own sentence contains the handoff: *"I just kind of remember"* (the cue-fired notice) → the decision — *"maybe I should go back"* — → the deliberate going-back and the merge. Between notice and act sits the project's own decision-slot rule: whatever retrieval returns feeds a choice; nothing auto-runs.
- **"This difference is really important" — confirmed, with a derivation.** The two architectures put their cost on opposite sides: scanning pays at READ time, per past item, out of the mover's scarce span, and grows with the past's size; resonance pays at WRITE time (links, cues, conditions) plus a small per-hit noticing cost. The deep distinction is **whose budget pays** — the mover's grasp vs the substrate's standing structure — not serial-versus-parallel.
- **The practical consequence is a writing discipline first** (the deliberate scales stay, and bounded substrate-paid lookup remains legal): if dissolved context is ever to return unbidden, **the record must be written resonance-ready — encoded with the cues future contexts will actually present.** A past thing stored without those cues never fires.
- **The project readout is three-tiered:** the record already WRITES per-item subscriptions at scale (43 `trigger:` conditions across 46 nascent seeds, plus every finding's re-open conditions), and it even WROTE a polling instruction (the seed index's own header: *"Re-scan nascent seeds' maturation-triggers when passing through"*) — **what it lacks is a standing observer.** The only two recorded firings happened because the runner performed the firing event with its own hands.

## Finding

The prior finding (the 13-08 context line) established how dissolved past context can be brought back: one retrieval discipline at three scales. All three are acts the mover undertakes. What that finding did not say — what it quietly presupposed — is where the impulse to undertake them comes from: what makes *"is there something relevant back there?"* arise at all. His question drills exactly into that open layer, and his dichotomy names the two candidate mechanisms.

### 1. The verdict — resonance, with his (b) credited

For human long-term remembering, the answer to the literal dichotomy is **neither exactly**.

Option (a) — the main process scanning all the past things — fails at scale by the project's own prior verdict on the full spiral: the cost grows quadratically, the whole working span gets spent on the past, and the selection layer floods. A serial sweep over everything you have ever thought is not what a brain can be doing every few seconds. Scanning exists — but only as the *deliberate* mode (below), over small candidate sets, when it's worth paying for.

Option (b) — a subprocess per past thing that notifies the current context — is much closer, and the correction is precise. What actually happens, on the standard account of memory retrieval: **the current context IS the query.** Whatever you are presently holding — the topic, the images, the goal — is broadcast against your stored past by the memory substrate itself; a past trace fires when its stored form overlaps the present cue. Psychology's names for the pieces: encoding-specificity (a memory is recallable through the cues it was stored with), spreading activation (activity flows along associative links), pattern-completion (a partial cue re-activates a whole stored pattern), and the involuntary-memory literature (unbidden remindings are frequent and normal). **[Evidence grade: general knowledge — this specific literature is NOT yet in the project's harvest record; the harvested paper 1 did absorb adjacent notification-side mechanics — salience-tagging, selective replay, synaptic tagging-and-capture — so the family is partly owned. A working model at literature grade, not certainty.]**

So his (b) is right that receptivity is **per-item** — each past thing genuinely has its own firing conditions — and right about the **direction** — the past announces itself to the present, unbidden. Its single miss is the watcher's nature: there is no active subprocess doing the watching, no standing computation per trace. **The watching is structure, not computation** — the past doesn't watch; it's shaped so the present lights it up. Read loosely, his (b) already IS this view in informal words; the correction targets only the literal reading (*"it just GOES and notifies"*), and the literal reading matters because it misdirects design — it would have us build N little daemons, when the resonance architecture calls for something else entirely (section 3).

The name used here — **resonance** — is the record's own word: the project's retrieval germ p29-S1 already speaks of *"reading which nodes resonate."*

### 2. The pipeline, and why the difference is really important

**The two modes hand off; they don't compete.** Deliberate search is real: attention-paying, costly, chosen when cues are unreliable or the stakes are high. His own sentence contains the whole pipeline: *"I just kind of remember"* — the cue-fired notice, effortless and unbidden — then *"maybe I should go back and check it out"* — a decision, then a deliberate act — then *"merge these contexts"* — the deliberate work the notice only initiated. Between notice and act sits the project's own standing rule, verbatim: *"whatever retrieval returns feeds a choice; nothing auto-runs."* The notification initiates; selection decides; the deliberate mode executes.

**The cost derivation — his importance claim, confirmed on owned lines.** Scanning pays at **read time**: per past item, out of the mover's span — *"retrieval costs grasp"* — and the bill grows with the size of the past (quadratic at full form: the spiral verdict). Resonance pays at **write time** — links, distinctive cues, registered conditions — plus a small **per-hit noticing cost**: it is not free, since a hit (including a false one) costs attention to receive and judge. Two precisions matter. First, **resonance replaces the FINDING cost, not the RETRIEVING cost**: once notified, going back and re-loading still spends grasp; what's saved is the per-item search to learn that something relevant exists at all. Second — the sharpest form of the distinction — **the question is whose budget pays**: the mover's grasp (scarce, span-bounded) or the substrate's structure (cheap, standing). An implemented resonance — say, a similarity lookup over the record — still computes over items, but it spends substrate budget; at the mover's ledger it is scan-free. Serial-versus-parallel was never the real axis; mover-budget-versus-substrate-budget is.

**The consequence: a writing discipline first.** Resonance fires only on overlap — a past thing encoded without the cues its future contexts will present **never fires**; failed reminding is the everyday proof. So if dissolved context is ever to return unbidden, the record must be **written resonance-ready: encoded with the cues future contexts will actually carry** — links, distinctive phrasings, conditions stated in tomorrow's vocabulary. The deliberate scales stay, and bounded substrate-paid lookup remains the legal form of scanning; but the architecture question turns out to decide, first of all, how to *write*.

### 3. The project readout — three tiers, and the observer gap

Held against both architectures, the project's machinery reads honestly like this:

**The deliberate mode is operational at three grains** (the prior finding's own statuses): the capped backward touch is wired (the pipeline's warm loop), the index scan is manual (this dive's greps were index scans by hand), the second line is designed with zero runs. The periodic-sweep germs (the global demote pass; the integrity sweep) are polling-shaped by design — same family.

**The cue-fired mode exists at three tiers, and stops one short:**

1. **Subscriptions are written at scale.** 43 of the 46 nascent seeds in `devdocs/seeds/_seed.md` carry a literal `trigger:` condition — a registered, per-item firing condition phrased as a future project event ("the first bar-i venture begins"; "the meaningful-traversal spec is written"). Every finding's Refinement Triggers section adds more. This is (b)-architecture on paper — per-item receptivity, declared.
2. **A polling instruction is written too.** The seed index's own header says, verbatim: *"Re-scan nascent seeds' maturation-triggers when passing through."* The record even wrote itself the checking rule — a polling convention, executed only when a dive happens to pass through, and itself scan-family.
3. **No standing observer exists.** Nothing holds current context against the registered conditions as a matter of course. The only two recorded firings — one seed's trigger, twice — happened because the runner *performed the firing event with its own hands* (revising the analogy family) and knew the subscription; no other firing is recorded (a recorded absence, not a proven never).

One honesty rider: the subscriptions' encoding looks **half-decent by inspection** — the conditions use canon vocabulary a future run's context would plausibly contain — but the match-rate is **unmeasured** (nobody has tested whether trigger phrasings actually meet future contexts), and an observer would itself be the instrument that measures it. Either way the sequence is fixed: **the windows exist; their observer doesn't.** In the project's own Popperian vocabulary: the prediction windows are written; nothing watches them.

Two germs already wait on exactly that gap: **p29-S1** (propagate a query through the concept-map link-graph and read which nodes resonate — the resonance form) and **p1-S5** (owned, from the harvested neuroscience: a strong finding's landing triggers re-evaluation of its nascent neighbors — the narrowest observer shape).

### 4. What could close the gap — a field, enumerated, not chosen

Six candidates, each grounded in owned structure; all user-gated; every one delivers its hits into the decision-slot; none is a daemon. **This finding picks none of them.**

- **(α) Event-wired checking** — when the runner's own hand performs a canon edit / venture start / family revision, grep the registered windows for that event-class. (Generalizes the two recorded firings — the natural experiment. Small event-time read; zero standing cost.)
- **(β) A per-run resonance pass** — at run start or run end, hold the current context against the index/record by substrate-paid matching; hits go to the decision-slot. (The budget-currency frame's legal bounded scan.)
- **(γ) Write-side cue discipline** — encode-for-future-cues as a writing convention: conditions phrased in the vocabulary future contexts will carry; distinctive cues. (The cheapest lever — pure writing; raises every other member's hit-rate.)
- **(δ) The narrowest observer first** — mechanize p1-S5: on a strong finding landing, re-read the *neighbors'* triggers only. (Owned germ; landing-triggered; too scoped to flood anything.)
- **(ε) Propagate-and-read** — p29-S1's link-graph resonance. (Needs the concept-map graphs machine-walked; today they are hand-authored and read.)
- **(ζ) The null option** — keep the human as the observer. (Today's tier-honest reality; works when events pass through the operator's hands; conditions outside them stay dark.)

**Assembled in one line: retrieval has two initiators — deliberate and cue-fired — and the cue-fired one is bought at write time.** Unbidden return is a writing discipline.
