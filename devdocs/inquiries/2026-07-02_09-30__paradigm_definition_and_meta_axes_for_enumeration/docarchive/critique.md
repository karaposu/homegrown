### C1 — The definition + membership test + NOT-list
**Prosecution (strongest):** (i) *the grammar case:* a grammatical paradigm is JUST a table — where's the "bundle of commitments"? (ii) *"cohering per whom?"* — the ≥2-cohering-choices test smuggles judgment. (iii) *the margin case:* isn't "dark mode" a micro-paradigm of UI theming?
**Defense:** (i) the inflection table IS a commitment-bundle carried by an exemplar: "follows -are conjugation" commits the speaker to a cohering SYSTEM of endings across cases — the bundle is implicit IN the exemplar; grammar is the exemplar-FORWARD carrier of the same structure Kuhn's matrix carries commitment-forward. The two-sided definition already accommodates both carriers. (ii) "cohering" is operationalized by the exemplar/practitioner evidence in practice: choices cohere when practitioners actually hold them together toward the paradigm's own criterion — borderline cases exist and were already admitted (graded membership per the prototype-theory ground). (iii) the margin case SPLITS under the test and illustrates the boundary perfectly: "dark mode" the toggle = one choice = an option; "**dark-first design**" = a practice-bundle (contrast strategy, elevation-via-lightness, OLED economics) = a genuine paradigm of the theming practice. Same words, two referents.
**Collision:** all three prosecutions produce presentation obligations, not damage.
**Verdict: SURVIVE with refinements applied:** **(R1a)** the membership test's wording: *"cohering = held together in real practice, mutually supporting toward the paradigm's own criterion"*; **(R1b)** the exemplar-forward vs commitment-forward carrier note (grammar vs Kuhn — one structure, two carriers); **(R1c)** the dark-mode/dark-first pair enters the finding as the boundary's illustration.

### C2 — The scope (practices + step-zero)
**Prosecution (strongest):** the hardest object-domain asks: *"paradigms of mathematics"* — is math a practice, or an object-domain with structure-paradigms? *"paradigms of history"* — historiography resolves to practice, but **"the feudal paradigm"** names a PERIOD'S organizing pattern, not anyone's way-of-working. Does practice-attachment need a second attachment point?
**Defense:** mathematics resolves cleanly — constructive vs classical proof practice, set-theoretic vs category-theoretic foundations are WAYS OF DOING math (and their split is largely on the CRITERION facet: what counts as a valid proof). Historiography likewise (Marxist / Annales / great-man). The feudal-paradigm case is genuinely different: paradigm-as-**era-pattern** (a system's organizing configuration) rather than paradigm-as-way-of-a-practice.
**Collision:** the era-pattern use is real and does NOT fit the practice-attachment — but it also isn't the enumeration-target: nobody runs paradigm-DETECTION to generate new feudalisms… except when a practice takes the patterns up (worldbuilding; institution design) — at which point the era-patterns become families in THAT practice's map. So: no second attachment point in the core concept; a **penumbra note** instead.
**Verdict: SURVIVE with refinement applied:** **(R2)** the finding carries the penumbra: *"paradigm" is also used for system-level organizing patterns (the feudal paradigm); those uses are adjacent, not enumeration-targets — and they re-enter the core exactly when a practice (worldbuilding, institutional design) takes them as material.* The scope's honest-failure property is strengthened, not weakened.
