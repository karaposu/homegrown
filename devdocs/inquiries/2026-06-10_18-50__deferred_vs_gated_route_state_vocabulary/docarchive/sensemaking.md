## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

#### Ambiguity A1: Is the user right that DEFERRED is the wrong word — and what exactly is the difference?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** the words are near-synonyms; both mean "not now, later" — the distinction is pedantry, and DEFERRED reads fine.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the corpus's own template assigns them DIFFERENT definitions with different machinery: DEFERRED items carry revival triggers a chooser re-judges ("deliberately postponed"); GATED items carry conditions that release automatically ("do not act until the MUST resolves"). A ledger entry marked "deferred" asserts somebody weighed and postponed — for gate-held routes that assertion is FALSE (nobody chose; the precondition is unmet). In a record system whose value is auditability, a word that lies about agency is a defect, not pedantry.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** the user is right; the difference = **agency** (chosen vs ineligible) × **trigger-nature** (judgment-invitation vs self-releasing condition). **No longer allowed:** disposition-words for dependency-states anywhere in the gate texts.

#### Ambiguity A2: What is the replacement tag?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** WAITING-ON is plainer and friendlier than GATED; pick readability over pedigree.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** GATED is already the corpus's word for exactly this state (the COULD-pattern, verbatim), the holding mechanisms are literally named GATES (a gate-held route being "gated" is self-description), and the collision sweep is clean. WAITING-ON would add a synonym where one word already serves — the exact two-names-one-thing disease the day's naming work kept killing. Readability is preserved by always naming the condition: `gated-on: meaning-stability(concept)`.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** **GATED-on(condition)** — with the condition always named. **No longer allowed:** new synonyms; bare "gated" without its condition in ledger entries.

#### Ambiguity A3: The taxonomy — and does BLOCKED fold into GATED?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** both are dependency-states; one word (GATED) covers them; three states + parked is simpler than four.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** internality changes what a chooser can DO: a gated route is unlockable from inside (run the dive; stabilize the meaning — the condition is workable); a blocked route waits on the world (a ship-date, the user's decision) and cannot be hurried. Folding them deletes exactly the information a Selector needs at choosing time — and the template already uses both words with these flavors.

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution — the four-word map:** **DEFERRED** (chosen; judgment-revival) / **GATED-on(condition)** (precondition-unmet; self-releasing; workable from inside) / **BLOCKED-on(external)** (outside dependency; not hurryable) / **PARKED** (the ledger ACTION; entries are parked-AS-state). Checkpoint behavior falls out: gated entries are POLLED; deferred entries are RAISED for re-judgment. **No longer allowed:** folding the states; "parked" used as a state.

#### Ambiguity A4: What happens to the guarantee's name and the plain-English "deferral"?

The guarantee's real content is the ledger's promise, which "PARKED ≠ LOST" states more accurately anyway (covering all three states, not just gate-holds).

**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** the guarantee = **"parked ≠ lost"**; the name-section's semantics re-word without the word family: *"lazy, gated progression on decoupled clocks — the meaning lane progresses eagerly; the artifact lane advances when its release condition fires."* The purge applies only near tag contexts (the principle's texts); general prose elsewhere keeps ordinary English "defer." **No longer allowed:** the tag-word as prose inside the principle's own texts.
