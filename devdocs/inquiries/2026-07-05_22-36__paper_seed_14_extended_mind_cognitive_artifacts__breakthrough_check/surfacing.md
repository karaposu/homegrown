## Part 1 — The paper's claims as FACTs (territory 1: paper 14, verbatim where load-bearing)

- **A-1 [core] EXTERNAL ARTIFACTS GENUINELY ENHANCE COGNITION.** FACT. The mind is capacity-limited ("there are limits to its capacity to remember, calculate, navigate, plan, and learn"); we "create and use artifacts that scaffold us in performing cognitive tasks"; they "complement… the information-storage and processing abilities of our embodied brains by proving [providing] task-relevant information."
- **A-2 [core] OFFLOADING FIXES FLEETING THOUGHT.** FACT. "Information held in conscious thought is often fleeting and so one advantage of externalizing thought is that it becomes fixed"; and externalizing lets us "perform operations on the externalized information that are very hard, if not impossible, to perform in the brain." Offloading is ancient (clay tablets, written language).
- **A-3 [core] PERSONAL-VIEW vs SYSTEM-VIEW (Norman).** FACT. An artifact doesn't improve biological memory; what it "really do[es]… is transform one cognitive task into another" (retrieve-from-biological-memory → retrieve-from-the-list). "Only when we take a systems perspective, can we speak of enhanced cognitive abilities" — pilot-plus-checklist is enhanced "because it is more reliable."
- **A-4 [core] THE EXTENDED MIND / OTTO'S NOTEBOOK (Clark & Chalmers).** FACT. External info is constitutive of a cognitive state given "trust and glue": "a high degree of trust, reliance, and accessibility, and we must have endorsed it at some point in the past." The notebook plays "the same causal roles as information stored in his biological memory."
- **A-5 [core] EMBEDDED vs EXTENDED — the ontological split.** FACT. "There is an ontological difference between embedded and extended cognitive systems regarding the location of the physical substrate that realizes cognition." Extended/distributed: scaffolding is "constitutive of a cognitive state or process." Embedded (Rupert): artifacts "have important roles in human cognitive functioning but are never part of cognition."
- **A-6 [core] DIMENSIONS OF INTEGRATION (Heersmink's own account).** FACT. Integration is "a matter of degree… a multidimensional phenomenon" varying along: "the kind and intensity of information flow between agent and scaffold, the accessibility of the scaffold, the durability of the coupling…, the amount of trust…, the degree of transparency-in-use, the ease with which the information can be interpreted, the amount of personalization, and the amount of cognitive transformation." Dense on these = extended; shallow = embedded.
- **A-7 [supporting] OUTSOURCING DIMINISHES ONBOARD CAPACITY.** FACT. Nav systems "decrease the level of detail in our internal cognitive maps"; Sparrow 2011 / Google effect: "when we know information is easily available externally we tend to put less effort into memorizing it"; Carr: technology "makes us stupid"; Socrates-on-writing.
- **A-8 [supporting] TRANSACTIVE MEMORY (Wegner).** FACT. Dyads/groups develop "a cognitive system shared by people… developed for encoding, storing, and retrieving information"; the married couple jointly reconstructs "Desert Song" via mutual cue-giving.
- **A-9 [core-to-paper, peripheral-to-us] THE MORAL SUPERSTRUCTURE (the paper's actual thesis).** FACT. Moral status of artifacts scales with "degree of dependency and integration"; personal identity is partly constituted by one's artifacts (Otto's notebook "a central part of his identity as a cognitive agent"; SenseCam life-narrative); enhancement ethics (authenticity / fairness / equality / the fuzzy treatment-vs-enhancement line).
