## Paper FACTs (A-n)

- **A-1 — the paper is a MEASUREMENT/psychometrics review.** It catalogs five statistical indices of how well confidence judgments track performance. Not a theory of monitoring; a comparison of ways to MEASURE monitoring quality. Source-type: a methods review (weaker than a theory).
- **A-2 — ABSOLUTE accuracy** (the "calibration index", Formula 1, ~132-154): mean squared deviation between confidence and performance per item. "Measures judgment precision." REQUIRES item-level ground truth (was the answer correct?).
- **A-3 — RELATIVE accuracy** (Formula 2, ~155-175): a correlation (Pearson r / gamma) between a SET of confidence judgments and performance. THE KEY DISTINCTION (~173-175): "an individual who makes consistent confident judgments may be high with respect to relative accuracy without necessarily being high with respect to absolute accuracy." Absolute and relative are DISSOCIABLE — good at one, bad at the other.
- **A-4 — BIAS** (Formula 3, ~177-195): signed (not squared) mean deviation → direction (over- vs under-confidence) + magnitude of the lack of fit.
- **A-5 — SCATTER** (Formula 4, ~196-213): variability difference between confidence for correct vs incorrect responses.
- **A-6 — DISCRIMINATION** (Formula 5, ~214-245): mean confidence on correct minus mean confidence on incorrect items. "Ability to distinguish between correct and incorrect outcomes."
- **A-7 — HYBRID scores** (Brier, ~246-262): combine absolute-accuracy + scatter; the author PREFERS individual scores ("they necessitate a more precise interpretation of the underlying metacognitive process").
- **A-8 — the three recommendations** (~342-419): (1) understand the CONSTRUCT being measured; (2) SELECT the measure matching the construct/goal; (3) use MULTIPLE measures to TRIANGULATE.
- **A-9 — the paper's own FRONTIER** (~425-433): "there is no model which attempts simultaneously to link metacognitive processes such as monitoring to a corresponding theory of how to measure these processes. Such a model would be indispensable to the future study of metacognition." Also: "virtually nothing is known about the relationships between the different types of outcome scores" (~318-320); predictive-validity gap (~445-456).
- **A-10 — QUARANTINE (reverse-lens, LARGE):** all five formulae + the Brier hybrid + reliability statistics (Cronbach's alpha, Monte Carlo) + the empirical apparatus (1-100 confidence scales, item-level correct/incorrect scoring, delayed-JOL effects). Non-transferable to a harness with no numeric confidence-performance dataset and no per-item ground-truth oracle.
