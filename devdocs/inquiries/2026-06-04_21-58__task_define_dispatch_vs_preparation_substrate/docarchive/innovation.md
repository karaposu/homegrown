### P1 — Premise-and-Concept Bundle

**Piece question:** Is the always-invoke premise accepted, and what is the corrected concept name with three-axis justification?

#### Principal candidate (elaborated premise + concept commitment)

The user's **always-invoke premise** — that the runner always invokes `/surfacing` and therefore no dispatch decision exists — is **structurally accepted**. Three convergent grounds support acceptance:

- **/Surfacing's lightweight design.** /Surfacing's spec describes the discipline as lightweight; its cost on an empty-territory invocation is bounded (quick empty result). Conditional-invocation would require additional decision-machinery at the runner layer without clear benefit. Always-invoke is consistent with /surfacing's design.
- **Cost-bounded empty invocations.** For a self-contained task (e.g., "explain pure functions in JavaScript"), /surfacing invoked with vacuous purpose returns empty quickly. The runner gets an explicit empty-confirmation rather than an unattended skip; this is structurally cleaner.
- **Runner-architecture knowledge.** The user works directly with the project's runner; their domain knowledge of the actual invocation pattern is the most direct evidence available at this layer.

Under the accepted always-invoke premise, **"dispatch substrate" is precisely-wrong** as the framing of MQ2's answer's substrate-role. "Dispatch" is a technical term with a specific meaning — runtime routing/decision among options. With no decision to make (invocation is unconditional), no dispatch happens. The term is misapplied.

The corrected concept is **"preparation substrate."** The name is selected via three convergent justifications:

- **Function-aligned.** MQ2's answer prepares `/surfacing`'s input (purpose + territory + bias formulation by the runner) — preparation, not routing.
- **User-language-aligned.** User explicitly said "prepare things in very lightweight way" — "preparation" maps directly to user's framing.
- **Lightweight-connoted.** Preparing ≠ executing or routing; the noun phrase carries the lightness that Task-Define's lightweight-stance requires.

Alternative concept names considered and rejected on structural grounds:

- **"Input substrate"** — terse and function-accurate but loses the lightweight nuance; sounds more like data-passing than prepared content.
- **"Framing substrate"** — too broad; framing connotes context-setting beyond MQ2's per-item scope.
- **"Pre-shape input"** — awkward as a noun phrase; less linguistically clean.
- **"Lightweight-priors"** — emphasizes lightness over function; function should lead naming for architectural precision.

**Reversal direction (a): premise rejected.** Maybe /surfacing isn't always invoked; some runner pattern uses conditional invocation. Tested at sensemaking Ambiguity 1; rejected on structural defensibility (lightweight design + cost-bounded empty + runner-architecture knowledge). 

**Reversal direction (b): keep "dispatch substrate" with semantic broadening.** Tested at sensemaking Ambiguity 2; rejected on precision-loss cost (broadening "dispatch" introduces architectural ambiguity).

**Reversal direction (c): alternative concept name.** Tested via Load-bearing concept test on "preparation substrate"; alternatives rejected on (input loses lightweight nuance; framing too broad; pre-shape awkward; lightweight-priors emphasizes lightness over function).
