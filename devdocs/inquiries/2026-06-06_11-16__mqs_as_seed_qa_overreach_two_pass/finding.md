### 2. The overreach concern — real, with PERMISSION-not-CONSTRAINT mitigations

The user's concern was: at cold context, articulate_simple is guessing the A when the A requires project-specific context articulate doesn't have. The example illustrated the worry; let's look at the structural state.

Articulate_simple's substrate (per `2026-06-03_15-39` + `2026-06-04_07-48`) is:

- The task statement (always)
- The LLM's general knowledge (always)
- Warm session context if present (per `2026-06-05_20-02` anti-fetching boundary)

No specific project artifacts. No code-base reads. No file-system probes. Cold context = task + general knowledge only.

At cold context, two mitigation rules already exist:

- **MQ2 hypothetical-relational mode** (from `2026-06-04_21-12`): MQ2's substrate is expressed in *"tasks of this kind typically have these artifacts available; if so, they would bear on..."* mode. The A is a hypothesis-set, not an assertion-set.
- **MQ4 cold-empty-valid rule** (from `2026-06-06_10-37`): MQ4's output may be empty when no exclusions perceivable. Empty is valid, not a failure.

Both are real and load-bearing. But both are **PERMISSIONS**, not **CONSTRAINTS**. They ALLOW the safe behavior (hypothetical framing; empty emission). They don't FORCE it. An LLM at cold context can still:

- Phrase its MQ2 A in assertive mode despite the hypothetical-relational permission
- Emit a confident MQ4 A despite the empty-valid permission
- Confidently answer a Q whose A requires project knowledge the LLM doesn't have

This is the **rules-as-permissions-not-constraints** property. Naming it explicitly is part of honest acknowledgment. The mitigations help; they don't solve.

The user's example is INTRINSIC (the "from scratch" signal is IN the task statement). Per the just-committed MQ4 finding, intrinsic exclusions are handled by **MQ3 + MQ-aggregate-resolution** via the §13 Example C "from-scratch" pattern, not by MQ4. The user's routing of the example to MQ4 was a structural slip. Gently corrected.

**But the user's broader concern survives the routing fix.** The overreach property is general — it applies to any Substrate-MQ at cold context. The example illustrates the pattern, even if it mis-routes the specific operation.

### 3. The structurally correct resolution — the two-pass design

The user proposed articulate2 + staged-surfacing as the resolution: *"a initial surfacing will be followed by articulate's second staege and it will be followed by another surfacing stage."*

This design isn't new. It was committed at `devdocs/inquiries/2026-06-05_00-11__task_define_two_pass_with_surfacing_between/finding.md` and explicitly deferred at §9 of the meaning-layer doc:

> *"The two-pass design — where articulate runs, then /surfacing fires, then articulate runs again on a refined preparation substrate — is a separate construction. Articulate_simple covers everything that happens before the first /surfacing invocation."*

The design's structural logic is exactly the resolution of the overreach concern:

1. **Pass 1** (articulate_simple) runs on cold context — task statement + general knowledge. Produces MQ outputs in hypothetical-relational mode where appropriate; produces empty MQ4 where extrinsic boundaries aren't perceivable. **The Qs are confident; the As are honestly hypothetical or empty.**

2. **/surfacing fires** — using the pass-1 MQ2 substrate (and MQ4 if non-empty) as the guidance for what kinds of context to surface. **Project context is loaded.**

3. **Pass 2** (articulate2) runs with the broader substrate now available: task statement + general knowledge + warm context from pass-1 + /surfacing-supplied project context. The hypothetical-relational hypotheses can now be **confirmed, refined, or revised**; the empty MQ4 can now be **populated with extrinsic exclusions visible in project state**.

The 2-pass architecture **decouples Q-generation from A-confirmation**. The cold-context substrate is sufficient for Q-generation (perceiving what to ask). The warm-context-after-/surfacing substrate is what A-confirmation requires. Without the split, articulate has to guess A's at cold context (overreach). With the split, articulate1 honestly produces Qs + hypothetical As, /surfacing brings the context, articulate2 finalizes the As.

This IS the resolution the user proposed. The architectural intuition was right.
