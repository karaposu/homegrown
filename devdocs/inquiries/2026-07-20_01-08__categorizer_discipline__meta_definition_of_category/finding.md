# Finding: the categorizer — a meta-definition of category, and how one is chosen for an entry

## Question

*"Tell me what a 'categorizer' thinking discipline would mean — like a meta-definition of category: what it is and what it is not. Let's dive deep. And it should contain meta-insight about judging categories to pick one for an entry, based on what axis concepts a categorizer would choose."* A standalone, meaning-layer ask (what the discipline IS — not a spec, not a process).

## Finding Summary

- **A category is a named region in a chosen axis-space — purpose-relative, with a membership judgment attached.** Three things make it: an axis (or several) someone chose, a boundary someone drew, and a name that lets one word do the filing.
- **What it is not, five ways:** not a score, not a natural fact found lying around, not a bare label, not a pile, and not the thing's identity.
- **The categorizer is the discipline that places entries:** given an entry, a purpose, and a category-system, judge the entry's home region — under the system's membership regime ("pick one" is the partition regime, the focal case; some systems legally allow stacked membership).
- **The meta-insight, in its honest form: categorizing is two stacked judgments — first which AXIS governs, then which region on it** — and mis-categorization is usually an axis error, not a bin error. The axis judgment is mostly pre-made at design time; it re-opens exactly at the hard cases (faceted systems, competing systems, misfits) — which is where a categorizer earns its name.
- **The axis-concepts a categorizer chooses by compressed honestly to three questions and one gate:** the handling question (primary — along which difference will the consumer DO something different?), the stability question (will the entry's value hold over its life?), the asymmetry question (which mis-filing is unrecoverable?) — all gated by the system's kind (closed/generated vs open/practice).
- **Six ways it fails,** each violating a named part: the axis error, the pile, the score-smuggle, the forced fit, the bare label — and reification (the once-right cut held after the purpose moved).
- Working-definition grade: zero runs as a named discipline; every part is exercised in this project only as embedded practice. No seeds (the definition is this finding's own content).

## Finding

The project defines its thinking disciplines by what they ARE as cognitive operations — a verb, a unit, a NOT-list, a mechanism-meaning, failure modes. This finding gives the categorizer that treatment at the meaning layer only, anchored in the project's own working category-devices and framed by the general theory of categories (every general-theory claim below is marked as such).

### 1. What a category is

**A category is a named region in a chosen axis-space — purpose-relative, with a membership judgment attached.**

In plain words: someone picked a dimension — or several — along which things differ (the **axis-space**: one axis, or many axes with a notion of nearness across them); drew a line (the **boundary** — a membership test where the edge is sharp, or a distance-to-the-typical-case where membership is graded [general theory: prototype categories]); and gave the region a **name**, so one word can do the filing. All three are constitutive: without an axis, nothing to differ along; without a boundary, nothing in or out; without a name, no use in passing.

**Purpose-relative, twice grounded.** By this project's own practice: the individuation doctrine — *"there is no goal-free answer… the goal fixes the resolution"* — the same two things merge under one purpose and split under another. And by theorem [general knowledge]: the ugly-duckling result — without a purpose's weighting of features, any two items share equally many predicates; similarity, and therefore category, does not exist unweighted. **Categories are made for purposes; they are not found lying around.**

One honest boundary case, accepted rather than patched: a numeric interval ("temperature 20–30°") satisfies the definition — a named region on an axis with a perfectly explicit test. That is right: **binning is categorizing at its most literal.** Most categories differ from the interval by having richer axis-spaces and fuzzier edges — not by kind.

**What a category is NOT — five members, each with its ground:**

1. **Not a score.** A category sorts; it never grades [owned rule: types-never-scores]. "Urgent" as a bin is a category; "8/10 urgency" is a measurement wearing a bin's clothes.
2. **Not a natural fact found lying around.** The axis was chosen — see the double grounding above.
3. **Not a bare label.** A name without a membership judgment decorates; it does not categorize. Live in-house instance: `docs/discipline_taxonomy.md` — cited in a discipline spec as "the four-category taxonomy" — does not exist; the name has carried weight with no system behind it.
4. **Not a pile.** A category that excludes nothing does no work; half of categorizing is the reasoned Excluded list [owned practice].
5. **Not the thing's identity.** WHAT something is (individuation — same-or-different) comes before WHERE it files.

### 2. What the categorizer does — verb, unit, NOT-list

**The verb.** To categorize is: given an **entry**, a **purpose**, and a **category-system**, judge which named region the entry belongs to — **under the system's membership regime.** The regime is a property of the system, not of category itself: **partition** systems give every entry exactly one home — "pick one for an entry," the focal case of this question — while **composite-legal** systems allow stacked membership (this project's yield-kinds are one). **The unit:** the assignment — one entry placed, with its ground stated (which axis governed; which test passed).

**What the categorizer is NOT:**
- **Not individuation.** Identity is upstream — you must know what the entry is before filing it.
- **Not ranking or scoring.** A placement is not a grade.
- **Not naming or minting.** It uses given names; coining is another act.
- **Not clustering.** Direction of fit: clustering lets the data draw the boundaries; categorizing holds the boundaries and places items.
- **Not system-design.** Choosing the system's axes is the designer's act.

**The misfit nuance (where the last two NOTs earn their edges).** When no region fits: in a **closed/generated** system — one whose categories a generator produces, covering by construction — the misfit is evidence about the generator; signal it, never cram. In an **open/practice** system — instance-grounded, extensible by its own rule — an instance-grounded extension is legal [owned precedents: "add new shapes as evidence accumulates"; the thirteenth yield-kind admitted from the record]. One entry CAN move a boundary — but only by the system's own extension rule, never by free redrawing. That is the honest overlap with clustering, and its limit.

### 3. How the categorizer judges — the meta-insight

**Categorizing an entry is two stacked judgments.**

**First, the axis judgment: which dimension of variation governs THIS entry's filing.** The axis-concepts a categorizer chooses by — tested down from five candidates to **three questions and one gate**:

1. **The handling question (primary): along which difference will the CONSUMER do something different?** A category system earns its keep only if entries in different categories get handled differently — filing that changes no downstream handling is decoration. Two corollaries: **mis-categorization is usually an axis error, not a bin error** (filed by topic when the consumer needed urgency — every bin on the wrong axis is wrong at once); and the handling question contains its own **level sub-question** — at what level along the chosen dimension does handling actually differ? Too fine, every entry is its own bin; too coarse, one pile. (The "basic-level category" idea [general theory] is this sub-question's classic form; ease-of-saying mostly folds in too, since say-ability serves retrieval, which serves handling.)
2. **The stability question: will the entry's value on this axis hold over the entry's expected life?** A fast-changing attribute is a bad filing axis for a durable record — the filing outlives the fact.
3. **The asymmetry question: which mis-filing is unrecoverable?** Choose the axis — and the uncertainty rule at its boundaries — so the costly miss is the one avoided [owned logic, transferred: over-merge hides, over-split stays visible; lean toward the recoverable error].

**The gate (not a criterion): the system's kind.** Within a system: in a closed/generated one the axes are the generator's — not choosable per-entry; in an open/practice one, the practice's attested axes are the menu. (Between systems — when the same item could file into more than one system — the kind joins the reasons; that is the two-level honesty.)

**Then, the region judgment: where on the chosen axis the entry falls.** The membership test at the boundary; distance-to-the-typical-case where membership is graded [general theory]; and at uncertain boundaries, the uncertainty rule comes from the asymmetry question — not from a universal default.

**The honesty clause — integral, not a footnote.** In most working systems the axis was fixed at design time (this project's seed types; its route verbs), the categorizer inherits it, and the daily act is the region judgment — the stack collapses to one live judgment. **The axis judgment re-opens per-entry at exactly three moments,** all real in this project's own record: **faceted systems** (an entry located on several axes at once — the route-type's three facets; which facet governs a given consultation is decided per use); **competing systems** (the same item could file into the seeds index, canon, or memory — a recurring real judgment here); and **misfits** (no region fits — the thirteenth-yield-kind moment). So the axis judgment is **the deep judgment, mostly pre-made, re-opened at the hard cases — which is exactly where a categorizer earns its name.**

### 4. How it fails, and where it sits

**Six failure modes, each violating a named part (meaning-layer sketch):**
- **The axis error** — filed on the wrong dimension; every bin wrong at once; the consumer mis-served wholesale. [Violates the axis judgment.]
- **The pile** — a catch-all absorbing everything; no exclusion, no work. [Violates not-a-pile.]
- **The score-smuggle** — grades dressed as types. [Violates not-a-score.]
- **The forced fit** — a misfit crammed into a closed system's nearest bin instead of signaled. [Violates the misfit gate.]
- **The bare label** — assignment to a name with no system behind it. [Violates not-a-bare-label; the in-house instance stands.]
- **Reification** — the once-right cut held after the purpose moved; treating the chosen axis as the world's own joints. [Violates not-a-natural-fact; the axis error's time-shifted cousin.]

**Kinship (light).** Individuation is the upstream sibling (identity before filing). The project's route-type is a working faceted system; its seed gate is live per-entry assignment; tag-vs-category [general] names the two membership regimes. The grade: a **working definition** — zero runs as a named discipline; every part exercised here only as embedded practice.

**In one line: a category is a purpose's way of cutting the world so that different cuts license different handling — and the categorizer is the judge of where an entry falls once the cut is chosen, and of when the cut itself must be re-chosen.** (Tighter: categories are cuts; categorizers place entries — and re-judge cuts at the hard cases.)

## Reasoning

- **The region formulation held against three rivals:** a category as a RULE alone (a bare predicate — no filing use without axis and name), as a LIST of members (breaks on new entries — a category must receive the unseen), as a NAME alone (the bare-label is-not). The region subsumes what is right in each: the rule is its boundary, the name its handle, the members its current occupants.
- **The interval counter-example was accepted, not patched** — binning is categorizing at its most literal; a definition that excluded it would be gerrymandered.
- **The criteria compressed from five to three-plus-gate, honestly:** "grain" folded into the handling question's level sub-question; "system-kind" reclassified as the gate (within a system it constrains choice rather than motivating it; between systems it joins the reasons — stated). Stability and asymmetry resisted absorption (time-facing and cost-facing — handling today can be identical while either differs).
- **"The categorizer is a clusterer run one entry at a time" — rejected with a sharpened edge:** direction of fit separates the pure cases; the open-system extension is the honest overlap, bounded by the system's own rule.
- **"One judgment only (the axis is never the categorizer's)" — conceded locally, refuted generally:** in single-axis closed systems the stack collapses; the three re-opening moments are real, owned occurrences — the stack is the general form.
- **The sixth failure mode (reification) was added by the adversarial pass:** the not-a-natural-fact is-not had no violation mode; its practical violation (the once-right cut held after the purpose moved) is distinct from the axis error (wrong-now vs right-once-kept-too-long).
- **What survived:** the definition with its three constituents and five is-nots; the verb with the regime note; the two stacked judgments with the honesty clause; the three-questions-plus-gate; the six modes; every general-theory claim marked.
