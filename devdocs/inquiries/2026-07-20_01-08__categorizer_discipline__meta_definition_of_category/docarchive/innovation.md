## P1 — The category meta-definition (finding-ready)

**A category is a named region in a chosen axis-space — purpose-relative, with a membership judgment attached.** In plain words: someone picked a dimension along which things differ (the **axis**), drew a line on it (the **boundary** — a membership test where the edge is sharp, a distance-to-the-typical-case where it is graded [general theory, marked]), and gave the region a **name** so one word can do the filing. All three are constitutive: without the axis there is nothing to differ along; without the boundary nothing is in or out; without the name the region can't be used in passing.

**Purpose-relative, twice grounded.** By the project's own practice: the individuation doctrine — *"there is no goal-free answer… the goal fixes the resolution"* — the same pair of things merges under one purpose and splits under another. And by theorem [general knowledge, marked]: the ugly-duckling result — without a purpose's weighting of features, any two items share equally many predicates; similarity, and therefore category, does not exist unweighted. Categories are made for purposes; they are not found lying around.

**What a category is NOT (five members, each with its ground):**
1. **Not a score.** Types-never-scores [owned]: a category sorts; it never grades. "Urgent" as a bin is a category; "8/10 urgency" is a measurement wearing a bin's clothes.
2. **Not a natural fact found lying around.** The double grounding above — the axis was CHOSEN.
3. **Not a bare label.** A name without a membership judgment decorates; it does not categorize. The live in-house instance: the project's own `docs/discipline_taxonomy.md` — cited as "the four-category taxonomy" by a discipline spec — does not exist; the name has carried weight for months with no system behind it.
4. **Not a pile.** A category that excludes nothing does no work — half of categorizing is the reasoned Excluded list [owned practice].
5. **Not the thing's identity.** WHAT something is (individuation — same-or-different) precedes WHERE it files; a categorizer that hasn't settled identity is filing fog.

**Piece-level Inversion (compliance):** "a category is a RULE / a LIST / a NAME" — each tested: the rule alone is a bare predicate (no filing use without an axis and a name); the extensional list breaks on new entries (a category must receive the unseen); pure name is is-not #3. The region formulation subsumes what is right in each — the rule is its boundary, the name its handle, the members its current occupants. `Inversion logged: tested, region formulation held.`

## P2 — The categorizer: verb, unit, NOT-list (finding-ready)

**The verb.** To categorize is: given an **entry**, a **purpose**, and a **category-system**, judge which named region the entry belongs to — **under the system's membership regime**. The regime is a property of the system, not of category itself [the K6 catch]: partition systems give every entry exactly one home (his "pick one for an entry" — the focal case); composite-legal systems allow stacked membership (the project's own yield-kinds). **The unit:** the ASSIGNMENT — one entry placed, with its ground stated (which axis governed, which test passed).

**The NOT-list (intrinsic grounds):**
- **Not individuation.** Same-or-different identity is upstream; you must know what the entry IS before filing it.
- **Not ranking or scoring.** Types-never-scores; a placement is not a grade.
- **Not naming or minting.** The categorizer USES given names; coining a new category's name is another act.
- **Not clustering.** Direction of fit differs: clustering lets the data draw the boundaries; categorizing holds the boundaries and places items.
- **Not system-design.** Choosing the system's axes is the designer's act; the categorizer works within a given system.

**The open-system nuance (the misfit case).** When no region fits: in a **closed/generated** system, the misfit is evidence about the generator — signal it, never cram. In an **open/practice** system, an instance-grounded extension is the system's own legal move [owned precedents: the intervention-shapes' "add as evidence accumulates"; the 13th yield-kind admitted from the record]. Extension is still not design-from-scratch — it is the vocabulary growing by its own rule.

**Piece-level Inversion (compliance):** "the categorizer IS a clusterer run one entry at a time" — tested: the direction of fit separates them cleanly in both pure cases; the honest middle is exactly the open-system extension — one entry CAN move a boundary, but only by the system's own extension rule, instance-grounded — the inversion sharpened the nuance rather than merging the verbs. `Inversion logged: tested, sharpening kept.`

## P3 — The judgment meta-insight (finding-ready; the required center)

**Categorizing an entry is two stacked judgments.**

**First, the axis judgment: which dimension of variation governs THIS entry's filing.** The axis-concepts a categorizer chooses by — adjudicated this run from five candidates down to **three questions and one gate**:

1. **The handling question (primary): along which difference will the CONSUMER do something different?** A category system earns its keep only if entries in different categories get handled differently — filing that changes no downstream handling is decoration. Two corollaries: *mis-categorization is usually an axis error, not a bin error* (filed by topic when the consumer needed urgency — every bin on the wrong axis is wrong); and the handling question contains its own **level sub-question** (the grain: at what level along the chosen dimension does handling actually differ? too fine — every entry its own bin; too coarse — one pile. The basic-level idea [general theory, marked] is this sub-question's classic form — which absorbed the former "grain" criterion).
2. **The stability question: will this entry's value on the axis hold over the entry's expected life?** A fast-changing attribute is a bad filing axis for a durable record — the filing outlives the fact [kin to the owned kind-stability point].
3. **The asymmetry question: which mis-filing is unrecoverable?** Choose the axis (and the uncertainty rule at its boundaries) so the costly miss is the one avoided [the owned lean-to-split logic, transferred: a hidden entry is worse than a duplicated one wherever hiding is the expensive failure].

**The gate (not a criterion): the system's kind.** In a closed/generated system the axes are the generator's — not choosable per-entry; in an open/practice system the attested axes of the practice are the menu. The gate constrains what the three questions may range over. *(Adjudication log: the staged five compressed honestly — "grain" is the handling question's level sub-question, and "system-kind" is a constraint on choice, not a reason for one; stability and asymmetry are genuinely independent [time-facing and cost-facing]. Three-plus-gate is the honest structure.)*

**Then, the region judgment: where on the chosen axis the entry falls.** The membership test at the boundary; distance-to-the-typical-case where membership is graded [general, marked]; and at uncertain boundaries, the uncertainty rule is set by the system's failure asymmetry — not by a universal default.

**★The honesty clause (integral, not a footnote).** In most working systems the axis was fixed at design time — the seed index's type column, the route's verb — and the categorizer INHERITS it; the daily act is the region judgment, and the stack collapses to one live judgment. The axis judgment RE-OPENS per-entry at exactly three moments, all real in this project's own record: **faceted systems** (an entry located on several axes at once — the route-type's three facets; which facet governs a given consultation is decided per-use); **competing systems** (the same item could file into seeds, canon, or memory — which system serves the consumer is a weekly judgment here); and **misfits** (no region fits — the 13th yield-kind moment). So the axis judgment is *the deep judgment, mostly pre-made, re-opened at the hard cases* — which is precisely where a categorizer earns its name.

**Piece-level Inversion (compliance):** "one criterion suffices — handling subsumes all" — tested above and PARTLY absorbed (grain folded in; system-kind reclassified); stability and asymmetry resisted (handling today can be identical while stability or miss-cost differ). `Inversion logged: tested, compressed to 3+gate.`

## P4 — The gate, the failure modes, the close (finding-ready)

**The system-kind gate, first-class.** Closed/generated systems (a generator covers by construction — the seed function-typology's presence × confidence-direction square): a misfit indicts the generator. Open/practice systems (instance-grounded, extensible by rule — the intervention-shapes; the yield-kinds): a misfit is a legal extension moment. A categorizer must KNOW which kind it serves — treating open as closed forces bad fits; treating closed as open corrupts the generator.

**The failure modes (meaning-layer sketch; each violates a named part):**
- **The axis error** — filed on the wrong dimension; every bin is wrong at once; the consumer mis-served wholesale. [Violates the axis judgment.]
- **The pile** — a catch-all category absorbing everything; no exclusion, no work. [Violates not-a-pile.]
- **The score-smuggle** — grades dressed as types; "good/bad" wearing category clothes. [Violates not-a-score.]
- **The forced fit** — a misfit crammed into a closed system's nearest bin instead of signaled. [Violates the misfit gate.]
- **The bare label** — assignment to a name with no system behind it. [Violates not-a-bare-label; the in-house instance stands.]

**Kinship notes (light; the standalone honored):** individuation is the upstream sibling (identity before filing); the route-type is the owned faceted instance; the seed gate is live per-entry assignment; tag-vs-category [general] names the two membership regimes.

**The working-definition grade:** zero runs as a named discipline; every part exercised only as embedded practice — a meaning-layer definition awaiting use, not a tested spec.

**Assembly check (one-liner, tested):** *"A category is a purpose's way of cutting the world so that different cuts license different handling — and the categorizer is the judge of where an entry falls once the cut is chosen, and of when the cut itself must be re-chosen."* Tested against a tighter form ("categories are cuts; categorizers place entries and re-judge cuts") — the longer form keeps the handling clause, which is the definition's engine; kept, tighter form as a closing echo.
