## SV2 — The biology-grounded answers, and the refined structure-not-biology rule

**A-ORG resolved (honesty-bounded): mostly ONE organism.** A single mycelium is typically one genetic individual — one interconnected hyphal body (the *Armillaria* "humongous fungus" is one individual over kilometres). The "collaboration of the same species" reading has a grain of truth only at the fusion-boundary: same-species hyphae can anastomose (fuse) into a larger network, so the individual's edge is fuzzy — but the core case answering the user is **one connected body, not a collective of separate organisms.**

**A-FLOW resolved (honesty-bounded): the WHOLE network.** Nutrients/water/carbon translocate across the network toward growing tips and away from spent regions; foraging mycelium actively reallocates — thickening cords toward good patches, abandoning and recycling unproductive ones. So **growth at X/Y can draw on whole-network materials, translocated to where growth is happening — not only the local path-A portion.** The network funds its promising fronts from a shared pool and defunds the dead ones.

**A-BIO re-tested — the rule survives, refined (not dropped).** The prior finding's "structure-not-biology" rule is relaxed here, and the honest question is whether relaxing it breaks it. It does not — it sharpens into a disciplined boundary:
> Real mechanism *informs* the mapping where it is textbook-solid (translocation happens; single-genet networks exist; there is no brain — allocation is local rules + physical flow). The structural *model* carries where the science is thin (exact routing, the degree of "decision"). Contested claims are bounded out (the mycorrhizal "wood-wide web" is a different, disputed structure — excluded).

This is a genuine re-test result: the rule doesn't collapse under relaxation; it becomes "real-mechanism-informs, model-carries-where-thin, contested-bounded-out." (Guard against the Clean-Resolution-Trap: the temptation is to now import lots of fascinating mycology; resisted — only the load-bearing, solid mechanism is used.)

## SV4 — The two multihead models, and which the project is

**A-MULTI resolved: two genuinely different models, and the project is BOTH — as complementary views.**

Steelman the collapse ("both are just many-units-plus-a-shared-thing, so they're the same model"): rejected on a real axis. The models differ on **coordination-mechanism and mortality-structure**:

- **HUMANITY model** — many INDEPENDENT organisms (minds), each mortal, bridged by an EXTERNAL archive (writing). Coordination is asynchronous, across deaths. No shared live body; no shared live resource pool. The bridge is the record.
- **MYCELIUM model** — ONE organism, many growing tips, sharing ONE LIVE resource pool via translocation across a connected body. Coordination is synchronous, within one continuous life. No per-head death — a dead tip's material is reclaimed.

These are distinct (async-across-deaths vs sync-within-one-life; external-archive vs internal-live-pool). And **the project genuinely has both properties**: its sessions ARE mortal (humanity-like — a session ends, its live context is gone) AND its corpus + budget ARE shared-and-live across the running system (mycelium-like). So the two models are not rivals but **two views of one architecture at two scales**:

- the **MYCELIUM view** illuminates the INTRA-system, live picture: one harness, many traverses, one shared corpus + one budget, reallocated live toward what pays;
- the **HUMANITY view** illuminates the CROSS-session, mortal-heads picture: sessions die, records bridge them.

**The mycelium's addition:** it supplies the SHARED-SUBSTRATE / LIVE-RESOURCE dimension the humanity model (independent heads) structurally lacked. This bears on two inherited commitments (re-tested in SV6): the memory's-reach ladder (14-23) and the hero-constraint (both models keep no-total-sight — humanity by independent-but-partial minds, mycelium by a single body with no controlling centre).

## SV5 — The layer-status, the satisfaction, and the pairing re-test

**A-LAYER adjudicated on structural grounds: the allocation dynamic is a THIRD CONCERN that emerges at multi-front scale — most honestly the multi-front scaling of the move-layer, funded from the record-layer's shared pool. Not a wholly independent new layer; not mere enrichment.**

Work the three readings against the grain:
- **D1 (a wholly new third layer).** Overclaims. A new *layer* in this family means a scene-concern with its own mechanics; allocation has no mechanics independent of the move-dynamics (reinforce/backtrack) and the shared pool (the record). It is not free-standing.
- **D2 (mere enrichment of the move-layer's reinforce).** Underclaims. Reinforce, in the prior finding, is ONE search choosing among ITS OWN branches. Allocation-across-many-fronts-from-one-shared-pool is a *system-level* question — which of many quasi-independent fronts gets the shared budget — that single-search reinforce does not answer. There is a real new concern here.
- **D3 (the multi-front scaling axis) — committed.** When you go from one tip (one traverse = the move-layer's search) to many tips on one body (many traverses on one substrate), the reinforce-and-backtrack dynamics *become* an allocation problem, funded from the shared record-pool. Allocation is the move-layer's dynamics **generalized to many fronts drawing on the record-layer's pool.** It is a genuine third concern, sitting at the intersection of move and record, not beside them.

So the finding names an **ALLOCATION dimension** (fund-the-promising, reclaim-the-dead, across many fronts on one shared pool) — third concern, intersectional, not a rival layer. (This preserves the prior finding's two-layer account rather than overturning it — re-tested in SV6.)

**A-SAT explained — why mycelium is "by far the most satisfying."** Here is the deeper reason, and it is structural, not merely felt: **the mycelium is the first single scene in the family that holds all the concerns at once.** The prior scenes were single-concern — the observatory and humanity picture the RECORD; the fungus-as-move pictured the MOVE. A living foraging mycelium, taken fully, is simultaneously:
- the growing tips (the MOVE — explore/exploit/backtrack),
- the network-as-grown-path (the RECORD — the body *is* the memory of where it searched),
- the translocation across that network (the ALLOCATION — funding the promising fronts from one pool).

One connected picture carries move + record + allocation together. **That unification is why it satisfies where single-layer scenes felt partial** — it is the family's first whole-architecture scene, not a better single-layer one.

**But the pairing rule (15-11) is PRESERVED — re-tested here.** Does the fuller mycelium, with whole-network reallocation, now acquire the proportion-correction that the prior finding said the fungus lacked (and so dissolve the pairing requirement)? No. Reallocation follows *gradients* — fund where it pays, defund where it's spent. It does not RE-READ the whole record to correct over-investment in a rich-but-suboptimal seam (which still pays, so reallocation keeps funding it). The comet-hunter's error is exactly that — over-investing in a rich seam without re-reading for proportion — and gradient-following reallocation would *fund* it, not correct it. **The mycelium has a record (the network) but no re-reading-for-proportion operation** (that is a distinctly cognitive act the organism doesn't perform). So the mycelium pictures the substrate, the search, and the allocation — but not the critical re-read. The pairing rule holds: the mycelium is the drive and the substrate; the proportion-correction still needs the record-layer's re-reading. **The satisfaction (it holds three concerns at once) and the pairing (it still lacks the re-read) are both true.**
