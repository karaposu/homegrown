## The Six Finding-Ready Pieces

### Piece Q1 — What the biology actually answers (honesty-bounded)

One rule, stated once and held: this dive lets real mycelial mechanism *inform* the mapping where the science is solid, lets the structural model carry where it is thin, and bounds out what is contested. On that footing, the user's two questions have clean answers.

*Is a mycelium one organism or a collaboration of the same species?* Mostly **one organism** — a single genetic individual whose thread-like hyphae form one interconnected body (the largest known individual organism is such a fungal network, spanning kilometres of forest). The "collaboration" reading has a grain of truth only at the edges: hyphae of the same species can fuse (anastomose) into a larger network, so the boundary of the individual is a little fuzzy. But the core answer is a single connected body, not a committee of separate organisms.

*When it branches into A and B, and A into X and Y, does growth draw on the whole network or only the local path?* **The whole network.** Water, carbon, and nutrients move across the connected body toward the growing tips, and the mycelium actively reallocates — thickening its cords toward good patches and abandoning starved regions, recycling their material back into the pool. Growth at X or Y is funded from the shared network, not only from the path-A portion beneath it.

*(What stays out: the mycorrhizal "wood-wide web" — the fungus-and-plant resource-sharing story — is a different structure and a scientifically contested one; it is not this seed. And the precise routing of translocation, and any talk of the mycelium "deciding," are held lightly: there is no brain here — the allocation is local rules and physical flow.)*

### Piece Q2 — What it maps to, and the one new move

The mapping to the project's traversal architecture is close. The shared, translocated pool is the project's shared substrate — the record-corpus any traverse can read *and* the budget of attention and context the system spends. Translocation toward the growing tips is the funnelling of effort to the branches that are paying. Growth drawing on the whole network is one traverse reading the whole relevant corpus — surfacing sweeps it, fork-recall reaches back — rather than only its local branch. Same-species hyphae fusing into one network is separate inquiries connecting through the refines-and-supersedes graph: the citation edges fuse independent searches into one body of work. And the whole thing being one connected network is the plain fact that the project is one system running many fronts on one shared substrate.

One mapping is new, and sharp. In the prior finding, *backtrack* meant "when a line dead-ends, re-activate an earlier fork and try another branch." The mycelium adds a second half: when it abandons a starved region it **recycles that region's material back into the pool.** So backtrack is not only re-aiming — it is **reclaiming the budget from the dead line and redirecting it to a live one.** The fork-recall move gains a funding consequence. This enriches the move-layer; it does not overturn it.

### Piece Q3 — The two multibody models, and why the project is both (CENTER)

The user's instinct — "a multihead multibody logic" — points at something real, and it is worth being precise, because there are **two** different multibody models in play and the project runs on both.

The **humanity model** (the prior humanity analogy) is *many independent organisms* — separate minds, each mortal — bridged by an external, persisting record. Its coordination is asynchronous and reaches across deaths: I read what someone long dead wrote. There is no shared living body and no shared live resource; the bridge is the archive.

The **mycelium model** is *one organism with many growing tips* sharing one live resource pool through translocation across a connected body. Its coordination is synchronous and happens within one continuous life; there is no per-head death — when a tip is spent, its material is reclaimed by the network.

These are genuinely different — asynchronous-across-deaths versus synchronous-within-one-life; an external archive versus an internal live pool — and the project has *both* properties at once. Its sessions really are mortal: when a session ends, its live context is gone, and the next session inherits only the record. Yet its corpus and its budget really are shared and live across the running whole. So the two models are not rivals; they are two views of one architecture at two scales. The **mycelium view** shows the live, intra-system picture — one harness, many traverses, one shared substrate, reallocated toward what pays. The **humanity view** shows the cross-session picture — sessions die, records bridge them. What the mycelium *adds* to the family is exactly the dimension the humanity model, built from independent heads, could not carry: a shared, living substrate that funds the work. What it cannot replace is what humanity uniquely holds: genuinely independent, mortal heads whose only bridge is the written record.

### Piece Q4 — The allocation dimension, and why mycelium satisfies (CENTER)

Is this resource-sharing a *new layer* of traversal, beside the move-layer (one search's explore/exploit/backtrack) and the record-layer (many searches accumulating in proportion)? The honest answer is: it is a **third concern, but not a free-standing layer.** Read it three ways. To call it a wholly new layer overclaims — it has no mechanics of its own; it is built entirely from the move-dynamics and the shared pool. To call it mere enrichment of the move-layer's "reinforce" underclaims — reinforcement is one search choosing among *its own* branches, whereas this is a system-level question: which of *many* fronts gets the shared budget. The truth sits between: **allocation is the move-layer's dynamics generalised to many fronts drawing on the record-layer's pool.** It is what emerges when you go from one growing tip to many tips on one body — a genuine third concern, sitting at the intersection of move and record rather than beside them.

Which explains, structurally, why the mycelium is "by far the most satisfying." The earlier scenes were each *single-concern*: the observatory and humanity picture the record; the fungus, in the prior finding, pictured the move. A living foraging mycelium, taken whole, is all three at once — the **growing tips are the move** (explore, exploit, backtrack), the **network is the record** (the body *is* the memory of where it searched), and the **translocation across it is the allocation** (funding the promising fronts from one pool). It is the first single scene in the family that holds the whole architecture in one connected picture. That is why it satisfies where the single-layer scenes felt partial — not because it is a better move-scene or a better record-scene, but because it is the family's first *whole-architecture* scene.

### Piece Q5 — What it still cannot do, and what it costs

It would be tempting to conclude that this fuller mycelium — now with whole-network reallocation — finally supplies the proportion-keeping the prior finding said the fungus lacked, and so no longer needs to be paired with a record-layer scene. It does not, and the reason is exact. Reallocation follows *gradients*: fund where it pays, defund where it is spent. It never re-reads the whole record to catch a find that is being over-weighted. The comet-hunter's error is precisely that — pouring effort into a rich seam without ever asking whether it is the whole picture — and gradient-following reallocation would keep *funding* that rich seam, not correct it. The mycelium has a record (the network) but performs no re-reading-for-proportion; that is a distinctly cognitive act the organism does not do. So the pairing rule stands: the mycelium pictures the substrate, the search, and the allocation, but the proportion-correction still needs the record-layer's deliberate re-read.

And the costs, stated plainly. The mycelium is one body, so the clarity of *independent* minds is weaker here than in the humanity analogy: many tips of one organism, sharing one genome and one pool, are not many independent thinkers, and the diversity-of-separate-perspectives that humanity carried is softer. (This is the same cost the prior finding already booked — one organism, no per-head death — re-confirmed, not softened.) One mapping is suggestive rather than literal: in the mycelium the network is at once the memory and the resource-conduit — one structure — whereas in the project the shared corpus (memory) and the shared budget (attention, compute) are related but not identical; the elegant coincidence should not be allowed to overclaim. And the biology stays honest: the exact routing, and the degree of any "decision," are not claimed.

## Assembly Check — The Emergent

Reading the surviving pieces together, one structure emerges that no single piece states — and one seductive overreach that must be killed to see it clearly.

**The killed overreach (extrapolation):** because the mycelium holds three concerns at once, it is tempting to extrapolate that it is the family's *one true scene* and every other analogy is just a view of it — the observatory is its record-facet, the fungus its move-facet, humanity its mortal-heads-facet. This overclaims and is killed. The mycelium genuinely unifies move + record + allocation, but it does **not** contain the independent-mortal-heads property: its tips are not independent, not separately mortal, not bridged only by a record. Humanity's picture is not a facet of the mycelium; it is a distinct model of a distinct property.

**The emergent that survives:** the family converges not on one scene but on **two complementary whole-ish scenes.** The mycelium is the family's **whole-architecture scene for the live, intra-system view** — the first single picture holding the search-move, the record, and the allocation together. The humanity scene remains the distinct picture for the **mortal-independent-heads, cross-session view**. This is the honest answer to the user's sense that "we are close to a close-enough analogy": we are — the mycelium is the closest *single* scene, carrying three of the family's four concerns; the fourth (independent mortal heads bridged by records) is humanity's to keep. Convergence looks like a **pair of complementary scenes**, not a lone winner.

A second, smaller emergent, routed not asserted: the prior finding proposed a move-layer axis for the analogy scorecard ("does the scene depict the search-move?"). This dive suggests a companion — "does the scene depict allocation across fronts from a shared pool?" — a whole-architecture axis. Scoped as a candidate for the harness, not a redesign of it.
