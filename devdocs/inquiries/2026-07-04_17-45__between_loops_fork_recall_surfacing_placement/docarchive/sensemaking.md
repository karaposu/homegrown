## SV3 — Perspective Checking (Clean-Resolution-Trap guard)

**Steelman 1 — "it's ONE operation, not two halves" (the collapse, B4).** *The post-loop surfacing and the pre-loop fork-recall are the same act ("surface similar past paths"), just timed differently.*
→ **Engage:** a WRITE produces a durable artifact (links persisted in `_route.md`) consumed later by other sessions; a READ pulls direction-relevant past into attention NOW for this composition. Different products (a persisted graph vs an in-attention context), different consumers (future cold sessions vs the present runner). The collapse conflates *producing* a record with *using* one. **Two-halves holds.**
→ **Harder steelman — "the write-half is unnecessary; the read-half can recompute each time" (the status quo).** Recompute-each-time IS the inefficiency the user targets: cold navigation improvising the links (A5/F4). The write-half's value is amortizing the surfacing cost *while the traverse's context is warm* (F2). **The write-half earns its place on efficiency, not elegance.**

**Steelman 2 — "mutate old routelisters (E3) is right; bidirectional graphs are richer."**
→ **Engage:** bidirectionality is real value — BUT it needs no mutation. A reader computes reverse edges from the UNION of forward-links (if traverse N links to traverse 3, any reader knows 3←N without editing 3's file). Mutation buys *nothing* that append-forward + read-time reversal doesn't, while costing the append-only property, concurrency-safety under multihead (A1), and re-triggering the over-integration wrinkle (AN6). **E3 loses on structural grounds, not just analogy-aesthetics.** (This is the non-sycophantic correction: the user floated "update old routelisters??" — the honest answer is *no*, and precisely *why*.)

**Steelman 3 — "reject the whole migration; cross-inquiry memory belongs in the navigation session."**
→ **Engage:** selection + steering DO stay in navigation (A1/F3). But writing a traverse's OWN links is local knowledge, cheapest while warm (F2); forcing a cold recompute is the A5/F4 failure. The migration is of the WRITE only. **Partial migration survives; total rejection loses.**

**Steelman 4 — the read-half home: "something must precede articulation → a new protocol (D4)."**
→ **Engage:** with the write-half producing explicit links, the read-half's job shrinks to *read the links relevant to the candidate direction* — a READ, which belongs at the traverse's context-assembly (the topic-read, D1). A new protocol is needed only if something happens that ISN'T reading; nothing does. **The read-half homes to a doctrine line (D1), enriched by the now-explicit links.**

## SV4 — Ambiguity Collapse (the adjudications)

- **The SHAPE → TWO HALVES of one mechanism.** The operation is a write-then-read pair over the path-graph: a WRITE-half (post-routelister, records the anastomosis links) + a READ-half (pre-loop, surfaces direction-relevant past). This IS SEED-2's missing "third shape": SEED 2 saw only "topic-read IS it" vs "a new pre-protocol" because it lacked the WRITE/READ axis. The genuinely-new thing is the WRITE-half; the READ-half was always the topic-read, merely starved of an explicit record to read.
- **The MECHANISM → APPEND-FORWARD (E2), not mutate-old (E3).** Each traverse writes its own backward-pointing cross-links into its own `_route.md`; the graph is the union; no old artifact is touched. Warranted by the slime-trail (deposit, not revise, C2), anastomosis (grow a new link, C1), and reinforcement-by-accumulation (an old path "thickens" as many links accumulate pointing at it, C3) — and by the structural argument (SV3-2: mutation buys nothing).
- **REACTIVATION → a NAVIGATION act fed by E2 (E4), not a loop mechanism.** "Reactivate old paths" = the navigation session (or a future read-half) CHOOSES to re-enter an old path, reading accumulated forward-links. The loop makes reactivation *possible* (writes links); it does not perform it. This keeps selection where A1 puts it.
- **The READ-half HOME → a doctrine line on the topic-read (D1)**, enriched by the explicit links. Not a new pre-articulation protocol (D4).
- **The WORK-SPLIT → write-in-loop, steer-in-navigation.** The loop OWNS writing its own links (local, cheap, warm-context, F2); the navigation session OWNS consuming the union + comparing across heads + committing the next move (F3, A1). The over-integration wrinkle (AN6) polices the boundary: the loop's write-role must not bleed into corpus-mutation (which is why E3 is out) or into steering (which stays in navigation).

**Non-sycophancy ledger (what the user proposed → verdict):**
- post-routelister surfacing → **AFFIRMED** as the write-half.
- "just surfacing, rest to navigation" → **PARTIALLY:** surfacing WITHOUT persistence loses the benefit; append-forward is the minimal *persistent* form that pays off.
- "a mechanism to reactivate old paths" → **REFRAMED:** reactivation is a navigation act fed by the links, not a loop mechanism.
- "maybe update old traverses' routelisters??" → **CORRECTED (no):** append-forward yields the same graph without breaking the append-only record.
- the efficiency principle / work-split → **AFFIRMED** with a boundary (write→loop; steer→navigation).

## SV6 — Stabilized Understanding (the deliverable shape)

The between-traverse "fork-recall" is **two halves of one memory mechanism over the path-graph**, and the answer to SEED-2's decidability is that its missing piece was never a pre-loop protocol — it was a post-loop WRITE-half:

1. **The WRITE-half — a new post-routelister loop step** (the user's insight, affirmed). After routelister writes this traverse's paths, surface the past traverses/paths this one connects to and APPEND-FORWARD cross-links into this traverse's own `_route.md`. Cheap (context is warm), local (writes only its own record), analogy-clean (anastomosis growth / slime-trail deposit). This is the genuinely-new operation; it makes between-traverse memory explicit incrementally, so a cold navigation session inherits a link-graph instead of improvising it.

2. **The READ-half — a doctrine line on the topic-read** (SEED-2's fork-recall, homed). At the next traverse's context-assembly, read the (now-explicit) links relevant to the candidate direction. No new protocol; the topic-read IS this operation, enriched by the write-half's links.

3. **The MECHANISM — append-forward, never mutate-old.** The graph is the union of each traverse's own forward-links; reverse edges are computed at read-time; old artifacts are never edited. "Reactivation" of an old path is an emergent consumer act (it thickens as links accumulate), performed by the navigation session, not the loop.

4. **The WORK-SPLIT — write-in-loop, steer-in-navigation.** The loop owns writing its own links; the navigation session owns consuming the union + comparing heads + committing the move. The over-integration wrinkle polices the boundary.

5. **The EMERGENT — the record gains a third function: CONNECT** (beside accumulate and avoid-redundancy), scoped to the analogy-family's record-layer account.
